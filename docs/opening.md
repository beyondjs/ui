# Opening a product

`@beyond-js/ui/opening` owns the first browser-document presentation, from the first paint until the product draws a useful screen. It serves plain DOM and React applications equally, before either framework starts. The product keeps its session, admission, destination and request deadlines.

## What a person sees (0.25.0, 0.31.0)

The opening applies the family's experience principle (EXP-01) through precision rather than decoration. It says only what is true now, and offers only what can work.

- **Quick.** The family lockup (the wordmark, its divider and the product's name, in the bar's proportions) and a trace: a line with a light that travels along it while work runs, in the colour of work in progress, like the family's indeterminate band. Nothing to read. The identity is placed a little above the middle and never moves while the opening says more below it. Since 0.31.0 it fades in as the document first paints rather than popping onto the page.
- **Not quick (past 800 ms).** Unless the opening covers a structure the product already drew (Arrival, below), the steps, all at once, each with the family's mark for its state (done a filled circle, running a half-filled circle, late a diamond, failed a square) and its time in tabular figures: "Loading Conduict 0.4 s", "Confirming your session 1.2 s". The running step's time counts every 100 ms and is never announced. One live region says each new step.
- **Longer than usual.** Each opening that finished without recovery keeps its steps' durations on this device (`localStorage`, `<key>.opening`, the last 20 per step, durations only). From five openings on, a step running past its 90th percentile says "· usually 0.2 s" beside its time and the live region says once that it is taking longer than usual (D50).
- **Late (12 s, an engineering default).** "Taking longer than expected" / "You can keep waiting or reload.", with **Reload** and closed **Technical details**. "Usual" is said only where it was measured; the bound says "expected".
- **Couldn't open.** The product cannot open in this page: its code could not load or link (one of the page's own scripts or an import that did not arrive, a module naming an export its dependency lacks, a syntax error before the document's modules ran), or it threw while its modules ran, before it drew anything. "Couldn't open Conduict" and **Reload**, with no sentence that restates the button. The failed step's time stops, and the trace becomes a plain divider: nothing says it still runs. Code that could not load first reloads by itself, once per address within 30 seconds (this tab's session storage), so a failure that passes is never seen and one that persists is said instead of looping. A script of another origin or an extension's that fails is not the product's code and changes nothing. If the product moves anyway, the opening waits again, keeping the failed step; a step that failed stays failed whatever is said next.
- **A failure the product may outlive.** Any other uncaught failure (after the modules ran, or a promise nobody handled, such as JSON that does not parse) is kept for the technical details and said nothing about while the product moves. With no new step within 4 seconds, the opening says "Taking longer than expected" if the product drew something, or "Couldn't open" if it drew nothing. The details keep the first failure, or the one that made the product unable to open, never a later one that replaces it. Chrome does not report a rejected `response.json()` nobody handled, so there the late bound answers it.
- **Offline.** "You're offline" / "Opening continues as soon as you're back online." Nothing else: Reload would put the browser's own error page in place of the opening, and the opening continues by itself. Code that could not load meanwhile loads again when the connection returns; otherwise the product's own reads continue. Over a start that threw, which reconnecting cannot fix, "Couldn't open" stays said and its Reload waits for the connection.
- **Technical details** hold the step, every step with its time, the error, the opening's version ("Opening: @beyond-js/ui 0.25.0"), the address and the time, with **Copy details** (selected for the keyboard when the browser refuses the clipboard). On a development server a failure shows them open.
- **A development server that is out of date.** A document drawn by another version of the package (its server has not restarted since the package changed under it) is drawn again in this version's form, keeping its wordmark, so the page is never left unstyled. That cause is established, so a late or failed opening says it with the remedy that works: "Conduict's development server is out of date. Stop it and start it again, then reload." (a restart inside the same process keeps the earlier adapter). The details add the version that drew the page ("Page drawn by"), and the console says it once.
- **A development server's error overlay.** When Vite shows its own overlay (a compile error), the opening steps aside at once: the overlay says what failed in full, and the opening never hides or covers it.
- **Arrival (0.31.0, D185).** Once the product's root holds its structure with no step pending, the opening keeps covering it until its first screen is complete: nothing drawn in the root still waits for a first read (the family's shared wait: `waiting`, `Section`'s and React's `Waiting`, `skeleton`, the placeholder rows of `Collection` and `DataTable`; a wait in a part not drawn, such as a closed panel, does not count) and the family bar has loaded (its `data-state` is no longer `loading`). It covers the structure for at most 1 s (`OpeningScreen.budget`, an engineering default), then hands over to what is drawn: the structure with each region's wait in place, as LOAD-01 has it. A quick product therefore arrives once, complete, instead of passing through its structure and its waits in a fraction of a second, and a slow one is shown with its waits saying the rest. Meanwhile the opening says no steps, and the product's last step ends where its structure was drawn, so "usual" measures the opening alone. An opening that already said its steps, or why it cannot simply wait, hands over at the structure: the person waited, and the region's waits now say more. A failure the product meets once its structure is drawn is the product's to say: the opening steps aside at once.
- **Hand-over.** When the first screen is complete, or the budget ran out, the product is shown and takes the pointer at once; the wordmark and the name move into their places in the product's family bar (`.bui-family .bui-lockup-mark`, `.bui-lockup-name`) while the surface fades. While they move, the bar is marked as landing (`data-bui-landing="flight"`): its own wordmark and name wait hidden under the moving ones, and its separator and the product menu's chevron, which do not move, wait unseen, so nothing of the bar shows through the identity, a bar drawn again meanwhile included (0.31.0). On landing (`landed`) they show where the moving ones arrive, the separator and the chevron fading in, and the mark then goes. Without a bar in view it fades alone. It lasts 360 ms and is bounded at 700 ms. Under reduced motion nothing moves: the trace is a still dashed band and the presentation is removed at once.
- **Reduced motion (0.21.2):** the trace's light, the running step's pulses, the steps' arrival, and since 0.31.0 the stage's entrance and the landing's fade, are declared only under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`, so no more specific rule of the package's or a product's brings them back; the trace is a still dashed band.
- **Restored from the back-forward cache before it opened** (a person who pressed Back from a sign-in), the page loads again rather than staying on a frozen opening.
- **Without its controller.** A line shown by the stylesheet alone after 15 s says "Conduict hasn't opened yet. Reload this page to try again.": it cannot know that anything failed. The controller removes it as soon as it runs. With JavaScript disabled, only "Turn on JavaScript in your browser to open Conduict." shows.

The opening shows no standing help: an explanation appears only with a cause and its way on.

## Application entries

Add `new OpeningPlugin({ product, logo, key, root, pages }).plugin` to Vite's plugins. `logo` is the product's existing wordmark file, relative to Vite's root. `key` is its existing `Preferences` key; `appearance`, `locale` and `device` match the product's defaults. `root` defaults to `#root`. `pages` lists exact root-relative HTML filenames, defaulting to `['/index.html']`; include `/desktop/index.html` only when it is an application entry. Hidden session landings are excluded.

The plugin serves its generated assets in development and emits the same files in production. Its `configResolved` is asynchronous since 0.25.0, as Vite allows. The small deferred controller restores device preferences before the application modules run; the document's tokens follow the system when no choice is available. The opening's stylesheet declares the family's faces so its first paint is in Inter and Rubik; once the application is ready and declares the same faces from its own `@beyond-js/ui/fonts.css`, the controller loads the faces in use under the application's declaration and removes the opening's `@font-face` rules (0.17.1). The controller and styles are separate same-origin assets, so the page keeps its CSP and Trusted Types restrictions without inline scripts or markup sinks.

A static host uses `new OpeningDocument({ product, logo, key, root })`, serves its `files` map at the configured `base` and sends `render(html)` for its application document. Since 0.25.0 the document paints the identity, the trace and the one announced line, stamped with the package's version (`data-version`); the controller draws the steps and the recovery.

## A development server serves what is installed (0.25.0)

Products vendor this package several times a day, while their development servers keep running. A server started earlier kept serving the copy it loaded: Node's module cache held the configuration's `@beyond-js/ui/opening`, Vite held its prepared dependencies, and a page could mix both versions and never open. Since 0.25.0 the Vite adapter keeps the server current by itself:

- each time Vite resolves its configuration, the document is built from the document module as installed (`document.js?installed=<manifest time>`), never from the copy the configuration imported first;
- from the moment the development server listens until it closes, `OpeningInstall` polls the installed manifest every second. Once another copy is installed and the install has settled (npm's `node_modules/.package-lock.json`, which it writes last, is newer than the manifest, or 60 s passed without it, and both have been still for 1.5 s), the server logs it and restarts (`server.restart(true)`, which prepares its dependencies again). Open pages reload as on any Vite restart. A restart that fails leaves the earlier server running and the next install is still followed; an install of another package changes nothing; a server that never listened (middleware mode) is never restarted.

A server whose configuration still holds an earlier adapter (one started before its product vendored 0.25.0) needs one restart of its process to begin; from then on it follows every install. A static host that keeps running while its product vendors the package again follows installs with the public `OpeningInstall` (`new OpeningInstall(<the installed package's directory URL>).watch(version => …)`): when it calls back, read the package's files again and build the document from the document module imported afresh, as the adapter does. The adapter's contract with Vite and with `OpeningDocument` stays stable across versions, since a later install's document is built by this adapter. Serve CSS, JavaScript, SVG, WOFF2 and text with their corresponding MIME types. The adapter never sends or keeps a credential.

## Readiness and the steps a product names

Mark the product's **initial** waiting element with `data-bui-opening-wait`. The controller observes the configured root until it contains its structure with no pending marker, then until that first screen is complete, within the budget (Arrival, above). Draw a page's regions with the family's shared wait (LOAD-01): the opening waits for those and for the family bar, never for a product's own placeholder. Products with an existing state marker can supply `pending`, a selector within that root. A useful refusal or unavailable screen ends opening in the same way as content. Do not put this marker on internal loading after the page becomes usable.

Name the step with the marker's value: `session` while the product reads its session, `signin` just before it leaves for Beyond Accounts' sign-in, `projects` while it passes through Beyond Projects' session. The opening says each in the family's words and in the document's language; `loading.step({ step, language })` (React `Loading.step`) gives the product's own waiting line the same words, and `loading.steps` lists them. Any other value, or none, is said in the product's own words: since 0.21.2 one short line, its status line (`role="status"`) or else its first line of text, to the end of the first sentence and without its ellipsis, so a note a region adds while it waits starts no new step; else "Opening {product}". A document drawn by another version of the package (a development server whose package changed under it, before the server's process restarts) is drawn again in this version's form and says that cause if it cannot open (0.25.0; before, 0.21.2 only ended it on readiness). A product that leaves for a sign-in keeps its waiting element (`signin`) until the browser leaves, so the opening never ends on an interim page.

Until the hand-over, application siblings are hidden from sight, focus and the accessibility tree, including while the opening covers a drawn structure: focus moved to a part of the product meanwhile does not take, so a product that moves focus on arrival does it once its content has arrived. Readiness delays no content: the hand-over starts the moment the first screen is complete, and at most the budget after the structure. If recovery held keyboard focus, focus goes to the arriving heading, main region or first control, without scrolling. Otherwise the application keeps its own focus. Reload uses the browser's current full address and is the person's choice, except after offline code failures and back-forward restores described above. Products continue to own automatic service-outage retries, last-attempt information and reconciliation of unknown changes.

Public documents that already contain their content, native OS splash screens and session-renewal landings do not acquire an application opening. A retained client-rendered product can adopt the document presentation without adding product capability.

## Hosts that serve the package's sources

Bundled products need nothing more. A host that serves `@beyond-js/ui`'s source modules to the browser itself, as Projects' service does from `src/dom/`, serves `src/opening/words.js` too: since 0.21.0 `src/dom/feedback.js` imports the opening's words from it, so `loading.step` and the opening say a step in the same words.

## In-page loading lines

`loading(label)` / React `Loading` draws an announced waiting line whose mark, since 0.21.0, is work in progress's half-filled circle in the colour of work in progress, pulsing a few times and then still (still at once under reduced motion), as `Status`' busy. Structural skeletons retain their shape without continuous animation. Since 0.31.0 (D185) a region's wait and its placeholders are seen only past a 300 ms grace, then fade in, so a quick read never flashes them; a loading line that answers the person's own action shows at once.
