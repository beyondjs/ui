# Component catalog

Every component of `@beyond-js/ui` 0.27.0: what it is for, its variants and main options, the states it states in words, and its consumers. DOM names come from `@beyond-js/ui` (or `/dom`); React names from `@beyond-js/ui/react`. Every component takes `labels` for its copy (strings with `{placeholders}` or functions of the values) and every DOM class has `mount(parent)` and `destroy()`. Type declarations in `types/` list every option.

**Consumers** (each product vendors the tarball in its own `tools/` and records its adoption, residual copies and evidence in its own repository; the family reference catalogs them component by component in `branding/src/family/components/consumers.js`): the coordinated consumers vendor 0.20.2 since 2026-10-22: Beyond Home (plain DOM, including its windowed host at `/windows/`), the Delegate application (React, the whole application), Branding (plain DOM), the Conduict interface and its surface for the windowed host (plain DOM), the Projects interface (plain DOM served by its service), the Packages interface, the Snapshots and Accounts frontends and the retained Workspace client (React), the CDN administration and backoffice (React), Labs and Docs Site. Installation and final browser acceptance are recorded separately in [the release evidence](reviews/2026-10-22/release-0.20.2.md). Conduict vendors 0.21.1 for its All conversations. A product that passes its own `labels` to `Collection` adds `end` and `keys`, or their words fall back to English.; no other consumer has vendored them ([0.21.1](reviews/2026-10-23/release-0.21.1.md)). Every product that names itself beside the wordmark uses `Lockup`.

## Foundations

**Tokens** (`@beyond-js/ui/tokens`, `@beyond-js/ui/tokens.css`). Token set 0.2.0, `status: 'approved'`: the owner approved the palette, roles, type scale and elevation on 2026-09-29 (decisions D08, D09, D10, D12 and D20, recorded in `tokens.approval`). It is token set 0.1.0 (packages 0.1.0 to 0.2.2) plus `--color-border-control`, `--weight-regular`, the window elevations and the amended label; nothing else changed. Each entry's `provenance` still says whether its value was captured from the public site or introduced by the family reference. Components read semantic roles only (`--color-<role>`), never primitives, and so do products on their own surfaces instead of local color values. The theme follows `data-beyond-mode` (`light` or `dark`), falling back to the system preference. Density follows `data-density="compact"` on the root; coarse pointers always get 44 px targets. Consumer: Branding (tokens only).

- **Borders.** `--color-border` and `--color-border-strong` separate content: dividers, in-page surfaces, tags. `--color-border-control` (`#767676` light, 4.54:1 on white; `#8e99bb` dark, 5.78:1 on the dark surface) is the boundary of inputs, selects, checkboxes, toggles, menu and disclosure buttons and secondary buttons, a `graphic` pair (3:1) the token tests check in both themes. A divider inside a control (between toggles) keeps `border-strong`.
- **Token set 0.6.0 (decision C2, 2026-10-17, decided under the owner's delegation, amending D08, D09, D10, D12 and D52; `tokens.approval.visual`).** Coral is kept for the primary action (`--color-action`), the selection (`--color-accent`, `--color-surface-selected`) and focus; `--color-link` is the text's color, and a standalone link (crumb, way back, a row's name) underlines on hover while a link in a sentence or a link-styled button keeps a quiet underline (WCAG 1.4.1). Inter is the interface's face (`--font-sans`, 400/500/600, `--weight-semibold` 600 for the page title), Rubik the wordmark's (`--font-brand`, the lockup's name); identifiers, paths and hashes take `.bui-mono` (or `data-mono`). A card or panel may be raised inside a page (`--color-surface-raised`, `--elevation-raised`, `.bui-raised`, `Section`'s `raised`). A disabled primary is grey (`--color-disabled`, `--color-on-disabled`), never a dimmed coral. The light theme has one surface family: the family bar and the sidebar sit on the page's surface (`--color-family-bar` white, `--color-sidebar`, `--color-sidebar-hover`), tooltips keep their own dark roles (`--color-tooltip`, `--color-on-tooltip`) and the bar's hover (`--overlay-lift`) is per theme. Dark text is off-white (`#e6e9ef`, DQ-10). The sidebar's current item sits on the selection's surface; the avatar is neutral.
- **Token set 0.8.0 (D187, the owner's direction of 2026-10-25, amending D126's coral for focus; `tokens.approval.focus`).** `--color-focus-edge` is the light a box that takes typing or keys gives its own border while it has the focus, in the text's tone (`#121f36` light, `#e6e9ef` dark), a `graphic` pair (3:1) on the canvas, the surface and the raised surface. `--color-focus` stays the coral of the ring, the cursor's inner ring and the caret. See "Focus" below.
- **Type.** `--text-title-1` 1.625rem, `--text-title-2` 1.1875rem, `--text-title-3` 1rem, `--text-body` 0.9375rem (15 px), `--text-small` 0.8125rem (13 px), `--text-label` 0.75rem (12 px, the smallest text) with `--tracking-label` 0.02em. Weights: `--weight-light` (300) only at body size and larger; `--weight-regular` (400) for small text, tables, hints and reasons; `--weight-medium` (500) for headings, labels, names and buttons. A label (table heading, tag, menu heading, a stacked row's column label) is told apart by weight 500 and the muted color, shown as written in sentence case: no component transforms case (D20).
- **Inter and Rubik** (`@beyond-js/ui/fonts.css`). Since 0.15.0 the package also ships Inter 400, 500 and 600 in the same subsets (`@beyond-js/ui/fonts/inter-<subset>-<weight>-normal.woff2`, the files of `@fontsource/inter` 5.3.0, unchanged, with `OFL-Inter.txt`); `--font-sans` names Inter, so a product that imports `fonts.css` draws its interface in Inter with no change, and `--weight-light` text draws Inter 400. Earlier: the package ships Rubik 300, 400 and 500 in the latin and latin-ext subsets as woff2 (`@beyond-js/ui/fonts/rubik-<subset>-<weight>-normal.woff2`, the files of `@fontsource/rubik` 5.3.0, unchanged) with its licence (`@beyond-js/ui/fonts/OFL.txt`, SIL Open Font License 1.1). `fonts.css` declares one `@font-face` per weight and subset with its `unicode-range` and `font-display: swap`, so a page downloads only the weights it uses and latin-ext only when its text needs it. `styles.css` loads no font: a bundled product imports `@beyond-js/ui/fonts.css` once, beside the two stylesheets; a product that is not bundled serves `fonts.css` and the woff2 files together, keeping their names. Products drop Google Fonts, `@fontsource` packages and their own copies.
- **Elevation** (D10). Only what overlaps other content casts a shadow: `--elevation-menu` (menus, popovers, tooltips, toasts, drawers), `--elevation-dialog` (dialogs and, since 0.7.0, side sheets), and `--elevation-window` and `--elevation-window-focus` (windows of Beyond Home's windowed host at rest and focused, from its frame shadows, resolved per theme). Inset or ring shadows that mark a state or draw focus are marks, not elevation. Surfaces inside the page are flat, with 1 px borders, except, since 0.15.0 (C2), a raised card or panel (`--elevation-raised`: `.bui-raised`, a `raised` `Section`, `CommandCard`, `Checklist`); a unit test holds the stylesheet to it.
- **The brand orange** (D12). `#e46f4e` (coral-400, the wordmark) is the brand orange; on light backgrounds its text and fill variant is `#9e533e` (coral-600, the light `action` and, since 0.15.0, the light bar's marker), on navy its text variant is `#ffa385` (coral-200; the dark `link` until 0.14.3). `#cb6245` (coral-500) is only the light `accent` and `focus` color, never called the brand color. White text never sits on `#e46f4e` (3.14:1): the dark action puts navy on it (`on-action`). The primitives state this in their `use` field.

**Motion.** 150 ms and 200 ms with one easing, for entering panels and dialogs and for control color changes. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` nothing animates or transitions; spinners stop and state stays in words. Since 0.19.1 a still spinner is work in progress's half-filled circle (D129), never an open ring, which is the shape of what rests.

**Motion follows the person's action** (since 0.14.0, D95, approved on 2026-10-10 in settled contract S29; token set 0.5.0 adds `--motion-exit`, 120 ms, and `--motion-ease-exit`, both engineering defaults to measure). Only what the person opens or closes, and what overlaps, animates, with an entry and an exit: menus (`ActionMenu`, `ChoiceMenu`, the composer's split menu, a disclosure's floating panel) leave as an inert picture of themselves while the real one is hidden at once (`Leaving`: nothing in it can be reached after `close()` returns, never a copy beside a reopened one); a page's panel kept in view enters from its edge and leaves toward it with transform and opacity (`data-motion`), its columns changing once, at the start when it shows and at the end when it hides, so the thread's text never reflows during the motion and the pressed toggle never moves; an activity row's and group's body and a `bui-summary` disclosure's content ease their height where the engine interpolates to an automatic size (`interpolate-size`, `::details-content`; Chrome today) and fade elsewhere (an activity body's exit needs a transition of `display`, which Firefox does not run yet: there it closes at once), with nothing measured in script and nothing scrolled for the person. Live content never moves: a live dot (`bui-pulse`: a progress status, a running step, a sidebar mark, a recording) pulses four times, about five seconds, then holds; a loading skeleton keeps pulsing. Under reduced motion no transform, height or scroll animates and no picture is drawn; since 0.14.1 neither is one kept where a host removes animations by a preference of its own (a picture whose computed animation is `none`, or lasts no time, is removed at once), so a host needs no rule of its own for the pictures.

## Actions

**Button** (`Button`; React `Button`, `useBusy`). Variants `primary` (one per view), `secondary`, `quiet`, `danger`; `small`; `glyph`; `href` renders a link styled as a button. `busy` keeps focus and place, marks `aria-disabled`, shows a spinner and says it is working; `run(work)` and React `useBusy()` wrap an asynchronous action so a second press is ignored. Since 0.26.0 (D165) a button may be `bare`: its glyph alone, its words visually hidden and its accessible name (`aria-label`, or `name` when given), shown by the family tooltip on hover and keyboard focus (the button is its own `Hint` root); only a glyph of the closed list stands alone this way. `bare`, `glyph` and `name` are setters too, so a control can take its words back (a composer's Send when a step needs them) or show another glyph for a moment (a copy's `check`); a button that was never bare keeps whatever name and tooltip a product set on it. Class `bui-button-bare`. Since 0.32.0 (D183) a button may be `waiting` for a step not yet taken (DOM option and setter, React prop), such as Add while nothing is chosen: it is quiet (`aria-disabled`) and still reachable, and a press only puts the person at `place()`, its own action never running; a link that waits does not navigate, nor a busy one (0.32.1); a form's submit waits through `Required` instead. Since 0.35.0 its own wait is marked `data-bui-waiting` (DOM and React), which `Required` and `Quiet` never free: a submit that waits for the product's own condition stays quiet once the form holds everything, and once its wait ends, a form whose parts still wait quiets it again (found by the Packages interface, whose Save was freed).

**ActionMenu** (`ActionMenu`). A menu button of actions (ARIA menu pattern) for secondary actions on a record. Items: `label`, `run` (React `onSelect`) or `href`, `disabled` with `reason`, `tone: 'danger'`, and since 0.34.0 `waiting` (D183): an item that waits for a step not yet taken looks quiet, as a disabled one, while a press still closes the menu and runs it, so its `run` puts the person where they can act; it is never a link. ArrowDown/ArrowUp open on the first/last item, arrows, Home and End move, Escape closes and returns focus, Tab closes, a press outside closes. A disabled item stays reachable and reads its reason. The list stays inside the viewport (since 0.1.4): `placement: 'auto'` (default) opens below the button, or above it when there is no room below and more above; `'below'` and `'above'` fix the side; it is shifted sideways when an edge would cut it and capped to the room on its side, where it scrolls. Focus moves into it, within it and back to the button without scrolling the page or any scroll container around it. Use links in navigation, not this menu.

**Disclosure** (`Disclosure`). A button that shows and hides a panel (account menu, header panels). Escape and the button close and return focus; a press outside closes by default. Since 0.20.3, `outside: false` (React `outside={false}`) keeps an in-flow section open when the person uses another control. This prevents collapsing content from shifting a pointer target between press and release. Floating menus retain the default. Since 0.20.4, `Help` opts out because its explanation flows in the page; its toggle and Escape remain available. It traps nothing. Closing hides the panel at once; since 0.1.5 an inert picture of a floating panel (`.bui-disclosure-leaving`: no identifiers, hidden from assistive technology) eases out where it was and removes itself. The picture keeps the panel's classes, so since 0.5.1 it never outlives a reopening: opening again or destroying removes it, and an open panel never has a copy beside it (a query for an entry's class in an open menu finds one). A panel in the page's flow (help) closes with no picture, which would hold its place, and with reduced motion there is none. A product that restyles the panel styles the picture with it. Since 0.11.2 a floating panel opens above its button when the viewport has no room for it below and more room above (`placement: 'auto'`, the default, as menus do; class `bui-disclosure-above`), and `placement: 'above'` (React `placement`) always opens it above, for a button whose page docks something under it, such as an answer's details over a docked composer (Conduict's review, minor 1); `'below'` never moves it. A panel in the page's flow (help) never moves.

## Forms

**Field** (`Field`). Label, hint, control and error wired with `aria-describedby` and `aria-invalid`. DOM `check()` uses the browser's constraints plus `validate(value)` and shows the consumer's `messages.<failure>`; after an error the field rechecks while typing. **Nothing written is no failure** (since 0.32.0, D183): a required field with nothing in it (blanks are nothing, an unchecked required box is nothing) is `missing`, and `check()` answers false for it with no message, no `aria-invalid` and no call to `validate`, so `messages.valueMissing` is never shown; a form puts the caret there instead. Only a value that is wrong is said. React `Field` wraps one control child and takes `hint`, `error`, `optional`. **A suggested value** (since 0.7.0, D56, CNT-95): `suggest` shows a value derived from context (a slug of a project's name, the next unique name, a directory from a package name), marked "Suggested" («Sugerido») after the label, and the field follows every new `field.suggest = …` until the person types a value of their own (`edited`); a field emptied and left takes the suggestion back, `follow()` returns to it, and a `value` the product gives stands. React: `const name = useSuggestion(suggestion)` returns `{ value, onChange, onBlur, suggested, follow }` with the same rule, and `<Field suggested={name.suggested}>` draws the mark. Since 0.32.1 a value written without a typed one (a suggestion offered, followed or taken back, in DOM and React) is told to the form with `Required.change`, so a submit waiting for the field is drawn able. `Field.labels.en` / `.es` carry the copy.

**Choices** (`Choices`). Checkboxes (value is an array) or radios (value is a string) in a fieldset with legend, hint and error; options may be disabled with a visible `reason`. Since 0.7.0 a radio group with one option that can be chosen is a statement, not a choice (D56): the legend as a label and the option as text (an `<output>`), with its value submitted under `name` (`stated`, `statement: false` keeps the radio). A single checkbox stays a checkbox: it is a yes or no. Since 0.7.1 the statement keeps everything the option said: its `hint` on its own line, describing the statement (`aria-describedby`), and its `status` (`[label, tone]`), which an option of a group also shows beside its label, so a lone AI engine reads "Claude Code · Ready" with "Connected · Max plan" under it and needs no `statement: false`.

**Select** (`Select`). A styled native select for short finite lists; keeps native keyboard and touch pickers. Use it for plain values a person reads at a glance; use `ChoiceMenu` for a few things that have a state, and `Picker` for searchable or large entity collections. A native select clips a chosen text wider than its box; since 0.5.0 (D44 as amended on 2026-10-03) that text shows whole in a tooltip on hover and keyboard focus, only while it is cut, measured in the select's own font (DOM and React alike; the tooltip is `aria-hidden`, since the select's value already says the whole text, and a press hides it). The platform's list shows every option whole. A product that draws its own select markup gives it the same tooltip with `Select.tip(control)`, which returns a `NameTip` to destroy with the control (declared since 0.5.1). Fixed labels must fit: where they may not, use `ChoiceMenu`, which wraps. Since 0.7.0 one option that can be chosen is a statement (D56): the option as text in an `<output>` that the `Field`'s label names, its value submitted under `name`; `stated` is true and `control` is the output; `statement: false` keeps the select. A disabled single option is not stated. Since 0.7.1 an option's `hint` (or `detail`) and `status`, which a native select cannot show, are kept by its statement. The React `Select` renders the same markup.

**ChoiceMenu** (`ChoiceMenu`, since 0.3.2; React `ChoiceMenu` since 0.7.0, CNT-94). One choice among a few things that have a state, such as a machine that may have failed or an AI engine that may need a sign-in. The button names the choice and its value (`label`, "Machine: lab"), with the state's dot and, when the state asks for attention (`danger`, `warning`), its words and a border of that tone; a calm state stays in the accessible name. It never cuts its words: it grows, and wraps in a narrow container (so D44 holds without a tooltip, checked again in 0.5.0). It opens the family's menu (ARIA menu pattern) with the options as `menuitemradio` (`value`, `label`, `detail`, `status: [label, tone]`, `disabled` with `reason`), the chosen one checked, and `actions` (`label`, `run`) after a separator, such as "New machine…". ArrowDown and ArrowUp open on the chosen option, arrows, Home and End move, Enter or Space chooses, Escape closes and returns focus, Tab closes and moves on, a press outside closes. A disabled option stays reachable so its reason can be read and is never chosen. `onchange(value)` runs only for a different choice; setting `value`, `options`, `actions` or `disabled` redraws without calling it. Since 0.7.0: printable keys move to the next option that starts with them (type-ahead); a list longer than `ChoiceMenu.threshold` (15), or with `search: true` or a number as its own threshold, opens on a search field above the options that filters by the beginning of the text or of any segment after `/`, `-`, `_`, `.` or a space, ignoring case and accents ("login" finds `feature/login-page`), says "Nothing matches …", chooses the first match on Enter, clears on Escape and closes on a second Escape; ArrowDown goes into the options and ArrowUp from the first back to the field; an option's `search` is what it matches when its label is not plain text. One option that can be chosen and no action is a statement (D56): "Infrastructure Docker on this computer" as text with its state and, since 0.7.1, its `detail` on its own line, `value` that option's and nothing to open (`stated`; `statement: false` keeps the menu). `name` submits the value in a hidden input; `labels` (`ChoiceMenu.labels.en` / `.es`: placeholder, search, none). React `ChoiceMenu` takes the same props with `onChange` and actions' `onSelect`, read from the latest props; option labels are strings there. Consumers: Conduict's composer (machine, AI engine, repository, saved instructions). Since 0.11.2 a closed menu draws a state's dot only beside its shown word: a calm state (a branch's "Default") is said in the button's name only, so a dot never stands alone (Conduict's review, minor 13). **A choice that waits for an earlier one** (since 0.34.0, D183), such as a branch until a repository is chosen: `waiting` (option and setter; React `waiting`) draws the button quiet (`aria-disabled`, the family's quiet look, no hover lift) and keeps it reachable by Tab; a press (a click, Enter or Space, ArrowDown or ArrowUp) opens nothing and only puts the person at `place()`, a function that returns the element where they can act (the earlier choice's `control`; React reads it from the latest props), saying nothing. Without `place` a press while it waits does nothing. `open()` returns false while it waits, and becoming waiting closes an open list, a person whose focus was in it put back on the quiet button, never on the page's body. Native `disabled` stays for a choice that can never be made, and one option stated (`statement`) is unaffected.

**ChoiceChip** (DOM and React `ChoiceChip`; since 0.11.0, built on `ChoiceMenu`). A compact choice for a composer's settings slot or a form: the chip shows a muted label, its value and, when there is one, a state in words with its tone ("Model Opus 5.5 Default", "Environment My first VM Stopped"); the state's dot only ever sits beside its word, never alone (D42), and a calm state is shown too, unlike `ChoiceMenu`'s button. It opens the same menu: options with their label, description (`detail`), state in words and, when they cannot be chosen, `disabled` with `reason` (reachable and read, never chosen); the same keyboard (ArrowDown/ArrowUp open on the chosen option, arrows, Home, End, type-ahead, Enter or Space, Escape back to the chip, Tab out), the search past the threshold and one option stated (compactly, with its state and hint). Since 0.15.0 (DQ-02) a chip with nothing chosen says its slot's name ("Machine") with its `glyph` instead of "Choose", unless the product gives a `placeholder`; `open()` returns whether it opened, and a chip folded behind a composer's Options unfolds them and opens. It takes `ChoiceMenu`'s options and `state` (`[label, tone]`): the chip's own state shown over the chosen option's, such as an engine that needs a sign-in whatever model is chosen; the `state` setter redraws without calling `onchange`. It is the target size (`--bui-target`, 44 px on a touch screen) and wraps rather than cuts. `ChoiceChip.labels` are `ChoiceMenu.labels`. Classes: `bui-chip` on the holder, `bui-chip-state`. The holder draws no box of its own: the chip's one border is its button's (since 0.11.1; the picker's chosen-chip box is scoped to its list, `.bui-chips > .bui-chip`, which 0.11.0's holder also matched, drawing a second border around the button). React: `<ChoiceChip label options value state onChange … />` with `ChoiceMenu`'s props. Since 0.11.2 each chip carries its whole face in words in `data-bui-tip` ("Model: Opus 5.5 · Default"), which a composer's family tooltip shows while it shortens the chip to its value; each state word carries `data-tone`. Since 0.26.0 an option may be `calm`: while it is chosen, its state is said in the chip's name and tooltip only (a machine busy with other work, which changes nothing for the choice), its menu still showing it; the chip's own `state` is never calm (`data-calm` on the state word). Since 0.34.0 a chip waits for an earlier choice as `ChoiceMenu` does (`waiting`, `place`), in place of a natively disabled chip the person cannot reach: a branch chip until a repository is chosen, a press on it going to the repository's chip.

```js
import { ChoiceChip, Composer } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const model = new ChoiceChip({ label: 'Model', options: [{ value: 'opus', label: 'Opus 5.5', detail: 'claude-opus-5-5', status: ['Default', 'neutral'] }, { value: 'sonnet', label: 'Sonnet 5' }], value: 'opus', onchange: value => conversation.model(value) });
const autonomy = new ChoiceChip({ label: 'Autonomy', options: [{ value: 'ask', label: 'Ask me' }, { value: 'folder', label: 'Work in its folder', disabled: true, reason: "This engine's route can't enforce it" }], value: 'ask' });
```

**Picker** (`Picker`). Searchable single or multiple entity picker over an asynchronous paged `source({ query, filters, cursor, limit, signal })` returning `{ items, next, total }`. Options: `label`, `multiple`, `selected` (initial, may carry `state: 'stale' | 'ineligible' | 'unavailable'` and `reason`), `filters` (finite selects), `name` (hidden inputs for forms), `hint`, `limit`, `delay`, `all` (multiple only: a "Select all shown" action that adds, in one change, every result shown that can be chosen; offered while one is left, focus returning to the search field when it leaves; since 0.1.3). States: loading, results count (`12 of 45 shown`), no matches for the query or filters, nothing to choose, failure with retry, "Load more". Choices are kept by id across queries, filters and pages, counted ("3 selected · 1 needs attention") and listed as removable chips; removing a chip keeps focus. Disabled results show their reason and are never chosen. Names are never cut (D44): chips, results and their descriptions wrap; its filters are `Select`s with the 0.5.0 tooltip. Keyboard: arrows and Page Up/Down move the active option (`aria-activedescendant`), Enter chooses without submitting a form, Escape clears the query. A late answer of an older query never replaces a newer one. The product revalidates on commit and reports findings with `mark(id, { state, reason })`; authorization stays in the product. Since 0.33.0 (D163) a search that did not answer joins the page's `Recovery` as a `Collection` does (`recovery`, `outage(error)`, `details(error)`; React props too): the line under the list says "Not available right now" with the outage's mark and no Try again, and the page's line searches again ([connection recovery](recovery.md#what-the-outage-affects-in-its-own-place)).

**The resource picker** (`Picker` since 0.7.0, D56 "Choose, never type", CNT-93, the repository connection review's section 8 and design S2). The same component with the parts a resource needs; every part is optional and the earlier picker is unchanged without them.

- **From [account ▾]** (`accounts: { items, value?, connect? }`): a `ChoiceMenu` labelled "From" («Desde») above the list with the accounts the results come from (`{ id, label, detail?, status?, disabled?, reason? }`, such as "acme · GitHub organization · Selected repositories"); one that cannot be used (suspended, removed, paused) stays listed with its one-line reason and is never chosen; `connect` (`{ label, run }`, "Install on another GitHub organization") is the action after them. The account in view is the picker's state (`account`), passed to the source as `account`; without `value` it is the first usable one; the `accounts` setter replaces them, keeping the one in view while usable, and a new account in view searches again. A choice records the account it came from (`selected[i].account`). One account and no action is a statement ("From acme").
- **The gate** (`gate`, the `gate` setter): `Unavailable`'s options (`title`, `reason`, `owner`, `action`, `secondary`, `kind`, default `association`) shown in place of the count, search and list while nothing can be listed yet, with the product's one action: not connected ("Connect your GitHub account to see the repositories you can choose", Connect GitHub), no installation (Install the Beyond app / Ask an owner or admin with a `CopyMessage`), single sign-on (Sign in to acme on GitHub, Check again), adding restricted. The source is asked nothing while it is gated; `gate = null` lists again.
- **Unavailable, never empty** (D40): every request is bounded (`bound`, default 20 s); a source that does not answer in time is aborted and the status says "The choices didn't answer in time, so they can't be listed now." with Try again; `explain(error)` gives the product's words ("Beyond Projects didn't answer, so repositories can't be listed now."). A failure is never "nothing to choose".
- **Rows** (`PickerItem`): `avatar` (an image address) or `glyph`; the name, then `marks` (`[{ label, tone?, reason? }]`: "In Website", "Already here", "Archived on GitHub"), each a tag whose reason follows in words; one line of facts: "Private" with a lock or "Public" in words (D11), `meta` (the default branch), "Updated 2 h ago" (`updated`), one `state` (`[label, tone]`); the description; and the reason of a `disabled` row. A row's one action is choosing it: it holds no link, button or field (Projects puts "Use in" beside the picker). Conflict marks that block are a `disabled` row with its `reason`; the product maps its codes (`in-project`, `in-other-project`, `archived`, `awaiting-confirmation`) to marks, `disabled` and words.

**Required, Quiet and React's Form** (since 0.32.0, D183): the family's one way to wait for what is still to give, so that no product keeps a copy of its own.
- **`Required`** (DOM and React) reads what a form waits for: a required control with nothing in it that the browser validates (blanks, though a password's spaces are characters; an unchecked required box, a radio group or a choice with nothing chosen, as the browser reads a choice), or a part the product marks `data-missing` (a code not typed to its end, a choice made in a `Picker`, which holds no required control of its own). Its controls are the form's as the browser lists them: one outside it that names it with `form` waits with it, one inside it that names another form does not. Given `fields`, it reads theirs, and the marked parts still (since 0.32.1). `new Required(form)` keeps the form's submit buttons quiet while a part waits (`aria-disabled`, still reachable), those outside the form that name it with `form` included; it quiets and frees only the buttons it quieted itself, so a product's own quiet state stands, and a busy one (`data-busy`) keeps its state, as a package `Button`'s own wait (`data-bui-waiting`, 0.35.0) does; once either ends, the form is read again (`Required.held(control)` says which). It hears the input and change of the form and of a control that names it, `Required.change` (a bubbling `bui-change`, sent with `Required.tell(node)`) from a part whose value was written without a typed one, as `Field` does for a suggestion and `SecretField` for Replace, and reads the form again in the next frame after its structure changes (a control added or swapped, required or disabled, a part marked), until `destroy()`. `hold(event)` at the start of a product's own submit handler stops the submission while a part waits, the caret put in the first one and nothing said: a marked part that knows where its caret goes answers `Required.ask` (a cancelable `bui-focus` event) and puts it there, as `SecretField` opens its fold; any other takes it on its control that carries `data-bui-caret` (a `Picker`'s search), its chosen option or its first enabled control, so mark the part still to choose rather than the whole editor that holds it. Text the browser could not read, such as letters in a number or a date typed half, is wrong, not missing: `hold` stops a submission for it too, whichever comes first, with the browser's own message (`reportValidity()`), since the product reads its value as empty (`unread`, since 0.32.1). `wrong` is the first control failing its own constraints, `Required.before(a, b)` the page's order and `Required.empty(control)` the rule for one control; `Field.missing` and `FocusedForm` read them. `report(event)` (since 0.34.0) stops a submission whose first part that needs something is a wrong control and reports that control with the browser's own message (`reportValidity()`, which takes the focus), answering whether it did; `FocusedForm` without fields and React's `Form` with `report` read it.
- **`Quiet`** (DOM and React) is one control that waits for a choice made elsewhere, such as Add while nothing is chosen: `new Quiet(control, { waiting, place, form })` draws it quiet while `waiting()` says so, and a press while it waits only puts the person at `place()`, the control's own handler never running (without `place` the press stays the product's), also inside a shadow root, closed ones too, whose press it hears from that root and reads by its path; `settle()` draws it again once what it waits for changes, and `form` is heard on every change of its fields and on `Required.change`. A package `Button` says the same with its own `waiting` and `place`, in React too; with no handler of a product's to defer to, a waiting `Button` without `place` only holds the press. A busy control and, since 0.35.0, a package `Button`'s own wait (`data-bui-waiting`) keep their state, the control drawn again once it ends.
- **A chooser and a held Send** (since 0.34.0): `ChoiceMenu`, `ChoiceChip` and `RefChooser` wait for an earlier choice with `waiting` and `place`, a press opening nothing and only going to `place()`; a composer's held Send (`disabled: { reason, place }`) puts a press, on Send or on its other ways to send, at `place()`; an `ActionMenu` item takes `waiting` for a press that only puts the person where they can act. A product never keeps a chooser natively disabled for a choice the person can make.
- **React `Form`** is the family's form for a product that sends its own submission: `<Form onSubmit>` keeps its submit buttons quiet while something waits (read again after every render), a press or Enter only puts the caret there, and `onSubmit` runs once nothing waits, where the product says what is wrong in a value, in its field. The browser's own validation is off, a form inside its React tree (a dialog's, through a portal) submits only itself, and its ref is the form element. `FocusedForm` remains the form whose submission the package runs.
- **The browser's own message for a wrong value** (React `Form`'s `report`, since 0.34.0, off by default). With `<Form report>`, a control that breaks its own constraints (its `type`, a `pattern`, a `min` or `max`, a product's `setCustomValidity`) and comes before anything that waits says the browser's own message and takes the focus (`Required.report`), and `onSubmit` does not run then, as `FocusedForm` without fields does; a part that waits before it still takes the caret, silently. Use it where a product has no words of its own for a value the browser already checks, such as CDN's administration, whose forms each called `form.reportValidity()` at the start of their submit handler. The default stays off: a product that says wrong values in its fields in its own words, such as Accounts, keeps them, and the browser's bubble would say the same thing a second time in another voice and style.

## Dialogs and questions

**Dialog** (`Dialog`; React `Dialog` with `open`, `onClose`, `actions`). Modal on native `<dialog>`, named by its title, described by its description. Focus moves to `[data-autofocus]`, the first field or the first action; Tab wraps; closing restores focus to the opener (the focused element, or, where a click does not focus a button as in Safari, the element last pressed; since 0.2.1) or `restore`, never to the page's body. Escape and the close button dismiss unless `escape: false`; the backdrop dismisses only with `backdrop: true`. While `busy` nothing dismisses it, including repeated Escape. Sizes `small`, `medium`, `large`. `Dialog.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.1) carry its close button's name (React `Dialog.labels`). The dialog never grows past the viewport: when the content is taller, the body scrolls on its own while the title, description and footer actions stay in view (at 320 px and 200 % zoom too); only on viewports shorter than 20 rem does the whole dialog scroll. Use it for short focused tasks (LR-07: a decision or a short edit that fits one 390 px screen); long create and edit flows deserve their own navigable screen. The React `Dialog` calls `onClose` only when the person dismisses the dialog it holds or `close(value)` runs inside it: closing it through `open`, a replacement when a shaping prop (`size`, `description`, `escape`, `backdrop`, the close label) changes, unmounting it and React's development double mount report nothing, and a destroyed dialog is never opened.

Dialog entry uses the standard motion token. Closing keeps the native modal and backdrop in the top layer through a transform/opacity exit on the exit tokens. Its contents become inert immediately; focus return, `onclose` and the opening promise finish after the animation, bounded at 400 ms. Reduced motion closes immediately. Reopening cancels the exit; destruction completes it immediately and releases its listener and timer. The React adapter preserves these rules, including a controlled reopen. `confirm`, `prompt` and `alert` wait for the exit before destroying their dialog.

**SideSheet** (DOM `SideSheet`, React `SideSheet`; since 0.7.0, LR-07, FAM-40). A task or an item opened from the inline end while the page it came from stays in view, such as Projects' add task hosted in Conduict (phase 1.1). A native modal `<dialog>` (the page behind is inert and does not scroll) named by its `title` (or `label`; since 0.19.0 the panel's title-2, semibold, D158, so its parts go in as `Section` at `level: 3`), with an optional `description` line, the body (`body`, `fill`) and a footer of `actions` that stays in view while the body scrolls; as tall as the window, `--layout-form` wide (`width: 'standard'`: `--layout-standard`, leaving the sidebar's width) and the whole width below the family cut (1024 px); the dialog's elevation and scrim; it slides in with `--motion-standard`, at once under reduced motion. Opening (`open({ restore })`, a promise of the value given to `close(value)`) moves focus to `[data-autofocus]` inside it, else to its title; Tab and Shift+Tab stay inside (moved by the sheet itself, so WebKit's Tab never reaches the page behind); closing returns focus to `restore`, else to the element that opened it, else to the constructor's `restore`, never to the body. DOM and React default `backdrop` to true in the pending source (the installed 0.19.2 archive defaults false): a primary pointer gesture starting and ending outside the sheet dismisses it through the same focus, busy and cleanup lifecycle. Padding, child controls, cross-boundary drags and cancelled gestures do not dismiss. The backdrop never activates the inert page underneath. `backdrop: false` is an explicit exception; forms are not blanket exceptions. `guard(reason)` receives `backdrop`, `escape` or `close` and must synchronously return true to dismiss. Return false while explaining or confirming unsaved work, then call `close(value)` after confirmed discard. A Promise is not accepted as permission. The React adapter reads the latest guard without remounting. A consumer’s own Cancel action uses the same preservation policy. Escape and the close button (an icon button with its tooltip, "Close" / «Cerrar») request dismissal with null through the same guard, except while `busy`: then nothing closes it, `close()` included. `error(node)` shows a failure at its top (an alert: "Beyond Projects didn't answer" with Try again) without closing it or losing what the person did; `error(null)` clears it. React `SideSheet` takes `open`, `onClose` (only for a dismissal or a `close(value)` inside, as `Dialog`), `busy`, `error` and `actions` (React content), `restore`, `width`. A non-modal sheet beside the main column is not built: on a wide region `ListDetail` keeps an item beside its list. `SideSheet.labels.en` / `.es`.

SideSheet keeps its native modal and backdrop through exit, with inert contents. Focus, scroll cleanup,
`onclose` and the opening promise finish afterwards, bounded at 400 ms. Reduced motion is immediate.
Entry and exit reversal capture the painted position and scrim opacity; reopen cancels removal and
keeps the same opening promise. Destruction settles immediately. These source changes await the
serialized package and browser motion validation; see the [current evidence](reviews/2026-10-22/release-0.19.2.md#pending-native-exit-and-global-default).

**confirm, prompt, alert** (DOM functions; React `useConfirm(labels)`). Promise-returning in-app questions: `confirm` resolves true or false, `prompt` the value or null, `alert` once acknowledged. Since 0.7.1 their copy ships in English and Spanish as `Question.labels`, also `confirm.labels`, `prompt.labels` and `alert.labels` (`confirm({ …, labels: confirm.labels.es })`; React `useConfirm(useConfirm.labels.es)`): Cancelar, Confirmar, Aceptar, Cerrar, the failure line, "Lo que se pierde", "Lo que se conserva", "Cómo deshacerlo", and a prompt field's "(opcional)" and "Revisa este valor."; `accept` stays the action's own verb. `tone: 'danger'` focuses the safe choice; `focus: 'cancel' | 'accept'` sets the starting button independently of the tone (since 0.1.3), so a consequential but not destructive step such as a publication starts on Cancel without the danger styling. With `work`, accepting runs the operation busy and non-dismissible; a failure stays in the dialog (`explain`) for retry or cancel, in the danger tone, or since 0.33.0 a `ChangeOutcome` or `{ text, tone: 'warning' }` in the warning tone, politely (D186). A prompt's Accept is quiet while its required field is empty, and Enter or a press there keeps the caret in the field, saying nothing (since 0.32.0, D183). Intended for guards such as Delegate's unsaved-drafts question; they do not replace the browser's own `beforeunload`.

**Consequence first** (decision D17; since 0.2.0). A destructive or irreversible confirmation states its consequence: `confirm({ …, consequence: { lost, kept, recovery } })` shows a short list under the message, each part only when given (text, a node or a list of them), labelled "What is lost", "What is kept" and "How to undo" (`labels.lost`, `labels.kept`, `labels.recovery`), shown as written, in sentence case (upper case by style until 0.2.2). The accept button is always the action's own verb (`accept: 'Delete project'`): "OK" and "Confirm" are wrong for a named action, and the default "Confirm" remains only for compatibility. Since 0.7.2 two more parts, both optional: `affected`, the work it stops or interrupts ("Work it affects" / «Trabajo afectado»: running conversations, deliveries in progress), and `costing`, what is still charged ("What keeps costing" / «Qué sigue costando»); the list reads affected, lost, kept, costing, recovery. A product whose confirmation is a dialog of its own (Conduict's, which also checks a lost answer) draws the same list with `consequence(parts, labels)` (DOM, also exported by `/react`; React `<Consequence parts labels />`), `consequence.labels.en` / `.es`, and returns null when nothing is stated; it replaces a product's local copy.

```js
await confirm({
	title: 'Delete Storefront?',
	message: 'The project is deleted for everyone in Northwind.',
	accept: 'Delete project',
	tone: 'danger',
	consequence: { lost: ['Its entries in each product', 'Its repositories'], kept: 'Each product’s own records', recovery: 'Deletion cannot be undone.' }
});
```

## A session that ended

**Session** (DOM `Session`, React `useSession`; since 0.8.0, the family rule D63 that presents D26, amended on 2026-10-07 so its dialog cannot be dismissed (0.9.0), Beyond Suite's `docs/family/session-renewal.md`). The one answer, in every product, to a session that ends while the person works. A product creates it once its own session read says who is signed in, with:

- `product` and `read()`: the product's read, answering `{ state: 'signed', person, expires? }` or `{ state: 'ended', reason }`; a rejection is an outage, never a sign-out;
- `start(mode)`: the product's hand-off start for `silent` (`prompt=none` in a hidden frame), `window` and `tab`;
- `other` (the bar's "Sign out of Beyond", offered as "Use another account"), `accounts` (Beyond Accounts' account page), `onrenewed` (read the view again, reconnect live transports) and `onchanged`;
- `delegate`, inside a window of Beyond Home's windowed host (`/windows/`), where the host asks for every kind (expired, revoked, suspended and Accounts not answering, since 0.9.2), so a frame never draws a dialog: `true` once signed in, `false` when the window is closing (the session then ends with nothing shown), and a rejection is asked again by itself after 5, 15, 30 and then every 60 s;
- `signin` (0.8.2), the product's own sign-in behind Continue, for an installed shell that signs in through the system browser;
- `source` (0.33.0), who the product's `read()` reaches (`projects`, `conduict` or a name; `accounts` by default; named by `CauseName.of`, "Beyond CDN" for `cdn`), named when the read rejects or times out. A read's own `{ state: 'unavailable', source }` or a rejection's `error.source` names it instead. `session.notice.source` and the `delegate` request carry it.

**The transport.** It sends every `UNAUTHENTICATED`, `SESSION_*` and `ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED` answer to `lost({ reason, replay })`, which resolves with whether to send the request again:

- `read`: once renewed;
- `write`: once renewed while the request waited;
- `false` (destructive or irreversible): never. Show `session.text('unsent')` beside the action.

**What the person sees.**

- **Expired, with a living Beyond session:** nothing. The session renews in a hidden frame.
- **Expired, without one:** one small dialog, "Sign in again to continue", with the person's avatar, name and email, "What you were doing stays here.", **Continue as {name}** (the sign-in in a window that closes itself) and "Use another account".
- **Revoked:** "You were signed out of Beyond" over an opaque backdrop.
- **Suspended:** no sign-in, **Open Beyond Accounts** and "Use another account".
- **A service not answering:** "{product} did not answer" («{product} no respondió»; since 0.33.0, before it "Beyond Accounts is not answering" whatever did not answer), "This page retries automatically", **Try again**, and retries by itself after 5, 15, 30 and then every 60 s. The hidden frame's failure names Beyond Accounts; the product's read names its `source`. Offline (0.33.0) it says "You're offline" and "Your session is renewed as soon as you're back online. Your work stays here.", offers nothing, and tries again at once on `online`.

**The dialog cannot be dismissed** (0.9.0): no ×, Escape nor press outside, for every kind. Since 0.9.2 it is announced as an alert dialog (`role="alertdialog"`) named by its title and described by its sentence (`aria-describedby`). Its actions are the ways on, and cancelling the sign-in window returns to the dialog. There is no state in which the page is open without a session (`state` is `signed`, `renewing`, `asking` or `ended`), so a request the page makes while the dialog asks waits behind it and is sent by its replay class once renewed. `FamilyBar` has no "Sign in" and the page carries no `data-session` marker.

**Live transports.** `check()` resolves with the state after looking. An event stream or poll that drops calls it first and shows its own "reconnecting" only for `signed`; otherwise the dialog speaks, and `onrenewed` reconnects it.

**Checks and tabs.** It looks at the session when the tab is shown, the network returns, the computer wakes and a minute before a known expiry. A renewal in one tab reaches the others (`beyond-session`). Someone else signing in drops what was held and calls `onchanged`. There is at most one silent attempt per minute, so a loop ends in the dialog.

**The landing.** The product's hand-off ends on a page that runs `@beyond-js/ui/session/landed`. The module has no imports, carries only the outcome, closes the sign-in window, and is a declared side effect since 0.8.1.

`Session.labels.en` / `.es`.

## Help

**Help** (`Help`). Essential help behind a toggle button next to what it explains, named "Help: <topic>". Opens with click, tap, Enter or Space; Escape closes and returns focus; the explanation flows in the layout at any width. `Help.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.2): «Ayuda: {topic}», «Cerrar la ayuda».

**Floating** (DOM and React export `Floating`; since 0.15.0, DQ-01, OWN-21). The one positioning primitive of the package's floating surfaces, public for a product's own popovers: `new Floating(element, { anchor, placement, align, gap, above, quiet })` with `show()` (false when the anchor is not drawn), `hide()`, `place()`, `anchor`, `side`, `open` and `retry`. Where the browser has the popover API the surface is a manual popover in the top layer while open (no scroller clips it, nothing covers it, it stays in the document for its events and styles); elsewhere it is fixed above the page. The anchor is an element or a virtual rectangle with its `contextElement` (a field's caret). It opens on its preferred side (`auto` and `below` prefer below, `above` above) and flips when that side has no room and the other has more, is shifted along the anchor and capped to the room (then scrolls); the room is the visual viewport (a phone's keyboard shrinks it) less the sticky family bar and any `[data-bui-edge]` at the top or bottom, unless the anchor is inside that edge. While open it is placed again on scroll, resize, a visual viewport change and a change of size, once per frame; an anchor scrolled out of the room hides it (`data-bui-away`). A hidden anchor (`display: none`, a folded composer's settings) is refused: the anchor dispatches a cancelable `bui-refused` with `detail: { reason: 'hidden', retry }`; a container that can show it cancels it and calls `retry()` (the composer's Options does), otherwise a console warning says why nothing opened. The surface carries `data-bui-floating`, `data-bui-side` and `--bui-anchor-width`. Used by `ActionMenu`, `ChoiceMenu`/`ChoiceChip`, the composer's suggestions (anchored to the caret's line), in-page `Disclosure` panels, `Tooltip`, the family tooltip (`Hint`) and cut names; a panel inside a header keeps the header's geometry, and a `SideSheet` is a modal dialog, already in the top layer.

**Relayout** (DOM `Relayout`, since 0.32.0). A page's part that moves the columns without the window's says so: `Relayout.tell(node)` sends a `bui-layout` event bubbling from it, and `Relayout.hide(element, hidden, node?)` and `Relayout.mark(element, name, on)` show, hide or mark and tell only when that changed. A part that measures itself listens for it as for the window's resize (the composer's toolbar decides in the frame the change lands). The page's panel and the Sidebar's panel tell it; a product that moves a column itself tells it too.

**Tooltip** (`Tooltip`). A supplementary description on hover, keyboard focus and a touch press; hoverable, Escape hides it, positioned inside the viewport (by `Floating` since 0.15.0). Never the only copy of essential information. Since 0.5.0, `when` (a function) is asked each time it would show and it shows only while that returns true: the package uses it to show a cut user-written name whole only while it is cut (D44).

**Hint** (DOM and React export `Hint`, React `useHint`; public since 0.11.1). The family tooltip of glyph-only controls (D11), the one the package's own icon buttons use, for a product's own: `new Hint(root)` over the element that holds them; every control inside with `data-bui-hint` and an `aria-label` (a glyph of the closed list alone) shows its name after a short delay on hover, at once on keyboard focus and for a moment on a touch press, with ` · {data-bui-shortcut}` when the control names a key; a click, leaving, blur and Escape hide it, and it never shows over what an expanded control opened. It repeats the accessible name, so it is hidden from assistive technology and describes nothing. It is drawn in the dialog that holds the root, else in the body. One hint per root (the nearest root wins for nested ones); `destroy()` releases every listener and leaves the root as it was. React: `const ref = useHint()` on the element that holds the controls (StrictMode-safe). Use `Tooltip` for a description that is not a control's name. Since 0.11.2 `new Hint(root, { when })`: a control with `data-bui-tip` (a shortened chip) shows that whole text instead of its name, only while `when(target)` answers true; without `when` a tip never shows. Since 0.15.0 it is placed by `Floating`, and a hint shown by a touch press leaves out its shortcut. A product's own key hints ("⌘K to search", "/ for commands") take `.bui-keys` or `data-bui-keys`, hidden where there is no keyboard (`hover: none` and `pointer: coarse`).

```js
const hint = new Hint(toolbar); // <button class="bui-icon-button" aria-label="Copy link" data-bui-hint>…more glyph…</button>
```

## Opening a product

The build-time public module `@beyond-js/ui/opening` supplies `OpeningDocument` for static servers, `OpeningPlugin` for Vite and, since 0.25.0, `OpeningInstall` for a server that keeps running while the package is installed again. The [opening guide](opening.md) defines application entry selection, readiness, recovery (only what is true, only what can work), a development server that follows installs, and continuity with DOM and React loading lines. It creates no session or request policy.

**When a product has arrived** (since 0.34.0). The document element carries `data-bui-opened` once the opening has handed over and the identity has landed on the family bar, and at once when there is no opening to run (no `#bui-opening` in the document) or it steps aside for a development server's error overlay. An opening that cannot open, its recovery shown, never sets it, and nothing removes it. It is the opening's one public mark of an arrival: a test or a tool waits for `html[data-bui-opened]` before it reads or presses the product (Playwright's `page.waitForSelector('html[data-bui-opened]', { state: 'attached' })`), never for the opening's own parts (`#bui-opening`, its `data-state`) or the bar's `data-bui-landing`, which may change. `OpeningExit.mark` names it.

## Collections and feedback

**DataTable** (DOM and React `DataTable`; since 0.15.0, DQ-12). A table of rows the product holds: a compact search whose placeholder names it (the label kept for assistive technology), headers that sort (`sort: true` or a comparison; `aria-sort`; one column at a time, ascending then descending, stable, numbers as numbers), groups with their count (`group: { by, label? }`), and pages of `size` rows (50) with "1–50 of 312", none when everything fits. The table never reads the address: every change the person makes calls `onchange({ query, sort, direction, page, filters? })` so the product writes it, and setting `state` (back, forward) draws it without calling it. States: `status = 'loading'` (skeleton rows in the table's shape, its loading line for assistive technology), `{ failed: reason }` (the reason or "This list could not be loaded." with Try again, `onretry`), and rows. No row at all keeps the tools reachable, hides the headers and pager, and shows the empty state (`empty`: a node or `{ title, text, action }`); a search with no match says "Nothing matches “…”." with "Clear the search". Since 0.21.1 its body hangs into the gutter as a list's rows do (D159, the list rule L1): the first and last cells keep their padding, so their words stay on the content edge while a hovered row's surface (`--overlay-lift`) reaches past them; dividers are `--color-border-strong` on the content edge, under the header and between rows, with no line above the first row. The primary column (`primary`, else the first) names and opens the row (`link(row)`, `onopen` for plain clicks; a button without `link`), the whole row its target; `mono` and `align: 'end'` columns. On a region under 36rem each row keeps its primary cell, then labeled secondary values joined by " · " and wrapping as needed. An empty value leaves out its label and its separator (`data-empty` on its cell): nothing, an empty text, or an element with no text and no element in it, and since 0.35.0 a React cell whose content renders nothing, read again whenever that content changes (found by the Packages interface, whose narrow lines kept an empty cell's label and began a line with "· "). A column may set `summary: true` when its content already names its meaning or contains independently named actions; it omits the repeated narrow visual label while keeping the table header. Dates and other ambiguous values retain labels. React: `render(row)` may return React content; `rows`, `state` and `status` are applied when they change. `DataTable.labels.en` / `.es`.

**Collection** (`Collection`; React columns may `render` React content). Since 0.15.0 (DQ-12): no range and no pager when everything fits on one page; the search label is visually hidden and its placeholder names what it searches; `presentation: 'list'` draws the rows as `ListRow`s (the primary column the name, columns with `end: true` at the row's end, the others its muted line). Compact list with search, finite filters, a table whose primary cell links to the detail (`link(row)`, `onopen` for applications that route themselves) and paging by `total` or `more`. States: loading (skeleton), failure with retry, empty with its first action, no matches with "Clear search and filters". `state` (`query`, `filters`, `page`) and `onstate` keep list context in the address across back, forward and reload. A page change moves focus to the new rows. An empty list shows no range (since 0.3.1): its empty state already says why. Rows stack with their column labels below 640 px. **Column priority** (since 0.7.0, LR-03, FAM-40): a column with `priority` (1, 2, 3 …) leaves as the region narrows, the highest number first, instead of the table scrolling; the primary column and a column without a priority always stay. The natural width of each column is measured once per drawing and the fit follows the region's own width on every resize (a `ResizeObserver`); a hidden column leaves every row. "Show 2 more columns" (`aria-expanded`) reveals them, the table then scrolling in its own box, and "Show fewer columns" hides them again; the choice is kept across pages and searches. Stacked rows show every column. `Collection.labels.en` / `.es` (since 0.7.0) carry the collection's copy. Since 0.21.1 its body and table share `DataTable`'s geometry: they hang into the gutter with words, dividers, skeletons and messages on the content edge.

**The family's list rules in `Collection`** (since 0.21.1, D162; the Beyond Suite's `branding/docs/list-patterns.md` states rules L1–L10). With `presentation: 'list'`:

- `row(row)` returns the row's own `ListRow` options (its state's `lead`, its `meta` line, its `menu`), applied over the options the columns give. The columns still give the name and the link.
- `group(row)` returns `{ key, label }`. Consecutive rows with one key go under its label (L4). Each group is a `ul.bui-rows` named by its label (`aria-labelledby`), inside `.bui-rows-groups`. The label is a label by design (`data-bui-label`).
- `end` is the list's end line, drawn only on its last page and never for an empty list (L5). `true` (the default) says "Nothing more" ("No hay más"); a text, a node or a function of `{ count }` gives the product's words; `false` draws none. A pointer screen also shows the keys ("↑ ↓ to move · Enter to open") in `.bui-keys`, hidden on touch, when the focus can move: since 0.31.1, on a page of two rows or more, or of one row a search reached (Down from the search goes to it). The table presentation draws no end line.
- A name given as text marks the search's words (L8). Up, Down, Home and End move between the rows' names, and Down from the collection's search reaches the first row (L7).

React `Collection` takes `row`, `group` and `end` (`true`, `false` or a text); `row` and `group` read the latest props.

**Recency**, **Matches** and **RowKeys** (DOM; React exports `Recency` and `Matches`; since 0.21.1, D162). `Recency` groups a moment for a list ordered by activity: Today, Yesterday, Previous 7 days, Previous 30 days, then the month ("September", "August 2025" outside the current year). It counts calendar days in the person's time zone and reads its clock on each call. A moment ahead of the clock counts as today; a value that is not a moment is Earlier. `new Recency({ locale, now, labels })`, `of(moment)` and `group(moment)`, which gives `Collection`'s `group`; `Recency.labels.en` / `.es`, and the month's name follows `locale` in sentence case. `Matches.mark(text, query)` returns the text's parts with each word of the query, ignoring case and taken literally, in a `<mark class="bui-match">`. `new RowKeys(list)` gives a product's own list of rows the arrow keys `Collection` has; `first()` focuses the first row's name and `destroy()` releases it. Keys with a modifier keep their meaning, and Tab still leaves the list.

**Collection tools** (`Collection.Tools`, and `Collection`/`DataTable`'s `tools`). Shared compact or expanded search and filters, with external placement for a page's action region. React adapters provide `toolsTarget` for a dedicated toolbar outlet. See [collection search and filters](list-tools.md) for placement, state, matching and ownership. **One name for the compact search** (decided in 0.34.0): its button and its field keep one accessible name ("Search requests", «Buscar en solicitudes»), since the button is a disclosure (`aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`) that opens the field it names, so both name one thing in its two states. A test opens it with `getByRole('button', { name })` and then reaches the field with `getByRole('searchbox', { name })`, never with `getByLabel(name)`, which matches both.

**Status** (DOM `Status`, React `Status` with `state`; since 0.15.0, C2, DQ-04). A state in words with a mark whose shape says it as well as its hue: a closed set of states (`Status.states`), each with its tone: `ready`, `done`, `success` a filled circle; `busy`, `running`, `waking` a half-filled circle that pulses a few seconds (an open ring until 0.18.3, which read as a letter beside the words); `attention` a diamond; `failed` a square; `asleep`, `stopped`, `idle`, `pending` a hollow circle; `unknown` (and any name outside the set) a dashed circle. The family's words (`Status.labels.en` / `.es`: Ready, Busy, Waking up, Asleep, Needs attention, Failed, Unknown…) unless the product gives its own `label`. `variant: 'badge'` draws a pill on the tone's surface; `live` announces a change politely; `state` and `label` patch in place. A product maps its own states onto the set; the words carry the meaning and the mark is hidden from assistive technology.

**ListRow** (DOM and React `ListRow`; since 0.15.0, DQ-12, DQ-09, DQ-05). One object in a list (`ul.bui-rows`): its mark (`lead`: a `Status` state, a glyph or a node), its name in the text color on the first line (`href` or `run`: a link or a button whose target covers the row), a muted line of `meta` joined by " · ", then at its end `facts`, a `status`, quiet `actions` and an overflow menu (`menu`: `ActionMenu` items, "More actions for {title}"). `select` makes it a picker's row with a named checkbox. `current` marks the object in view on the selection's surface. A narrow row (under 30rem, measured on the row) keeps its facts visible below the name and metadata; long facts and actions wrap as needed. Since 0.19.1 a list keeps its rows' words on the content edge: `.bui-rows` hangs its rows' inline padding into the gutter, so a hovered or current row's surface reaches past its words while each divider stays on the edge (P5); a list placed in a container with less than 12 px of inline room keeps that room for its hover. `update()` patches it in place (the name keeps focus, the menu its instance). Since 0.28.0 a name cut at the row's end shows whole in a tooltip on hover and keyboard focus (D44), and none that fits. React: the same props, callbacks `onSelect`/`onChange` read from the latest props. Interactive content in its lead, metadata and end slots remains above the stretched name link, including tooltip anchors and native controls. Its actions compose `ActionLine`, preserving `.bui-row-actions > button` for consumers. For two recurring actions without end facts, status or selection controls, opt into `ul.bui-rows.bui-rows-aligned`: keep absent action entries as `null` in their original slots. The list shares content-sized action columns, then wraps below the name under 48rem and stacks under 24rem. Hover is neutral (`--overlay-lift`); selection retains its own surface; dividers use `--color-border-strong`. Since 0.21.1 (D162, L2 and L6) every row's `lead` sits on its name's line, and on a screen with a fine pointer the overflow menu shows only on the row the pointer rests on, the row that holds the focus and while the menu is open; a touch screen always shows it. A row whose end holds only its menu keeps it beside its name at every width, and a narrow row's muted line wraps rather than cutting its last facts (L10). These apply to every `ListRow` a consumer draws once it vendors 0.21.1. An enabled action has its own boundary and underline over row hover; disabled actions do not gain enabled interaction feedback. Geometry and both-theme interaction need composed browser verification before adoption is accepted.

Long causes, repair controls, disclosures and Technical details belong in a sibling block below the object row, outside its stretched opening target. Compose a semantic list item around a `ListRow` with `tag: 'div'` and that context. Do not append a context column to the row or use grid placement on its flex children. Compact metadata stays in the row's supported slots.


React `ListRow` accepts phrasing React content or native nodes in `meta`, `lead`, `status` and `facts`. Stable portals preserve context, focus and child updates; products use these slots directly for badges, dates and secondary links. `title` stays text or a native node; a textual `lead` names an icon.

**CopyValue** (DOM and React `CopyValue`; since 0.15.0, D104, DQ-07). An identifier, path, hash or address a person copies: mono, cut at its end (or in its middle with its last 8 characters kept, `cut: 'middle'`), whole in a tooltip while cut and in its accessible name, then Copy (`CopyButton`, "Copy {label}"); with `href` it opens too. Identifiers belong here, never in sentences. Since 0.17.1 Copy never shrinks or wraps (`flex: none`, `white-space: nowrap`), even inside a box that breaks long words anywhere such as a fact's value: the value is what gets cut; React's host may shrink in a row (`min-width: 0`).

**Checklist** and **EmptyState** (DOM and React; since 0.15.0, DQ-17). `Checklist` is the ordered steps that set something up, with "2 of 4 done" and a bar: each step's `title`, `detail`, one `action` and its state (`todo`, `current`, `done`, `blocked` with `reason`; the first step not done is current); a done step folds to its title with a check; `steps` patches by `key` and keeps focus on a step's action. `EmptyState` is a place with nothing to show, told apart by `kind`: `empty` (no data yet: a title, at most one sentence, one primary action), `match` (no match for a filter, with "Clear the filter" when `clear` is given) and `context` (no project or organization chosen); a first run carries a `checklist` instead of empty sections. Since 0.24.0 (D163) `outage` is a place whose read did not answer: "Not available right now" («No disponible en este momento») by default, with the globe mark in the warning color and never an action, since who did not answer and Try again belong to the page's `Recovery`. `frame: false` (every kind) draws it without its box inside a region that is framed already, as `waiting`'s. Since 0.24.1 an outage's place is one quiet line by default, the mark beside its words in muted text and no box (`frame: true` asks for one), so several places of one outage never read as a stack of boxes. React's `EmptyState` and `Section`'s `unavailable` take the same default since 0.25.1; before it React drew the box unless `frame={false}` was passed.

**CommandCard** (DOM and React `CommandCard`; since 0.15.0, DQ-13; scoped decisions since 0.16.0, CNV-10). Since 0.16.0 `decisions` replaces Allow and Deny with the product's scoped decisions, `{ value, label, kind: 'allow'|'deny', keys?, note?, said? }` ("Allow once", "Allow for this conversation", "Deny and say why"), answered by `ondecide({ value, kind, note, command, scope })` (React `onDecide`): the first allow takes Enter and the first deny without a note Escape unless `keys` says otherwise; a decision with `note` first shows its field ("Tell it what to do instead"), whose Enter sends it and whose Escape goes back; the card then says the decision's `said` (or its label). `editable: true` lets the person correct the command before allowing (a mono field, never answered by Escape); the answer carries the corrected `command`. 0.15.0's `allow`/`deny` work as before. A request waiting for the person's answer: an attention edge on a raised surface, its title, the command whole in a wrapping mono block (folded past `CommandCard.lines`, 8, with Show all), an optional `scope` ("Always allow commands like this", passed to the answer), Allow (primary, ⏎) and Deny (Esc) with their keys in `.bui-keys` (hidden on touch). Enter allows and Escape denies only while focus is inside the card; `focus()` takes the person there on arrival. One answer at a time (`allow`/`deny` may return a promise; `aria-busy` meanwhile); a failed answer is said in place and the answers stay; then `state` is `allowed` or `denied` and the answers are replaced by the outcome.

**Skeletons** (since 0.15.0, DQ-16). Draw what comes in its own shape while it loads, never a spinner and a sentence: `skeleton({ rows, kind: 'list' })` for rows (a mark and two lines, as `ListRow`), `kind: 'table'` for a table (three cells a row; `DataTable` and `Collection` draw it themselves), `skeleton(n)` for lines of text. Draw what is already known at once (a title the sidebar holds, a header's facts) and the skeleton only for the rest; skeletons are hidden from assistive technology, which hears the named `loading` line instead. **A region's first read** (since 0.23.0, LOAD-01, the family's stable loading from Conduict's Home) waits in place with `waiting({ rows, kind, label, language, frame })` (React `Waiting`): one announced "Loading…" line (`role="status"`, «Cargando…» with `language: 'es'`, or a named `loading.text`) above a skeleton in the content's shape (`kind: 'list'`, one row by default; never a made-up count), on the raised frame the region's content will take, or unframed (`frame: false`) inside a region that is framed already. `Section` takes `waiting` (true or a shape) and `wait(shape)`: the heading and its actions stay, the body waits and the section is `aria-busy` until `children` is set. Draw the page's header, its actions and its regions at once and wait inside each region; a page-wide skeleton or spinner is only for a page whose structure is not known yet. Since 0.23.0 every skeleton line and mark is drawn in the divider role (`--color-border-strong`), after the sunken surface read as a blank page in the dark theme. **A wait is seen only when its read is slow** (since 0.31.0, D185): a region's wait and every placeholder (`waiting`, `Section`'s and React's `Waiting`, `skeleton`, `Collection`'s and `DataTable`'s placeholder rows) are in place, busy and announced at once, but seen only past a 300 ms grace, then fade in over `--motion-standard`; under reduced motion they appear at the grace with no fading. A read that answers within the grace goes straight to its content, and one that answers just after leaves no flash. Nothing waits for the wait: content replaces it the moment it arrives. A placeholder inside a wait is not graced a second time. A loading line that answers the person's own action (`loading`, a busy button) is not a region's wait and shows at once.

**CommandPalette** (DOM and React `CommandPalette`; since 0.16.0, DQ-21). ⌘K / Ctrl+K as the product's main way to go anywhere and do anything, keyboard first: a modal dialog (top layer) with no title bar and one large field (a `combobox` over a `listbox`), results grouped by the `sources` the product contributes, and a product hosting others (Beyond Home) adds theirs (`sources` setter). A source is `{ key, label, search(query, { signal }), empty? }`: asked as the person types (after `delay`, 120 ms), aborted when the query changes, bounded by `bound` (4 s: a source that does not answer says "Not available right now" in its group and never holds the others back); `empty: true` sources answer before anything is typed, after the person's `recent` items (kept on this device only, items with an address, `{ key, limit }`). Up and Down move across groups and wrap, Enter opens the active item (`run(item)`, else its `href` through `onnavigate` or the address; ⌘/Ctrl+Enter a new tab), Escape clears the field and then closes, a press outside closes, focus returns where it was. An item may show its own shortcut (`keys`, hidden on touch). `shortcut: true` listens for ⌘K / Ctrl+K (`keys` says it for the product's own button); a product keeps one palette per page and does not also enable the sidebar's ⌘K. Since 0.28.0 an option's label and detail wrap rather than cut, the detail going under the label when both do not share a line: the palette is an opened list, where a name is whole (D44) and an entry's "…" says it asks for more before it acts (D179). React: the same props, a ref with `open(query?)`, `close()`, `toggle()`. `CommandPalette.labels.en` / `.es`.

**ChoiceCards** (DOM and React `ChoiceCards`; since 0.16.0, DQ-15). A choice among a few options a person compares (a machine's size, where it runs, a plan): native radios in a `fieldset` with its `legend`, each drawn as a card with its `title`, one line of `detail`, short `facts` (cores, memory, price) and an optional `status`; the chosen card on the selection's surface. The arrows move and choose, Space chooses; the choice applies at once (`onchange`), never through a Save. A `disabled` card stays visible with its `reason` and is never chosen. `value` sets without calling `onchange`.

**Primaries** (DOM and React export `Primaries`; since 0.16.0, DQ-09). One primary action per region, checked while a product is developed: a page header, a section, a dialog, a side sheet, a composer, an approval card, an empty state or any `[data-bui-region]` holding two or more shown `.bui-button-primary` of its own is named in a console warning, once per region. `Primaries.enabled` is true in a development build (a bundler defining `process.env.NODE_ENV` as `development`); a `Page` (DOM and React) starts one by itself then, and a product may start one over its own root (`new Primaries({ root })`, `check()`). It never changes the page.

**Hierarchy** (DOM and React export `Hierarchy`; since 0.19.0, D158). A heading outranks what it names, checked while a product is developed: every shown heading (`h1`–`h6`) is semibold, and no text in the region it names (the element whose `aria-labelledby` names it, else its nearest `main`, `section`, `article`, `aside`, dialog, region or `[data-bui-region]`) is larger than it, or as large and heavier. A console warning names the heading and the text that outranks it ("the heading \"Load\" (12 px, 600) is outranked in the region it names by \"Processor\" (13 px, 400)"), once per heading. A heading that is a label by design (it groups a list's or a menu's items, as a notification day does, or names one item of a list, as a diff's file does) is quieter than its rows and says so with `data-bui-label`; it is not checked. `Hierarchy.enabled` is true in a development build; a `Page` (DOM and React) starts one by itself then, and so does an open `SideSheet` and `Dialog`, which sit outside the page. A product may start one over its own root (`new Hierarchy({ root })`, `check()`, `measure` and `wait` for tests). It never changes the page.

**Ellipses** (DOM and React export `Ellipses`; since 0.28.0, D179). A visible action is named whole, checked while a product is developed: a shown button, an element with the button role or a link styled as an action (`.bui-button`, `.bui-link-button`) whose visible words end in "…" (or "...") outside a menu is named in a console warning ("the action \"Choose a copy…\" ends in \"…\" outside a menu"), once per action. Inside a menu, a listbox or the command palette (`Ellipses.menus`) the "…" stays: it says the entry asks for more before it acts. A disabled or busy action is not checked, since a wait says "Saving…" while it runs (a busy `Button`'s `{label}…`); words only for assistive technology (`.bui-hidden`) do not count. `Ellipses.enabled` is true in a development build; a `Page` (DOM and React) starts one by itself then, and a product may start one over its own root (`new Ellipses({ root })`, `check()`, `Ellipses.words(node)`). It never changes the page.

**Writing a panel's parts (D158).** The type steps down from what names a region to what it holds: a side sheet's title (title-2, semibold), its parts as `Section` at `level: 3` (title-3, semibold), their sentences body text, their labels and notes small and muted, and their ways on one `ActionLine`. Every sentence of the same role takes one style, the empty one ("Nothing runs on it now.") included; a label never reads above the heading that names it; and a region's heading is never drawn as a label.

**Status, Badge, Callout, Loading, Skeleton.** `status(label, tone)` is a state with a dot and words (since 0.15.0 its mark takes the tone's shape: `warning` a diamond, `danger` a square, `progress` a half-filled circle since 0.18.4); `callout()` takes `details` (`{ request, time, text }`, since 0.15.0, D43) and draws "Technical details" under a failure; `badge` a short tag; `callout({ tone, title, body, actions, live })` a message block (`live` announces, assertively for danger); `loading(label)` an announced waiting line, whose mark since 0.21.0 is work in progress's half-filled circle in the colour of work in progress, pulsing a few times and then still (0.17.0–0.20.4 drew a ring with a dot, which read as a choice to make), and `loading.step({ step, language })` its words for a step of opening (`session`, `signin`, `projects`; `loading.steps`), as the document's opening says it; `skeleton(lines)` hidden placeholders, which since 0.17.0 keep the content's shape without a continuous pulse. Tones: `neutral`, `success`, `warning`, `danger`, `info` (and `progress` for status). `loading.labels` (React `Loading.labels`; 0.7.2) holds the default label, "Loading…" / «Cargando…». Since 0.7.6 a loading line can name what it waits for: `loading.text({ name, kind, language })` fills `loading.names` (`loading` "Loading {name}…", `opening` "Opening {name}…", `reading` "Reading {name}…"; «Cargando», «Abriendo», «Leyendo»), where `name` is the thing as the product calls it, article included ("the conversation", «la conversación»); React `Loading.names` and `Loading.text` are the same.

**availability** (DOM and React `availability`; since 0.2.0). The family's one availability vocabulary (decision D20), in order, each with its tone: Available (`success`), Closed access (`warning`), In preparation (`info`), Planned (`neutral`), Retired (`neutral`). Entries are frozen `{ key, label, tone }` with the keys `available`, `closed`, `preparation`, `planned` and `retired`; products show these words and no others, in sentence case, passing their own language by `key`: Since 0.7.2 each entry also carries `labels: { en, es }` (`state.labels.es`), so the table below is in the package.

| `closed` | Closed access | Acceso cerrado |

**Unavailable** (DOM `Unavailable`, React `Unavailable`; since 0.2.0). "Not available here" (decision D06): a missing permission, admission, association or capability explained in place instead of a refusal card, a raw error envelope or a hidden button. Options: `title` (what is unavailable), `reason` (why), `owner` (who can change it, after "Who can change this: ", `labels.owner`), `action` and `secondary` (nodes in the DOM, React content in React), `kind` (`access` with a lock, `association` with a plug, `capability` with an information mark, and since 0.15.0 `outage` with a globe for a service that does not answer, never a denial, PKG-16), `details` (since 0.15.0, D43: `{ request, time, text }`, drawn as the closed "Technical details"), `code` (the refusal's code as a neutral tag) and `level` (the heading, 2 to 6, default 2). Its tones are warning, information and neutral, never danger: it must not look like a failure of the product. It decides nothing: the product states the refusal it received. `Unavailable.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.1; React `Unavailable.labels`) carry "Who can change this: " / «Quién puede cambiarlo: ».

**An outage that tries again by itself** (DOM `Again`, `AgainLine`, `Unavailable`'s `again`; React `useAgain`, `AgainLine`, `Unavailable`'s `again`; since 0.17.1, D132). `new Again({ run })` reads again after 5, 15, 30 and 60 seconds, then every 60 seconds (`Again.steps`), at once when the browser comes back online and when the page is shown again; a try that falls due while the page is hidden waits for it to be shown. One try at a time: `now()` (a Try again) while a try runs gets that try. `run` answers by resolving with anything but `false`, which ends the waiting whatever the answer was, a refusal included; it did not answer when it rejects or resolves `false`. Only reads are tried again: a change whose answer did not arrive has an unknown outcome and is never sent again by itself. `start()` counts the failed read that found the outage as the first try; `stop()` ends it; `listen(listener)` hears `{ active, trying, last, next }`. Timers, clock, window and document are injectable (`timers`, `now`, `events`, `page`). `AgainLine` says "Last tried 12:04 · trying again by itself" / «Último intento 12:04 · se vuelve a intentar por sí solo» under the outage, "Trying again…" / «Intentándolo de nuevo…» while a try runs, hidden once answered, never a live region (`AgainLine.labels`, `AgainLine.say(moments, { labels, locale })`). `Unavailable` with `kind: 'outage'` and `again` (the read, or `Again`'s options) owns the schedule and the line, offers "Try again" / «Reintentar» (`labels.retry`) when the product passes no `action` (a product's own Try again calls `unavailable.again.now()`), and stops when destroyed; `again` is ignored for every other kind. React: `useAgain(failing, run, options)` returns `{ active, trying, last, next, now }`, starting while `failing` and stopping when it is false or the place goes away, `run` read from the latest props; React `Unavailable` takes the same `again` and `locale`.

**One outage, said once** (DOM `Recovery`, `LastUpdate`; React `Recovery`, `useRecovery`, `LastUpdate`; since 0.24.0, D163, the owner's direction of 2026-10-23). When reads of one page fail on one cause, `Recovery` says it in one line in the warning tone on the column's edge: the globe mark, who did not answer (`title`, from the family's catalog of causes), `AgainLine` and one Try again at the line's end (under the words below 480 px). It lists nothing: `set(key, { title, details, run, automatic })` registers a read, whose `title` and evidence appear only in its one Technical details (a row per read: what it is, its request, its time; the words once when every read said the same). Each region says it in its own place: `EmptyState`'s `outage`, `Section`'s `unavailable`, and over content kept from an earlier read `LastUpdate` ("Last updated 12:04", «Última actualización: 12:04»; "May be out of date" when the time is not known) or `Section`'s `updated`. A `Collection` joins with `recovery`, `outage(error)` and `details(error)`. `whole` (with `level`) makes the notice the page's body in `Unavailable`'s form when nothing on the page could be read; `title` and `whole` can change in place. React: `useRecovery(options)` makes a page's notice once (null until mounted) for `<Recovery notice />` and `<Collection recovery />`; `<Recovery title reads whole />` makes its own from props, a read leaving it when it leaves `reads`. See [recovery](recovery.md). Classes: `bui-recovery` (`data-recovery`: `line`, `page`), `bui-recovery-mark`, `bui-recovery-words`, `bui-recovery-title`, `bui-recovery-actions`, `bui-empty-outage`, `bui-last-update`, `bui-details-table`, `bui-details-said`.

**StatusRow** (DOM and React `StatusRow`; since 0.7.0, design S3, E54). One thing with one state, such as a GitHub connection, a person's GitHub account, an AI engine or a service connection: a region named by its `title`, with its `kind` beside it, a tone glyph, the state with how fresh it is (`state: { label, tone, checked, connected }` drawn by `Freshness`: "Suspended · Checked 2 min ago", or "Last known: …" with a neutral mark while the source is disconnected), the `reason` in words, "Who can change this: …" (`owner`), a few `facts`, then the state's one `action` and "More actions for {title}" (`more`, an `ActionMenu`). `update(values)` replaces any part. React `StatusRow` takes `action` as React content and `more` with `onSelect`. `StatusRow.labels.en` / `.es` (and `labels.freshness`).

**CopyMessage** (DOM and React `CopyMessage`; since 0.7.0, FR-5, D56 shared piece 9). A prewritten text a person copies to send or run elsewhere: a message for whoever can act ("Could you approve the Beyond app for acme on GitHub? …"), a link for them (`kind: 'link'`) or a command with its values filled (`kind: 'command'`, kept in its lines and scrolling in its own box), read-only and whole, with an optional `label` above and one action, "Copy message", "Copy link" or "Copy command", that says "Copied". The copy is bounded (`CopyMessage.bound`, 5 s, D40): a refused, missing or silent clipboard is said in place and the text is selected for the keyboard. The `text` setter replaces it. `CopyMessage.labels.en` / `.es`. Its holder carries its kind's class (`.bui-copy.bui-copy-command`), as does a command's code box (`pre.bui-copy-text.bui-copy-command`): since 0.34.0 the code's face, lines and scrolling and the inset's trim name the code box alone, so the holder's label, Copy and result keep the interface's face and its reading measure is counted in it (before, a command's whole holder took the monospace face, found by Beyond Home's adoption). Style the code box with both classes.

**CopyButton** (DOM and React `CopyButton`; since 0.11.2, Conduict's review minor 19). One action that copies a text and confirms it in place, never with a toast: the button says "Copied" / «Copiado» with the `check` glyph for `CopyButton.hold` (2 s), then its words again, and one polite region says it once. `text` is a string or a function read at each press (a link, a path); `label` ("Copy" by default, "Copy link", "Copy path"), `name` (its accessible name when the words are not enough, "Copy the path src/app.js"; it says the result while shown), `variant` (quiet by default), `small` (true), `onresult(copied)`, `labels` (`CopyButton.labels.en` / `.es`: `copy`, `copied`, `refused` "Not copied" / «No copiado», `said`, `why`, `selected`). The copy is bounded (`CopyButton.bound`, 5 s, D40): a refusal says "Not copied" on the button and why in the region, and when the text is shown somewhere `select()` returns that node, which is selected so the keyboard copies it. `copy()` copies and resolves whether it worked; `result` is `copied` or `refused` while said. Since 0.26.0 (D165) `bare: true` draws the glyph alone (`copy`, or `glyph: 'link'` for a link), its words its name and tooltip: a copy shows the `check` for `CopyButton.hold` with "Copied" as its name, a refusal keeps its glyph with "Not copied", and the region says either once. Class `bui-copy-button` (`data-result`; `bui-copy-bare` when bare), `bui-copy-button-said`.

**Bytes** (DOM and React export `Bytes`; since 0.11.2, Conduict's review minor 8). One size said the same everywhere: `new Bytes({ locale?, bytes? }).of(count)` says "300 B" below a thousand bytes (where `Intl` says "300 byte") and "12 kB", "6.3 MB" ("6,3 MB" in Spanish) above, in decimal units; null for what is not a number. The composer's attachments use it; a product's own lines (a sent message's files) use it too.

**ProviderWindow** (DOM and React `ProviderWindow`; since 0.7.0, FR-G2, design S2 2.3 to 2.4). A provider's own window, such as GitHub's installation page or Supabase's authorization: "Continue to {provider}" opens `href` (the product's start address, which holds the attempt on its server) in a window from the press; while it is open, an `Awaited` card "Finish on {provider}" ("Taking longer than usual" past the 90th percentile of `expected`, by default 2 and 10 min) with "Complete the steps in the {provider} window. This page updates by itself.", **Reopen the {provider} window** and **Cancel**. A message `{ type: 'beyond-provider' }` from `origin`, and only from it, or the window closing (polled, the only signal a cross-origin window gives) makes it call the product's `read()`, bounded by `bound` (20 s): `{ state: 'done' }` ends it (`onend('done', answer)`), `waiting` hands the product a state it draws itself (an owner's approval), anything else after the window closed says "The {provider} window closed before you finished. Nothing was connected." with Continue again; a read that fails or does not answer says it is not known yet, with **Check again**. A blocked window offers **Continue in this tab** and **Try the window again**; on a touch screen or inside a frame (`same: 'auto'`) the address opens in this tab and the product calls `check()` when the person comes back. The outcome is always the server's, never the window's. `state` is `idle`, `open`, `blocked`, `checking`, `closed`, `unknown`, `done` or `away`; the React ref exposes `open()`, `check()`, `cancel()` and `state`. `ProviderWindow.labels.en` / `.es` (and `labels.awaited`). Since 0.7.4 the window loses its opener; since 0.7.5 it opens blank on this origin, loses its opener there and then goes to `href`, the attempt is also read every `interval` ms (2500) while the window stays open, for at most `follow` (15 min), one read at a time, a same-origin `BroadcastChannel` (`channel`, 'beyond-provider') wakes it, and the page closes the window only while it is on this origin: a landing on another origin closes itself.

**Toaster** (`Toaster`; React `useToaster`). Transient outcome messages, one region per page: successes and information announced politely and removed after `duration`; failures assertive and kept until dismissed. A toast says what just happened on this screen; it is never stored and is not a notification. `Toaster.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.1; React `useToaster(useToaster.labels.es)`) carry the region's name and Dismiss. An outcome repeats the action's verb in the past tense with its object ("Project deleted", "Proyecto borrado"); the package offers no helper for it, because past tenses are the product's language. Since 0.1.6 the toaster sits `--bui-toaster-bottom` above the viewport's bottom edge (default `--space-4`), so a surface with a bottom bar of its own, such as the Desktop's dock, raises it there.

## Long operations

Since 0.5.0 (decision D50, approved on 2026-10-03, and the suite's long-operations guide). Work that takes minutes is followed, never guessed: these components are drawn from the operation's record (which lives on the server) and a clock, so a reload, another tab or a return an hour later shows the same. "Usually about X" is the **measured median**; "Taking longer than usual" starts **past the 90th percentile** (strictly), not at the median. Times keep current with the clock, never through a live region; a polite live region (`aria-live="polite"`) says only what changed. Nothing moves under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Every component takes `locale` for times of day (24-hour) and `labels`, and carries its English and Spanish copy as `Component.labels.en` / `.es` (frozen; in React too, `Steps.labels.es`), so a product in Spanish passes `labels: Steps.labels.es` instead of writing its own. Durations use the units `second`, `minute`, `hour`, `day` ("{count} s", "{count} min", "{count} h", "{count} d", the same in Spanish).

**Clock** (DOM and React `Clock`). The page's time and beat: `new Clock({ now?, every? })` (`now` returns milliseconds, `Date.now` by default; `every` the beat, 1000 ms). One timer while anything listens (`subscribe(listener)` returns the release), none otherwise; `tick()` re-evaluates every listener at once; `Clock.system` is the shared clock on the system time, the default of every component. Tests and products inject `now`; a product passes one clock to every component of a page. A throwing listener goes to `reportError` and does not stop the others.

**Steps** (DOM `Steps`, React `Steps`). The steps of a long operation. Options: `label` (the list's name, for assistive technology), `steps`, `clock`, `locale`, `labels` (`labels.details` reaches the technical details). Each step: `{ id?, label, state, since?, until?, expected?: { median, p90 }, phase?: { label, since? }, reason?: text | { text, details?: { text, request, time } } }`, with `state` one of `done`, `progress`, `stalled` (it finished its part and something else blocks it), `failed` and `waiting` (not begun; an unknown state reads as waiting). A finished step with both times says "Took {duration}" ("Took 1 min 24 s"); the step in progress says "{elapsed} so far · usually about {median}" (the median rounded to one unit), or "{elapsed} so far" without an expected time, and past its 90th percentile "Taking longer than usual · {elapsed} so far · usually about {median}" (`.bui-step-slow`); its phase reads "Now: {phase} · {elapsed}" ("Last phase: {phase}" on a step that stopped); a stalled or failed step shows its reason with "Technical details" folded under it. Every state is in words: "In progress", "Blocked" and "Failed" as words beside the name (`bui-step-state`, muted, in the warning or danger hue when blocked or failed; since 0.18.4 no status mark of its own, since the step's mark already gives the state its shape), "Done" and "Waiting" as hidden text; the mark is a check, a pulsing dot, an alert, a cross or the step's number, never the only sign. The step in progress has `aria-current="step"`. The `steps` setter (React prop) redraws; a step whose state changed (by `id`, else by place) is announced ("Startup: Done"), and so is a step that becomes slow, once ("Startup: taking longer than usual"). `tick()` re-evaluates now; `announced` reads the last words said. Since 0.10.0 `announce: false` (React prop `announce`) leaves out the steps' own live region, for a page that says changes through one region of its own (a plan inside a conversation): nothing is announced and `announced` still holds the words, for the page to say. Classes: `bui-steps`, `bui-steps-list`, `bui-step`, `bui-step-{state}` (and `data-state`), `bui-step-mark`, `bui-step-ring`, `bui-step-body`, `bui-step-head`, `bui-step-label`, `bui-step-state`, `bui-step-time`, `bui-step-phase`, `bui-step-reason`, `bui-step-slow`, `bui-announcer`.

Labels: `done` "Done" / «Hecho», `progress` "In progress" / «En curso», `stalled` "Blocked" / «Bloqueado», `failed` "Failed" / «Falló», `waiting` "Waiting" / «En espera», `took` "Took {duration}" / «Tardó {duration}», `running` "{elapsed} so far" / «{elapsed} hasta ahora», `usual` "{elapsed} so far · usually about {expected}" / «{elapsed} hasta ahora · suele tardar unos {expected}», `slow` "Taking longer than usual · {elapsed} so far · usually about {expected}" / «Está tardando más de lo habitual · …», `phase` "Now: {phase} · {elapsed}" / «Ahora: {phase} · {elapsed}», `stopped` "Last phase: {phase}" / «Última fase: {phase}», `change` "{step}: {state}", `late` "{step}: taking longer than usual" / «{step}: está tardando más de lo habitual».

**Awaited** (DOM `Awaited`, React `Awaited`). One card that moves while a person waits (Conduict's E52, generalized): its title says what is happening (`title`, a heading of `level`, 2 by default) and since when ("Since 19:47", or the day and time on another day); the time left of the usual one, "About {duration} left" (whole minutes rounded up) or "Less than a minute left", counted toward the median and, from the median on (state `late`), toward the 90th percentile; past the 90th percentile "Taking longer than usual" with **Check again**; a bar (`<progress>`) toward the usual time that only the end completes (up to 0.9 at the median and 0.95 at the 90th percentile), indeterminate when the time cannot be told (no `since` or `expected`) or has passed, with the time as its `aria-valuetext` (since 0.19.1, under reduced motion an indeterminate bar is a still dashed track along its whole width, never a band held in one place that would read as a share done); the operation's `steps` inside (a `Steps`); and, once `reason` is given (`{ text, way?, action?: { label, href? | run? }, details? }`), why it cannot continue and the way on in place of the time and the bar, with its action, its technical details and **Check again**. **Check again** runs `check()` once at a time (a second press is ignored; the button shows it is running) and, if the check fails, says "The check did not finish. Try again." in place. `end(outcome)` (`'done'` or `'failed'`; React prop `ended`) completes the bar for `done`, says "Done" or "Did not finish", announces the end and calls `onend(outcome)` (React `onEnd`) once: the hook where a product says "Ready · …" in the tab's title or leaves a notice. `update(values)` (React props) replaces `title`, `since`, `expected`, `steps`, `reason` or `check`; `state` reads `progress`, `late`, `slow`, `stalled`, `done` or `failed` (also `data-state`); `ended` the outcome. It announces becoming slow, a reason and the end. Classes: `bui-awaited`, `bui-awaited-head`, `bui-awaited-mark`, `bui-awaited-heading`, `bui-awaited-title`, `bui-awaited-since`, `bui-awaited-time`, `bui-awaited-bar`, `bui-awaited-body`, `bui-awaited-reason`, `bui-awaited-why`, `bui-awaited-actions`, `bui-awaited-note`. It sits in the page's flow, with no elevation.

**AwaitedLine** (DOM `AwaitedLine`, React `AwaitedLine`; since 0.7.1, design S5). `Awaited` in one line, for a row of a list (a clone, an update): the title, then "40 s so far · usually about 1 min" (the median as "usually about"), past the 90th percentile, strictly, "Taking longer than usual · 12 min so far" with **Check again** (`check`, one run at a time, a failure said in place), with a `reason` why it cannot continue in place of the time, and after `end(outcome)` (React `ended`) "Done · took 52 s" or "Did not finish", calling `onend` (React `onEnd`) once. Without `expected` it says the time so far alone, never a guess. Drawn from the record and the `Clock` only; a polite live region says becoming slow, a reason and the end, never the ticking time. A spinner, a check or an alert mark leads it; it wraps at narrow widths and is never cut. `update`, `tick`, `again`, `state`, `ended`, `announced`, `text`; `AwaitedLine.labels.en` / `.es`.

Labels: `since` "Since {time}" / «Desde {time}», `left` "About {duration} left" / «Queda aproximadamente {duration}», `brief` "Less than a minute left" / «Queda menos de un minuto», `slow` "Taking longer than usual" / «Está tardando más de lo habitual», `unknown` "Time left unknown" / «Tiempo restante desconocido», `progress` "Progress: {title}" / «Progreso: {title}», `check` "Check again" / «Comprobar de nuevo», `unchecked` "The check did not finish. Try again." / «La comprobación no terminó. Vuelve a intentarlo.», `done` "Done" / «Listo», `failed` "Did not finish" / «No terminó», and the announcements `ended` "{title}: done", `stopped` "{title}: did not finish", `late` "{title}: taking longer than usual", `blocked` "{title}: {reason}"; `labels.steps` and `labels.details` reach the parts.

**Freshness** (DOM `Freshness`, React `Freshness`). A state with how fresh it is: the state's word with its dot (`label`, `tone`: a status tone, or an `availability` entry's `label` and `tone`) and "Checked 2 min ago" (`checked`; "Checked just now" under `Freshness.recent`, 5 s), kept current by the clock. With `connected: false` it reads "Last known: Running · 10:42" (or "Last known: Running" without `checked`) with a neutral dot and muted words, so a lost live channel never reads as the work having stopped. The words always carry the meaning. `update(values)` (React props), `stale`, `text`. Labels: `checked` "Checked {duration} ago" / «Comprobado hace {duration}», `now` "Checked just now" / «Comprobado ahora mismo», `known` "Last known: {state} · {time}" / «Último estado conocido: {state} · {time}», `last` "Last known: {state}" / «Último estado conocido: {state}». Classes: `bui-freshness`, `bui-freshness-state`, `bui-freshness-age`, `bui-freshness-stale`.

**TechnicalDetails** (DOM `TechnicalDetails`, React `TechnicalDetails`; D43 as amended on 2026-10-03). A closed native disclosure, "Technical details", holding the source's own words (`text`, preformatted), the request identifier (`request`) and the time (`time`, shown in the person's language), with **Copy details**, which copies all of it for support: the words, then "Request: …" and "Time: …" in ISO 8601 (`report`). It copies through the Clipboard API under `TechnicalDetails.bound` (5 s); copied, it says "Copied"; refused (no API, no permission or no answer), it says so in place and selects the text for the keyboard. Both are said in a `role="status"` line. `update(values)`, `open`, `copy()` (resolves whether it copied). Since 0.24.0 (D163) `entries` (`{ label, text, request, time }`) are the reads one incident affected: a table with a row each (`affected` "Affected" / «Afectado», request, time), the words once above it when every read said the same and under their row otherwise, copied as one block per read. Labels: `summary` "Technical details" / «Detalles técnicos», `affected`, `request` "Request" / «Solicitud», `time` "Time" / «Hora», `copy` "Copy details" / «Copiar detalles», `copied` "Copied" / «Copiado», `refused` "Could not copy. The details are selected: copy them with your keyboard." / «No se pudo copiar. Los detalles están seleccionados: cópialos con el teclado.». Classes: `bui-details`, `bui-details-summary`, `bui-details-body`, `bui-details-content`, `bui-details-text`, `bui-details-facts`, `bui-details-actions`, `bui-details-result`, `bui-details-refused`. Since 0.11.2 its summary is the family's one disclosure affordance (Conduict's review, minor 12): `<summary class="bui-summary">` with the `chevron` glyph before its words, turned toward them while closed and down while open (no movement under reduced motion), in the text color, never a link's. A product's own `<details>` uses the same: `el('summary', { class: 'bui-summary' }, [icon('chevron'), words])`.

```js
import { Awaited, Clock, Steps } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const clock = new Clock();                         // one per page
const card = new Awaited({
	title: 'Starting My first VM', since: record.started, expected: { median: 120_000, p90: 240_000 },
	steps: record.steps, check: () => relay.refresh(), clock,
	onend: outcome => (document.title = `${outcome === 'done' ? 'Ready' : 'Not ready'} · My first VM`),
	labels: { ...Awaited.labels.es, steps: Steps.labels.es }
}).mount(main);
relay.onchange = record => (record.ended ? card.end(record.outcome) : card.update({ steps: record.steps, reason: record.reason }));
```

React: `<Awaited title since expected steps reason={{ text, way, action: { label, href | onSelect }, details }} check ended onEnd clock locale level labels />`, `<Steps label steps clock locale labels />`, `<Freshness label tone checked connected clock locale labels />`, `<TechnicalDetails text request time entries open locale labels />`. Records are applied when their content changes; `clock`, `locale`, `labels` (memoize) and `level` create a new instance; `check`, `onEnd` and `reason.action.onSelect` are read from the latest props.

## Header and notifications

**Lockup** (DOM `lockup`, React `Lockup`; since 0.1.6). The family treatment of the product name: the Beyond wordmark with the product name beside it, set apart by a divider, the same in every product. The consumer passes the wordmark asset it carries (`src`; the package ships no brand asset), `name` and, when needed, `alt` (default `Beyond`). The name is sized to the wordmark's cap height and aligned to its letters, not to its image box, whose lower part holds the drips of the O; `--bui-lockup-height` (default 21px) scales both. In the family header, pass `brand.lockup: { src, name }` (since 0.1.7). The owner rejected proposal R06 on 2026-09-29: the product name stays beside the wordmark, through this one lockup. Since 0.2.0 the name's box is trimmed to its cap height (`text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic`) where the browser supports it, so its cap top and baseline meet the letters' within 0.02 px at every height from 18 to 40 px in Chrome and WebKit and within 0.1 px in Firefox; without that support the earlier placement applies (up to 1.7 px off, measured by forcing it in those engines).

**FamilyBar** (DOM `FamilyBar`, React `FamilyBar`; since 0.2.0). The one bar every signed-in product renders (decisions D01 and D04), built on `Header`: navy in both themes, `--layout-family` tall, sticky at the top (`position: sticky; top: 0`, `z-index: var(--bui-family-layer, 45)`, below dialogs and toasts), one row from 1440 to 320 px. It renders the `beyond-family/1` descriptor the product relays from Beyond Projects (`GET /v1/family`). Ages are said at each update: a product sets `groups` again to keep them current. Since 0.11.1 the wording is `Age`'s (below), so a product's own list says an age as the sidebar does. A group of entries may say how many items it holds with `count`, beside its heading ("Recent, 12": the number visible, the comma for assistive technology; the list is named with it). Classes: `bui-sidebar-age`, `bui-sidebar-count`.
- **Age** (DOM and React export `Age`; since 0.11.1). The sidebar's age wording for a product's own lists (a table of conversations, a person's work across projects), with no DOM: `new Age({ locale?, labels?, now? }).of(moment)` answers `{ label, title, datetime }` (the short words, the full moment for assistive technology and a tooltip, the ISO string for `<time datetime>`), or null for no readable moment. `labels` default to the locale's language (Spanish for `es`, else English), `Age.labels.en` / `.es` (`now`, `minutes`, `hours`, `days`, `weeks`, the same units as `Sidebar.labels`), and a product may replace any; `now()` is measured at each `of`, so a product calls it again to keep an age current; `Age.moment(value)` reads a Date, milliseconds or an ISO string. Draw it as the sidebar does: the short words shown (`aria-hidden`) and the full moment in visually hidden text and the tooltip, in a `<time datetime>`, never a live region.
- **Top action** (`action: { label, href, glyph? }`, since 0.10.0): a link above the search with the `plus` glyph beside its words ("New conversation"), routed like any destination (`onnavigate`, the drawer closing). Class `bui-sidebar-action`.
- **Search** (`search: { label, placeholder?, source({ query, signal }), bound?, delay?, all?(query) }`, since 0.10.0): a search field named by `label` (its placeholder, the label by default) above the groups. Typing waits `delay` (250 ms) and asks the product's `source`, which answers entries (an array or `{ items }`); a newer query aborts the older one and its late answer is ignored. While a query is typed the results take the groups' place: "Searching…", the entries found under "Results" with "See all results" (`all(query)`, the product's full list), "Nothing matches “{query}”." or, when the source fails or does not answer within `bound` (20 s, D40, aborted), "The search didn't answer, so results can't be listed now." with **Try again** (never "nothing matches"). A polite region says the count ("3 results") or the sentence once per answer, never while typing. Escape clears the field and brings the groups back (an empty field lets Escape through, so a second Escape closes the drawer); Enter opens every result when `all` is given. Both forms share one search: a query typed in one is the other's. Classes: `bui-sidebar-search`, `bui-sidebar-query`, `bui-sidebar-results`, `bui-sidebar-line`, `bui-sidebar-retry`.
- **Objects, folding and the shortcut** (since 0.14.0, D97 and D98, approved in S29; SB-01 to SB-07 of Conduict's audit). Then come its groups of entries and its `more` link ("All conversations"). The heading of every group of entries is a disclosure button (`bui-sidebar-fold`, the chevron, `aria-expanded`) that keeps its count folded ("Recent, 3"); folded, the group lists only its current item; `fold: false` keeps a plain heading; sections never fold. The choice is kept per device and product (`localStorage` `bui-sidebar:{product}:{group key}`, `sidebar.folds`; storage that throws keeps it for the page). A group whose items need the person (`attention: true`) keeps its tone folded and opens by itself when an item that needs the person arrives. `shortcut: true` (React `shortcut`) makes ⌘K (Ctrl+K elsewhere) open the search, the drawer first below the cut; it is off by default, a product enables it, `sidebar.shortcut = false` lets a host keep the key, a key a host already handled (`defaultPrevented`) is left to it, and no single key ever opens it (WCAG 2.1.4). Inside Beyond Home's windowed host the application window's own document receives the key while it has focus. `sidebar.seek()` opens the search as the key does. Below the cut the objects' `action` with `row: true` ends the product row (SB-07). The current item is drawn in the text color at weight 500 with the accent bar and its surface, never in the link color (D98, every product). **Migration:** the top `action` and `search` keep working where they were; a product adopts the new order by moving them into an object group (`{ kind: 'object', heading, action, search, groups: [its entries groups], more }`) and dropping the top ones; its groups of entries fold from 0.14.0 unless they say `fold: false`. The context line stays the first child of each copy's `nav` with its class `bui-sidebar-context`: a stable hook a product may anchor its own line after (Conduict's presence line). Classes: `bui-sidebar-object`, `bui-sidebar-object-head` (`data-searching`), `bui-sidebar-object-title`, `bui-sidebar-object-action`, `bui-sidebar-seek`, `bui-sidebar-object-groups`, `bui-sidebar-fold`, `bui-sidebar-fold-label`, `bui-sidebar-group` (`data-folded`, `data-attention`), `bui-sidebar-start`.

**ProductNav** (DOM `ProductNav`, React `ProductNav`; since 0.2.0). A product's own navigation as a light row of tabs under the family bar: `items` (`{ label, href, current }`), `label` (the navigation's name, default `labels.nav` "Product"), `onnavigate` (React `onNavigate`) for plain clicks, `sticky` to stay under the bar (not sticky by default). **Header** (`Header`). The shared header primitive (signed-in products render `FamilyBar`, which is built on it): `brand` (link home: text, the family lockup with `lockup`, a `logo` node or an `image`; with a picture, `label` is the link's one accessible name and the picture is not read, since 0.1.7), `context` (breadcrumb entries), `nav` (product links), `notifications` and `account` slots, `onnavigate` for routed applications. **NotificationEntry** (`NotificationEntry`). The header bell with the unread count and a panel of recent items. Options: `adapter`, `href` (the full inbox), `onopen(destination)`, `onview`, `products` (display names), `locale`, `limit`, `interval`. The count is hidden while unknown, after a failure and while unavailable. A summary with `more: true` counted up to its bound, so the count reads "N+" for any N ("12+") and the bell's name says "12 or more unread"; without `more`, a count past 99 reads "99+". The panel sets its own text roles (since 0.4.0): its title (body size, weight 500, like the other menus' heads since 0.13.0) heads it whatever the product's base stylesheet, and an empty inbox is one quiet line. Since 0.13.0 (D79, D80) the count sits on the bell's glyph in the family's marker on the bar, ringed in the bar's color, with tabular figures, its right edge inside the bell's target so it never reaches the avatar; the panel shows one row per matter (items that share their product's `group` collapse into their latest, with "N updates" revealing the earlier ones, and `limit` counts matters, read from a page of `NotificationEntry.reach` items, 50 since 0.13.1, so a matter with many updates leaves room for the others); rows from today and from earlier days are set under "Today" and "Earlier" when there are both; with nothing unread "You are all caught up." stands above the rows; the head holds the title and "Mark all as read" (only while something is unread); the body scrolls between the head and the last row, "View all notifications", and fades at its lower edge while more is below. Opened from the keyboard, focus goes to the first row, and the arrow keys, Home and End move between the rows, their earlier updates and "View all". A quick answer replaces the panel's content directly; the loading indicator appears only after `NotificationEntry.delay` (250 ms; the fixture relay answers in about 80 ms) and goes with the answer; the body is `aria-busy` while loading, its height change is animated with `--motion-standard` except under reduced motion, and an answer that arrives after the panel closed is discarded. The panel states loading, empty (without "View all"), failure with retry, unavailable with "Try again" and no "View all" (for example a product running without Projects) and partial results naming the unreachable products by their display names from `products` (the id only when no name is given); it offers "Mark as read", "Mark all as read" (bounded by when the list loaded) and "View all". A row (shared with the inbox, since 0.13.0): the title in the text color (weight 500 while unread, with the dot centred on its first line and "(Unread)" for assistive technology), the producer's summary muted, then "{product} · {age}" in `Age`'s short words with the full moment on hover; the whole row opens the item (the title's button is stretched over it), and opening a matter marks each of its updates read (since 0.13.1); "Mark as read" or "Mark as unread" sits quietly at the end of that line, shown while the row is pointed at or holds focus and always where nothing hovers, a 24 px target on a pointer and the family target on touch. "View all" (a plain primary click) closes the panel and returns focus to the bell, then either follows `href` or, with `onview`, hands over to the product; a click with a modifier key opens elsewhere and leaves the panel. Opening an item asks the adapter, which rechecks access; an item that is gone is removed with a message. Item text is forgotten when the panel closes. Closing it from code (`close()`) returns focus to the bell when focus was inside the panel. Read-only `count`, `more` (the count stopped at the bound) and `missing` (product ids the last summary named as unreachable, which also sets `data-state="partial"`). `NotificationEntry.labels.en` / `.es` and `NotificationInbox.labels` (the same sets, 0.7.2) carry every string of the bell, the panel and the inbox in English and Spanish. Since 0.15.0 (X-07) opening a matter, or marking it read, reads all of it through the adapter's optional `readGroup({ product, group })` (and `unreadGroup`) (Beyond Projects' `POST /v1/notifications/read` with `{ product, group }`), also updates no page loaded; without it, or when it fails, the loaded updates are marked by their ids as before.

React `NotificationEntry` takes `onOpen`, `onView` and the same options as props. Its ref (`NotificationEntryHandle`) exposes `refresh()`, `open()`, `close()` and the read-only `count`, `more` and `expanded`, so a product closes the panel itself, for example on a route change.

**NotificationInbox** (`NotificationInbox`). The full inbox: unread or all, product filter, items grouped by their product's `group` with their earlier updates behind a toggle, set under "Today" and "Earlier" when the page holds both (since 0.13.0), with the entry's rows, "Load more" by cursor, mark read and unread, mark all read for the filter shown, open. States as the entry. `state` and `onstate` keep the filter in the address. Since 0.15.0 (X-07) it pages by matter: `list()` gets `by: 'matter'` and `limit` counts matters; from a relay that answers items (no `grouped: true`) it reads on, at most `Feed.pages` (5) requests, until it holds `limit` matters or the end, so one noisy matter never hides the others. A matter whose earlier updates no page loaded says how many it holds from the item's `updates: { count, unread, more? }`. Since 0.33.0 (D163) its relay's outage is the page form of `Recovery` (who did not answer, when it last tried, one Try again, its read in Technical details), with its filters hidden until the relay answers; `locale` picks its language.

**Adapter.** Both take `{ summary(), list({ state, product, cursor, limit }), read(ids | { before, product }), unread(ids), open(id) }`, shaped after `beyond-notifications/1` as the product's relay answers: `summary` → `{ unread, more?, sources?, unavailable?, available? }`; `list` → `{ items, next, sources?, unavailable?, available? }` with items `{ id, product, title, summary?, occurred, read, group? }`; `open` → `{ destination }` or a rejection with `code: 'NOT_FOUND'`. `available: false` means the aggregation is absent or unreachable. Beyond Projects answers `{ unread (0–99), more, sources: [{ product, state }] }`, where `unread` counts matters since 2026-10-09 (D80: unread items that share their product's `group` count once, as the panel shows them as one row): `more: true` means its count stopped at the scan bound, and a source with `state: 'unavailable'` is a product whose items are hidden because it did not answer (the partial signal, in a summary and in a list page alike). A product relay may instead name those products in `unavailable` (ids); both shapes are read, together if both are present. Marking read never performs a business action.

**Notification copy.** Both take `labels` (EN defaults; products pass ES or any language). Entries added or changed in 0.1.1: `button({ count, more })`, the bell's accessible name (`more` is true for a bounded count); `badge({ count, more })`, the visible count (default "N+" with `more`, "99+" past 99 without it); `partial({ products })`, where `products` is now the display names joined by ", ". Since 0.13.0: `today` "Today" / «Hoy» and `earlier` "Earlier" / «Anteriores», and `caught` ("You are all caught up." / «Estás al día.») is drawn by the panel too. Since 0.7.6 an unavailable aggregation says why when the relay's `{ available: false, reason }` names one of `beyond-notifications/1`'s codes: `cause` ("Notifications are unavailable right now because {reason}. Everything else works.") with the reason's own entry, `not_configured`, `not_platform_session`, `session_rejected`, `projects_unavailable` or `accounts_unavailable`, in English and Spanish. Any other reason, or none, keeps the plain `unavailable` sentence. Since 0.9.1 `session_rejected` speaks of access ("Beyond Projects could not confirm your access to them yet"): only `Session`'s dialog speaks of the session, and a product passes this reason only after `session.check()` answered `signed`.

## Pages

Since 0.6.0 (decision D52, settled contract S23, approved on 2026-10-04: full width for the frame and collections, readable widths for text and forms; LR-01 to LR-09 and LR-11 of the family's layout rules; D54 for the arrival line and the preferences dialog). Every signed-in page is the family bar, the product's navigation (the shared `Sidebar`, D53) and one **Page**: the region beside the navigation, edge to edge, whose content starts one gutter after it, never centered. Moving between products keeps the bar, the navigation's position, the gutter, the header, the width tiers, the section style and the control sizes (LR-11). The numeric values are engineering defaults, to be measured.

- **Page** (DOM `Page`, React `Page`). Place it in the shell's main element, after the `Sidebar` (or under `ProductNav`). Options: `template` (`overview`, `list`, `detail`, `settings`, `task`, `tool`; `data-template`, default `detail`; `tool` has full-bleed panes and no gutter, the recorded departure of Workspace's editor and Conduict's terminal), `width` (the main column's tier: `fluid` for collections and tools up to `--layout-fluid-max`, `standard` for a main column with its aside up to `--layout-standard`, `form` for inputs, settings and tasks at `--layout-form`, `reading` for prose at `--layout-measure`, and since 0.11.0 `thread` for a conversation's turns at `--layout-thread`, 80rem since token set 0.7.0 (D180; 52rem before); default `standard`), `arrival` (the `Arrival` line, first), `header` (the `PageHeader`), `children` (the main column), `aside` (a resource page's side panel; no panel without it), `label` (the panel's accessible name) and since 0.30.0 `raised` (the panel raised on a surface of its own, below). Setters `main`, `aside`, `raised` and `width` redraw; `region` is the main column's element.
- **The thread template and its dock** (since 0.14.0, D94, approved in S29). `template: 'thread'` is a conversation's page (its width is `thread` unless given): the frame fills the window under the chrome (`--bui-page-chrome`: the family bar, plus the sidebar's row below its cut; a host with other chrome, such as a window of Beyond Home's windowed host, sets it) and keeps no padding under its content, and the main column is a column whose last block is the **dock** (`dock`: a node, a component or a list; setter `dock`; React `dock`, React content): pushed to the column's end (`margin-top: auto`) and sticky at the window's bottom with one gutter and `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` under it, so its distance to the window's bottom is the same at the top, the middle and the end of the scroll and on a short or an empty thread (the acceptance measures it at 1440 and 390 px). A hairline above it is drawn only while content passes beneath it: an end mark after the dock (`bui-page-dock-end`) is watched by an `IntersectionObserver`, never a scroll handler, and the dock carries `data-stuck` meanwhile. `page.dock` is the `PageDock` (`element`, `stuck`), null in other templates; a dock given to another template throws. The header (and its compact line's content) ends at the thread's edge, so a toggle in it never moves when the panel shows or hides. Replace a product's own sticky dock (`position: sticky; bottom: 0` inside the frame, which stopped 48 px above the bottom at the end of the scroll and floated on a short thread) with this slot. Classes: `bui-page[data-template='thread']`, `bui-page-dock` (`data-stuck`), `bui-page-dock-end`.
- **A thread reads by turns** (since 0.29.0, D180, the owner's direction of 2026-10-24, amending D96; token set 0.7.0). In a thread the person's message, prose, the rail's items, activity rows and `LiveText` take the turn's width, `--layout-thread` (80rem); only the composer keeps a lane, `--layout-lane` (48rem), in the dock, standing under the middle of the turns in a centred thread (`lane: 'center'`). `.bui-lane` and `.bui-lane-wide` still give a product's own blocks those two widths (a product's own dock takes `.bui-lane`); a `RailItem`'s `wide` changes nothing in a thread, where every item takes the turn. From a 1024 px region a thread's gutter is 48 px (`--space-12`): it holds the index of its turns (`TurnIndex`, below), whether or not there are turns yet, so nothing moves when the second one arrives; the dock keeps one wide gutter (32 px) under it. A panel kept in view beside a thread comes next to the turns (one `--space-8` gap after them) and takes what they leave, between `--layout-aside` and `--layout-aside-max`; what is left beyond falls on the far side, never inside the thread. The wide form (`panel.wide`) is unchanged. Until 0.28.0 (D96, approved in S29) the message, prose, the rail's marks and the composer shared one lane, the 54ch reading measure after the rail's indent, and only code, diffs, tool output and approval cards widened to the 52rem thread.
- **The gutter** (`--layout-gutter`) is decided by the region's own width through a container query, never by the window's: 16 px under 640 px (`--layout-gutter`), 24 px up to 1023 px (`--layout-gutter-medium`), 32 px from 1024 px (`--layout-gutter-wide`). Products with the same navigation therefore start their content at the same place at every width (LR-01, LR-09). Unused width falls only on the far side.
- **The side panel** moves beside the main column once the region holds a form-width column and the panel (a 68rem region, about a 1,300 px window with the sidebar), at `--layout-aside`; below that it follows the main column (LR-03).
- **A resource page's raised panel** (`raised`, since 0.30.0; the family's resource page, the owner's direction of 2026-10-24: the person's work on the object leads the main column, its conditions stand beside it and its record is a tab). The aside is drawn on `--color-surface-raised` with a 1 px line in `--color-border`, `--radius-base` and `--space-5` inside, and no shadow (C2: elevation only for what overlaps). Its regions (each a `Section`) are divided by a line with `--space-5` on each side, and their headings take the body's size, still semibold and one step above the rows they name (D158). Its rows are the family's `ul.bui-rows`, which hang their padding into the panel's own, so a row's mark starts on the panel's edge (D159). A panel kept in view (`panel`) is never raised. The DOM page marks it `data-raised`; React takes `raised`.
- **Under a row's name** (since 0.30.0). What a list item holds after its row (its quiet actions, a closed fold, its technical details) takes `.bui-row-under`, which starts it where the row's name does and ends it one row padding before the item's end: `--bui-row-axis`, the row's padding, a state's mark and the gap (`calc(var(--space-3) + 9px + var(--space-3))`), measured from the item's edge. An item whose row leads with an icon, or with nothing, sets `--bui-row-axis` itself. The row stays the whole row's target; what is under it is the item's, never the row's.
- **A panel kept in view** (`panel`, since 0.10.0; the conversation's context panel, design 2.2 and 2.8). With `panel: { cut?, open?, onchange?, title?, labels? }` the aside is a `PagePanel` (`page.panel`): beside the main column from `cut` (the region's own width: CSS pixels or a `rem`/`px` length, `'68rem'` by default; Conduict uses `'73rem'`), `--layout-aside` wide, sticky under the family bar (`--bui-page-sticky` moves its top) with a scroll of its own; the person may hide it there (`close()`, `toggle()`), and `onchange(shown)` reports each choice for the product to keep on the device and give back as `open`. Below the cut it is out of the page's flow and `page.panel.open()` shows the same content in a `SideSheet` (titled `title`, else the panel's `label`; modal, focus in and back to the toggle that was pressed, else the panel's first toggle, also where a click does not focus a button, as in WebKit; `open(from)` and `toggle(from)` name another). The region's width is observed, and a change is decided in the next frame, never inside the observer's callback. A page made outside the document can only guess from the window's width: `mount()` decides as it places the page, and a page a product places itself (`append(page.element)`) in the task that made it is decided in the next frame, before it is first drawn; one placed later calls `page.panel.measure()` once placed. `page.panel.control(button)` makes a button the panel's toggle ("Details"): a press shows or hides it, or opens its sheet, and the button's `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` follow; it returns the release. Crossing the cut while the sheet is open closes it and shows the panel beside, focus included. `mode` reads `beside` or `sheet` (also `data-panel` on the page), `shown` the kept choice (setting it reports nothing), `expanded` whether it is in view now. Without `panel` the aside flows exactly as before. React: `<Page panel={{ cut, open, onChange, title, labels, head, wide }} panelRef={ref} aside={…}>` with `<PanelToggle label="Details" />` anywhere inside the page (such as the header's actions). Classes: `bui-page-panel`, `bui-page-slot`.
- **The panel's head, width and wide form** (since 0.11.0; Conduict's CB-07 and CB-Q4; D67 as amended by D96, approved on 2026-10-10 in S29, its two widths approved in token set 0.5.1). `panel.head: true` gives the panel a head of its own beside the main column: its `title` as an H2 and a hide control, the `close` glyph alone (D11's closed list) named "Hide {title}" («Ocultar {title}», `PagePanel.labels`, or `labels.hide`) with its tooltip; hiding moves focus to the panel's first toggle, else to the page's H1, and reports `onchange(false)` as `close()` does. In its sheet the sheet's own head and Close stand for it. `page.panel.title = …` renames the head. Beside the main column its width is fluid: from `--layout-aside` (22rem) up to `--layout-aside-max` (40rem since token set 0.4.1; 30rem before), taking what a capped main column leaves, so on a wide screen the extra width goes to the panel instead of a band between the thread and the panel (in the `thread` tier the turns keep their 80rem first, since 0.29.0, and the body stops at the thread, the gap and the panel's maximum; any width beyond falls on the far side, LR-02). `panel.wide` (or `page.panel.wide = true`, React `panel.wide`) is the wide form a product toggles, such as while the panel shows a diff: since 0.11.2 (Conduict's review M11) the panel takes every width the main column leaves, to the region's far edge, and the main column keeps its tier where the region holds it beside `--layout-aside-wide` (48rem), giving down to `--layout-aside` (22rem) first; at 2560 px beside a 250 px sidebar the thread keeps its tier and the diff takes the rest; at 1440 px the thread gives down to 22rem and the diff gets about 740 px. Content still starts one gutter from the navigation and is never centered (D52). `data-panel-wide` marks the page. (In 0.11.0 and 0.11.1 the wide form stopped at 48rem and the main column gave down to the form tier.) The panel is still decided before its first frame (0.10.0).
- **Reading width.** The package's own texts stop at `--layout-measure` (54ch since token set 0.3.1, measured so no line of prose passes 80 characters, LR-02; 68ch before): a callout's text, an empty state's body, a section's description, the header's facts, and since 0.7.8 every part that holds a product's sentences: a field's or a statement's hint, a choice's description and reason, `Unavailable`'s reason, the picker's status, a notice's summary, `Awaited`'s and a step's reason, technical details' text, a consequence, a question's message and the rest listed in `src/styles/prose.css`. `.bui-reading` gives a product's own paragraph the same measure and `.bui-form-width` the form tier. Since 0.6.2 every block of the main column, the side panel and a section's body may shrink below its content, so a wide table scrolls in its own box instead of widening the page.
- **PageHeader** (DOM `PageHeader`, React `PageHeader`). The page's one header (LR-05, D160): `crumbs` (`[{ label, href? }]`, only from the second level down: where the object in view lives, each level the person recognizes and can open, in the destination's own hierarchy), `title` (the H1, naming what is in view as its list names it: never the organization when a project or one of its resources is in view), `kind` (since 0.19.1: the object's kind or variant beside the H1, words drawn as a quiet tag such as "Checkout", or one control that chooses among its siblings; never part of the H1's name), `status` (one, right after the H1, D42: the H1 object's own state, never another object's), `facts` (one muted line of that object's properties: never another object's state or price), `actions` (the title line's end: one primary button, then an `ActionMenu`; centred on the H1's line), `tabs` (a `Tabs` below). Under a 640 px region the status and the actions follow the title. Since 0.19.1 a resource's page (template `detail`) keeps its actions within reach of its title: the title's line spans at most a form column and its side panel (64rem, LR-03) however wide the region, while the tabs and the facts keep their width. The H1 has `tabindex="-1"`: `focus()` (React `headingRef`) moves focus to it after a navigation. Setters redraw; empty parts take no room. Never in it: preferences, product switching, organization or project selectors (D46), return buttons. Since 0.6.1 its tabs scroll inside their row and never widen the page at 320 px. Since 0.6.4 the `<header>` takes no accessible name: inside `<main>` it has the generic role, which takes none (ARIA 1.2), and the H1 names the page; place the `Page` inside the shell's `<main>`, never beside it, or the header becomes a second banner. Label: `crumbs` "Breadcrumb". `PageHeader.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.2): the crumbs' name, «Ruta de navegación». Since 0.7.7 a crumb's link is a 24 px target (WCAG 2.5.8) on a line of unchanged height: its block padding is given back by an equal negative margin; since 0.7.9 it is about 26 px, so no rounding takes it under 24.
- **A compact header** (`compact`, since 0.11.0; Conduict's header on long threads). `compact: true` keeps the title and the one status in view on a long page: once the title's line has scrolled out under the family bar (and under the sidebar's product row below its cut), one sticky line, `--bui-page-compact` (3rem) tall, shows the title on one line (cut only while a tooltip shows it whole on hover, D44) and the status, and `compact: { actions: [nodes] }` adds the product's own actions at its end (React `compact={{ actions: <…/> }}`; the product's own controls, such as a second Create pull request, not the header's). The line's holder has no height and sits in the page's frame right before the header (`header.bar`: `Page` places it; a product that places the header itself places `bar` before it), so showing it shifts nothing; it fades in with `--motion-quick` and without motion under reduced motion; a panel kept in view sits under it. Its title and status are hidden from assistive technology, since the header says them. It is measured on scroll and resize at most once per animation frame (`header.compacted`, `header.measure()`); `destroy()` releases its listeners. Classes: `bui-page-compact` (`data-shown`), `bui-page-compact-line`, `bui-page-compact-title`, `bui-page-compact-status`, `bui-page-compact-actions`. Since 0.11.2 what the page scrolls to (an address's target or `?turn=`, `scrollIntoView`, a heading focused by code) lands below every sticky line: while a page has a compact line the document's `scroll-padding-top` is the family bar, the sidebar's row below its cut, the compact line and `--space-2` (Conduict's review, minor 2; the family proposal "an address that targets a part of a page lands it below every sticky line"). `--bui-page-compact` is also defined on `:root` for it. Since 0.29.0 (D181) the line says where the person is: `header.subject = { text, href }` (React `subject`) names the part of the page in view in place of the title, such as the message of a thread's turn being read once it has gone under the line (`TurnIndex`'s `onview`, below), as written on up to two lines (`white-space: pre-line`, clamped; the empty lines between paragraphs left out), quoted with a 2 px edge; `null` gives the title back. It is a link to that part (`href`) out of the tab order and, like the title, hidden from assistive technology, since the page says it in place; `data-subject` marks the line's holder. While a thread has a compact line, or a line says a subject, what the page scrolls to lands below its taller form (`--bui-page-compact-tall`, 3.5rem), so a jump to a turn lands at one place on every turn. Class: `bui-page-compact-subject`.
- **Tabs** (DOM `Tabs`, React `Tabs`). A resource's areas as links in the address with `aria-current="page"`, drawn like `ProductNav` and scrolling sideways with the current one in view. Options: `items`, `label` (default "Sections of this page"), `name` (0.7.6: what the tabs belong to, giving "Sections of {name}" when no `label` is given), `onnavigate`. `Tabs.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.2): «Secciones de esta página»; `named` (0.7.6): «Secciones de {name}». Since 0.19.1 a page's tabs start on the content edge: the first label lines up with the H1 and the current tab is marked under its words only, while the row's line keeps the edge (the alignment audit's P5). Since 0.15.0 (X-15, PKG-19) an end that holds tabs it does not show says so with a fade and a chevron that scrolls the row by most of its width (pointer only: each tab is reached with Tab); `more` and `data-more` say which ends (`start`, `end`). `ProductNav` does the same.
- **Section** (DOM `Section`, React `Section`). A flat section (LR-06, D10): `title` (an H2, or H3 with `level: 3`), one `description` line at the reading width, `actions` at the end of the heading's line and its content. Since 0.19.0 (D158) its title is semibold and takes its level's size: title-2 at level 2, title-3 at level 3, so a section inside a side sheet or a panel is one step below the title it sits under. Sections are set apart by space and a 1 px divider; never put a bordered box inside another. Since 0.24.0 (D163) `unavailable` (option, method, React prop) says in place that the section's read did not answer, with no action of its own, and `updated` puts `LastUpdate` above content kept from an earlier read.
- **ListDetail** (DOM `ListDetail`, React `ListDetail`; since 0.7.0, LR-03, FAM-40). List and detail: the open item (`detail`) sits beside the `list` once the region is `ListDetail.cut` (72rem) wide, decided by the region's own width, and takes the region's whole width, as its own page, on a narrower one, starting with its way back (`back: { label, href, onnavigate? }`, a link to the list's own address; not drawn while the list is in view). Without a detail the list takes the region. The product keeps the open item in its address (`&repository=rep_…`) and sets `detail` from it, so a reload and the history show the same; `focus()` moves focus to the detail. React renders the same markup.
- **Arrival** (DOM `Arrival`, React `Arrival`; D54, which merges D05 and FR-1). The first line of a page opened from another product: "Opened from {product} · Back to {product}" («Abierto desde {product} · Volver a {product}», `Arrival.labels.en` / `.es`). `href` reopens the exact place the person came from, built from the product's own configuration; it is also the way to cancel a task, and on a task's success the product returns there by itself. `onnavigate(item, event)` takes over a plain same-origin click; `ondismiss` offers Dismiss (an icon button with its tooltip). It is the only way back a page draws: never a button among its controls, never in the family bar. Since 0.7.7 "Back to {product}" is a 24 px target on a line of unchanged height, as a crumb is.

```js
const page = new Page({ template: 'detail', width: 'standard', arrival: from ? new Arrival({ product: 'Conduict', href: back }) : null, header, children: [new Section({ title: 'Repositories', children: [list] }).element], aside: [facts] }).mount(main);
```

## Resource panels

Since 0.11.0 (Conduict's conversation benchmark, CB-01, CB-04, CB-05, B-06: a panel of label and value rows with its numbers and states, and uses against a limit said honestly). Both are flat: no card and no elevation (D10, D52, D53); put them in a `PagePanel`, a `Section` or a side sheet.

**Facts** (DOM `Facts`, React `Facts`). Since 0.14.0 (D99, approved in S29) the value starts in a fixed column right after its label's, `--bui-facts-label` (9.5rem), not at the row's far end, so a label and its value read together; only a number aligns to the end, in tabular figures: a `number` value, or a row's `numeric: true` (`numeric: false` keeps a number at the column; `Facts.numeric(row)` says which; `data-numeric`). A row's action keeps the row's end; since 0.15.0 (PKG-19) a long value stays in the value column and wraps there, going under its label only in a facts container under 30rem, and under 16rem every value does. Label and value rows for a resource's panel, a description list (`dl` of rows, each a `dt` and its `dd`). Options: `head` (`{ title?, value?, state?, level? }`: the title as a heading of `level`, 3 by default, which names the rows; one summary value such as "3 files · +52 −3"; one state in words with its tone, `[label, tone]` or `{ label, tone }`, a `status` at the head's end; a state without words is none), `rows` (`[{ key?, label, value, mono?, action?, stale? }]`), `label` (the rows' accessible name when the head has no title) and `labels`. A row's value sits at its end; `mono` sets it in the monospaced face for identifiers, commands, paths and models; `action` is the row's own control (a node or a component, such as a quiet Copy button) after the value, at the row's end on the value's first line, its words on the value's baseline beside the label and under it (since 0.11.1: a long value wraps beside the action instead of pushing it to a line of its own, and its target grows around its words); `stale` marks a value that is not current: muted, followed by its note (`true` says "Not current" / «No actualizado», a string is the product's note, "Not reported since 23:10"), read with the value. When the container is narrower than 16rem (a container query, never the window) each value wraps under its label. Setters `head` and `rows`; rows are patched by `key` (else their label): a row that stays keeps its element, and an action already in place is never moved, so a focused Copy keeps its focus through a live change. Classes: `bui-facts`, `bui-facts-head`, `bui-facts-title`, `bui-facts-summary`, `bui-facts-state`, `bui-facts-list`, `bui-facts-row` (`data-stale`), `bui-facts-label`, `bui-facts-data`, `bui-facts-value` (`data-mono`), `bui-facts-note`, `bui-facts-action`. React renders the same markup, with `action` as React content. Since 0.11.2 a long value in words goes under its label, left-aligned, read as a sentence (Conduict's review M10): a row's `long: true`, or by default a string value longer than 40 characters (`FactsRow.measure`) that is not `mono`; `long: false` keeps it beside; `Facts.long(row)` says which; the row carries `data-long`. A value that wraps reads from its start (`text-align: start`), never ragged at its left; an identifier keeps its place beside its Copy.

**Meter** (DOM `Meter`, React `Meter`). A use against a limit, such as an AI engine's usage window, a budget or a quota: `label`, `value` (the share used, 0 to 1; more is past the limit; null is "Not reported"), `reset` (when it resets: the product's words, or a moment, said "Resets {time}" on the page's `Clock`), `stale` (not current: a moment, the product's time words, or `true`; "Not reported since {time}", "Not reported lately") and `thresholds` (`{ warning, danger }`, 0.8 and 0.95 by default; outside 0 to 1, or a warning past the danger, throws a `RangeError`). Its level is said in words past the thresholds, never by color alone: "Near the limit" (`warning`), "Almost at the limit" (`danger`), "Limit reached" (`full`, at 1 or more), none below. A `reset` moment that has passed is said by itself, "Reset since {time} · use not reported since", and makes the meter stale: the use after a reset is unknown until reported again (CB-04); while a reset is ahead it listens to the clock and releases it after, and on `destroy()`. Stale, the track and the value are muted. The track is `role="meter"`, named by the label, with `aria-valuemin` 0, `aria-valuemax` 100, `aria-valuenow` in whole percent and an `aria-valuetext` that says the use, the level and the note ("86% used · Near the limit · Resets 23:10"); the fill moves with `--motion-standard`, not at all under reduced motion. `update(values)` changes what is given; `level` (`ok`, `warning`, `danger`, `full`, `unknown`), `stale` and `track` read it; `clock` and `locale` (the percent and times) are options. Copy (`Meter.labels.en` / `.es`): `used` "{percent} used", `warning`, `danger`, `full`, `unknown` "Not reported", `reset` "Resets {time}", `stale` "Not reported since {time}", `unreported`, `passed`. Classes: `bui-meter` (`data-level`, `data-stale`), `bui-meter-head`, `bui-meter-label`, `bui-meter-value`, `bui-meter-word`, `bui-meter-track`, `bui-meter-fill` (`--bui-meter`), `bui-meter-note`. React `<Meter label value reset stale thresholds clock locale labels />` drives the DOM class.

```js
import { Facts, Meter, Button } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const changes = new Facts({ head: { title: 'Changes', value: '3 files · +52 −3', state: ['Not pushed', 'warning'] }, rows: [{ key: 'branch', label: 'Branch', value: branch, mono: true, action: new Button({ label: 'Copy', variant: 'quiet', onclick: copy }) }, { key: 'uncommitted', label: 'Not committed', value: '1 file' }] });
const usage = new Meter({ label: '5-hour window', value: 0.86, reset: limits.resets });
usage.update({ stale: limits.reported }); // a report that is not current
```

## Conversations

Since 0.10.0 (the conversation experience's component plan: a minimal message box, text that streams, activity normalized across agents). The pieces are generic: the product brings its words, its kinds of work and its renderer. Conduict adopts them first (its conversation, new conversation and Desktop window); a conversation's `Thread` (the ordered list, the sticky dock, follow-the-end) stays a product composition for now.

**The composer's missing step** (since 0.16.0, DQ-03). `step: { label, run }` (setter `step`; React `step: { label, onSelect }`) names what a message needs first on the primary action itself ("Choose a machine and send") instead of a disabled Send and a sentence in the toolbar: pressing it, or Enter, runs `run({ text })` (the product opens its chooser, creates what is missing); anything but `false` then sends the message as usual, `false` or a throw keeps the text; an empty field runs the step without sending; one step at a time, the action busy meanwhile. `step = null` restores Send.

**Composer** (DOM `Composer`, React `Composer`). A message box, the same wherever a person writes to an agent: one rounded box whose first band is an optional **state line** (`status`: one sentence, text or a node with its action, only when it changes what sending does; it describes the field), a **field** of one line when empty that grows with its text to twelve lines and then scrolls (`min` 1, `max` 12; immediate, never animated), named by `label` with no visible label and a `placeholder` that says what sending will do, and a **toolbar**: the **+** (since 0.26.0, below), the turn's `settings` and the product's `tools` at its start (context choices on a new conversation, such as `ChoiceMenu`s and a `RefChooser` with `layout: 'inline'`), then its `extras`, the reason sending is unavailable, Dictate, the `stop` action and **Send**. Options: `label`, `onsubmit({ text, action })` (required; returns a promise), `placeholder`, `status`, `tools`, `extras`, `actions` (`[{ id, label, primary?, words? }]`: the first marked primary, else the first, is Send, its words its name and tooltip; `words: true` draws them for an action that is not a send, "Check whether it started"; the others are a split menu, the `chevron` alone named "More ways to send" with its tooltip; "Send" by default), `stop` (`{ label, run, busy? }`, the `stop` glyph alone named by its label, busy while `run` works or while `busy` says so; null removes it, and focus on it then goes to the field), `add` (the + menu's entries, below), `live` (below), `disabled` (`{ reason, place? }`: the primary action stays reachable with `aria-disabled` and the reason said beside it in words, D44, also describing the field; the menu's items are disabled with it; since 0.34.0, D183, a press on the held Send puts the person at `place()`, such as the chooser of what sending waits for, sending nothing and saying nothing more than the reason beside it, while Enter in the field still never sends and leaves focus there; without `place` a press does nothing, as before; the split menu's other ways to send are quiet with it, a press closing the menu and going to `place()` too, and the reason is said once, beside Send, never under each, D101), `busy` (the product's own work), `submit` (`'enter'`, the default, or `'mod'`), `value`, `name`, `onchange(text)`, `explain(error)` and `labels`.

- **Keys** (the default `submit: 'enter'` is the conversation design's proposal Q-C, which awaits the owner's decision as the family's rule): on a hardware keyboard Enter sends and Shift+Enter adds a line; ⌘+Enter (Ctrl+Enter elsewhere) also sends; on a touch screen (no hover and a coarse pointer) Enter adds a line and the button sends; with `'mod'` Enter always adds a line. While an input method composes a character, Enter never sends (`isComposing`, and Safari's key code 229 for the Enter that ends a composition). Escape is left to menus: it never clears, sends or interrupts. A visually hidden hint with the field says the keys ("Enter sends. Shift+Enter adds a line.").
- **Sending** is one path for the keys, the button and the menu: the text, trimmed, leaves the field at once (`onchange('')`) and `onsubmit({ text, action })` runs with the action's id; one send at a time, and none while `busy` or `disabled` (`sending` reads it; the button shows "Send…" with a spinner). When the promise rejects, the text comes back (before anything typed meanwhile) and the box's state band says "Not sent. Your message is still here." on a row of its own above the band's sentence (`role="alert"`, the `alert` glyph, the danger hue; since 0.29.0, a line between the field and the toolbar before), or `explain(error)`'s words, or nothing when `explain` answers null (another voice speaks, such as `Session`'s dialog). Nothing to send is no failure (since 0.29.0, D183): Enter does nothing, Send is quiet and `aria-disabled`, the split menu's ways to send are disabled with it, and a press only puts the caret in the field, saying nothing; a `step`, which acts without a message, keeps Send able (before 0.29.0 a press said "Write a message first." in the danger hue). Focus stays in the field after sending.
- **Setters and methods**: `value` (without `onchange`), `placeholder`, `status`, `tools`, `extras`, `actions`, `stop`, `disabled`, `busy`, `add`, `live` (0.26.0); `submit(action?)` sends as a press does and resolves whether it was sent; `focus()`; `fit()` fits the field's height to its text (`mount` does it; a product that places the element itself calls it); `field` is the textarea.
- **One geometry** (since 0.26.0, D165, the owner's direction of 2026-10-23). Every control of the toolbar is one target tall (`--bui-target`, 44 px on touch screens) with one quiet edge (`--color-border`, `--color-border-strong` and a lift on hover or while open): a glyph alone is a round target (the **+**, Dictate, Interrupt, Options), a chip is a pill showing its value (its label, a ready state and a `calm` option's state are its name and its tooltip; any other state keeps its word, "Asleep", "Not connected"), and **Send** is the one filled control, the `up` glyph in a circle, named by its action ("Send", "Wake up and send", "Queue") with its tooltip. Send draws its words, as a pill ending with the glyph, only when they must be read before sending: a `step` ("Choose a machine and send") or an action with `words`. With nothing to send (no words, no ready file) it reads quiet, in the family's disabled surface, `aria-disabled`, and since 0.29.0 a press says nothing (`data-empty` on the composer, D183). The **+** opens the platform's file chooser at once (**Attach files**) or, with the product's `add` entries (`[{ label, run, keys? }]`, React `{ label, onSelect, keys? }`), a menu named **Add** with Attach files first (where files are taken) and each entry with the key that does the same in the field at its end ("Mention a file @", "Commands /"); with neither it is not drawn, and it never folds. `live: true` (setter `live`) says the product's work runs (the engine answering): the box's top edge is a line in the hue of work in progress (`--color-info`, never the primary's coral, C2), a light passing along it twice and then holding, as a live status dot pulses about five seconds (D95); nothing moves under reduced motion (`data-live`). Under the state line the field takes less room above it, and in a composer under 30rem the line's sentence sits closer to its edges, so a dock with a two-line state line stays within a quarter of a 320 × 640 screen. Focus draws the box's border in the focus color with a soft glow inside it, never past its edge (CV-16). **One inset** (since 0.29.0, D184): the box's parts keep `--bui-composer-inset` on every side, 12 px, and 8 px in a composer under 30rem so a phone's chips keep their room: the field's first line stands with its capitals one inset under the box's edge (`padding-block-start: calc(inset − (1lh − 1cap) / 2)`, measured from the font itself; 8 px where `lh` and `cap` are unknown), the toolbar's controls one inset above its lower edge and from its sides, the + on the text's edge, and the attachments and the tray on the same inset. Classes: `bui-composer-add`, `bui-composer-plus`, `bui-composer-attach`, `bui-composer-entry`, `bui-composer-keys`, `bui-composer-go` (`data-words`, `data-step`), `bui-button-bare`; stylesheet `composing.css`.
- **Layout**: the box takes `--color-border-control` and a radius of 2.5 × `--radius-base`; its focus is its own border. The toolbar keeps one row (since 0.11.2, below); with `compact: false` a composer under 30rem gives the toolbar's start a row of its own (at 320 px the choices, then the actions); every button takes `--bui-control`, 44 px on touch screens. With an empty field and no state line it is at most 96 px tall, its toolbar included (the acceptance measures it at 1440, 1024, 390 and 320 px in both themes). The placeholder stays the textarea's own, read by assistive technology, and is drawn by a line laid over the empty field (`aria-hidden`), so a long one ends with an ellipsis on one line in every engine; an empty field keeps its first line whatever its placeholder (Firefox would otherwise count a wrapped placeholder in its height). Classes: `bui-composer` (`data-submit`, `data-busy`, `data-disabled`, `data-empty`, `data-live`), `bui-composer-box`, `bui-composer-status`, `bui-composer-input`, `bui-composer-field` (`--bui-composer-min`, `--bui-composer-max`), `bui-composer-placeholder`, `bui-composer-problem`, `bui-composer-bar`, `bui-composer-tools`, `bui-composer-end`, `bui-composer-extras`, `bui-composer-reason`, `bui-composer-stop`, `bui-composer-send`.
- **Copy** (`Composer.labels.en` / `.es`): `send` "Send" / «Enviar», `more` "More ways to send" / «Más formas de enviar», `busy` "{label}…", `enter` "Enter sends. Shift+Enter adds a line." / «Intro envía. Mayús+Intro añade una línea.», `mod` "{key}+Enter sends. Enter adds a line." / «{key}+Intro envía. Intro añade una línea.», `touch` "Enter adds a line. Use {action} to send." / «Intro añade una línea. Usa «{action}» para enviar.», `failed` "Not sent. Your message is still here." / «No se envió. Tu mensaje sigue aquí.». `empty` ("Write a message first.") left the set in 0.29.0 (D183).
- **React**: `<Composer label onSubmit placeholder status tools extras actions stop={{ label, onSelect, busy }} disabled busy submit value onChange explain min max name labels ref />`; `status` (when not text), `tools` and `extras` are React content; callbacks are read from the latest props; `value` is applied when it changes; the ref has `focus()`, `submit(action?)`, `value`, `busy` and `sending`. A change of `label`, `submit`, `min`, `max`, `name` or `labels` (memoize it) creates a new box.
- **The state line, the box's first band** (since 0.11.0, CB-14; a band of the box since 0.26.0, D165, a line of its own above it before). The state line shares the box's edges, its words starting where the field's do, on a quiet band divided from the field by a hairline, so it never reads as the field's content and never floats beside it; it still describes the field. `status` may be `{ text, action }`: the action at the line's end is a node, a component, or `{ label, run }` drawn as a quiet button (an action that stays keeps its element and focus while the text changes). Classes: `bui-composer-line`, `bui-composer-status`, `bui-composer-status-action`.
- **Settings** (since 0.11.0; CB-15). `settings` (setter `settings`) are nodes at the toolbar's start, before the `tools`: the turn's choices, such as `ChoiceChip`s for the model and effort and the autonomy level, each showing its value since 0.26.0 with its whole face as its tooltip. The +, the settings and the tools share the toolbar's start (`bui-composer-start`), which wraps, and takes a row of its own in a composer under 30rem (with `compact: false`; by default they fold behind Options there, below). Class `bui-composer-settings`.
- **One toolbar row** (since 0.11.2; Conduict's review M3, the family proposal "a composer keeps one toolbar row"; the default, `compact: true`). The toolbar keeps one row by measured fit, never by a fixed width (`ComposerToolbar`, `ComposerFit`): it is measured when the composer's width changes (a zero-height gauge across it, so a change of level never resizes what is observed), when the toolbar's content changes and when fonts load, and tries in order: `full`, where each chip shows its value with any state that is not ready or calm (since 0.26.0 a chip's label is its name and tooltip at every level); `short`, where any state but one that asks for attention leaves the face too and a value past 9rem ends in an ellipsis, while the tooltip, which shows the chip's whole face ("Model: GPT-6.1 Sol · Medium · Default") on hover and keyboard focus, and the open menu say it whole (D44); and last `fold`, behind **Options** (below), which leaves out its summary when even that row does not fit. The actions keep their words where a step names them; a chip never wraps inside itself. `composer.toolbar.level` reads the level (`full`, `short`, `fold`; the root's `data-fit`, absent for `full`) and `composer.toolbar.measure()` decides it now; while Options is open or focused the level stays, so nothing focused is hidden. Since 0.32.0 a change of the window's size, and fonts that finish loading, are decided in the next animation frame. The frame a resize lands runs the window's resize listeners first, where the Sidebar and a page's panel follow the new width, then that decision, then its layout: no frame is drawn at the level of the width before. A width that changes without the window's is decided in the frame it lands too when the part that changed it says so (`Relayout`): a page's panel shown or hidden beside, its wide form and the Sidebar's panel do. **LiveText** (DOM `LiveText`, React `LiveText`). Text that arrives in pieces, such as an agent's answer while it is written. `append(piece)` adds to the end and `set(text)` replaces the whole draft (a snapshot after a reconnect); either draws at most once per animation frame, through the product's `render(text)` (a Node or a string: a Markdown renderer that builds text nodes) or as plain text with its line breaks; a hidden tab draws nothing until it is shown again, then the latest text once. It keeps at most `bound` characters (64 000; past it the pieces are dropped and `truncated` is true). While live it is `aria-busy` and ends with a **live mark**: a word for assistive technology ("Writing…" / «Escribiendo…», read in place, never announced) and a small dot that pulses, still under reduced motion, placed at the end of the last paragraph or list item. It has no live region: the page's one region says that work started or ended. `settle(text?)` draws the final text at once without the mark; `abandon(note?)` stops with the text so far, muted, and a note ("Unfinished · not kept" / «Sin terminar · no se guardó», `labels.abandoned`); a completed text that arrives later still settles an abandoned one. Pieces after either are ignored. `text`, `state` (`live`, `settled`, `abandoned`), `truncated`. Paragraphs, lists and headings keep the reading measure; code and tables in the product's rendering may use the column. Classes: `bui-live` (`data-state`), `bui-live-body` (`data-plain` for plain text), `bui-live-mark`, `bui-live-note`. React: `<LiveText text state note render bound labels ref />`, where `text` grows by appending (any other text replaces it), `state` settles or abandons, `render(text)` returns React content committed at most once per frame, and the ref has `append`, `set`, `settle` and `abandon` for a product that streams without rendering per piece.

With the times (`since`, `until`, or `duration` in milliseconds) it says how long it took, or, running, how long it has run on the page's `Clock` (never announced; under a second says nothing). Without a body the row is plain text. The head is a button with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` (Enter and Space toggle it); closing with focus inside returns it to the head. `update(values)` changes only what is given (`glyph`, `title`, `meta`, `state`, `since`, `until`, `duration`, `tail`, `body`): a live change keeps the row open or closed and keeps focus; a new `body` is built again at once when open, at the next opening otherwise. `open()`, `close()`, `toggle()`, `expanded`, `state`, `body` (the body's element), `watch(listener)` (hears every update; returns the release). Classes: `bui-activity` (`data-state`), `bui-activity-head`, `bui-activity-mark`, `bui-activity-text`, `bui-activity-title`, `bui-activity-meta`, `bui-activity-time`, `bui-activity-word`, `bui-activity-chevron`, `bui-activity-tail`, `bui-activity-body`, `bui-activity-whole`, `bui-activity-content`, `bui-activity-section`, `bui-activity-label`, `bui-activity-code`, `bui-activity-note`, `bui-activity-actions`, `bui-activity-result`.

**ActivityGroup** (DOM `ActivityGroup`, React `ActivityGroup`). Consecutive rows folded into one ("Read 3 files"): a disclosure row with its `glyph`, its `title` (text, or a function of the number of rows so the product writes its own plural) and `meta`, showing the state that matters most among its rows (running, then failed, denied, waiting, done); opened, it lists the rows, each still a row of its own. `rows` (set: rows that stay keep their place and focus, rows that leave are destroyed), `add(row)`, `remove(row)` (taken out, not destroyed), `update({ glyph, title, meta })`, `open()`, `close()`, `toggle()`, `state`, `expanded`; destroying the group destroys the rows it holds. Which rows fold together is the product's choice. Class `bui-activity-group`, its list `bui-activity-list` of `bui-activity-item`s.

**Rail** (DOM `Rail`, `RailItem`, `RailMoment`; React `Rail`, `RailItem`, `RailMoment`; since 0.12.0). One line down a column of marks, so a feed's events read as one sequence from what started them to their end, such as a coding agent's turn. `new Rail({ element?, label?, children? })` draws the line on a new container or on the product's own (`element`), whose children the product keeps placing; with `label` it is a named group. `ActivityRow` and `ActivityGroup` placed in it put their own mark on the line, unchanged. Any other event is a **`RailItem`** `({ glyph?, tone?, content? })`: a mark (the kind's glyph from the one catalog, a dot without one, a spinner while `progress`, still under reduced motion) and the product's content beside it (a paragraph, "Thought for 42 s", a failure said in one line); `update()` patches the mark and the content and keeps focus inside it; `body` is the content's element. **`RailMoment`** `({ text, datetime?, title? })` is a time of day at the line's far end, before an event that came well after the one above it (the product decides when; Conduict where two minutes or more passed). Marks are colored by their state beside the product's words, never alone (D42): a row's `failed` in the danger color, `waiting` and `denied` in the warning color and `running` in the info color (since 0.12.0 for every activity row, on a rail or not); a `RailItem`'s `tone` (`neutral`, `success`, `info`, `progress`, `warning`, `danger`; `Rail.tone()` reads anything else as `neutral`). The line is decoration (`aria-hidden` marks, a `::before` line); the marks take the rail's surface (`--bui-rail-surface`, the canvas unless the product's container sets it) so the line passes behind them. The rail carries no copy of its own.

Activity copy (`ActivityRow.labels.en` / `.es`, shared by `ActivityGroup.labels`): `running` "Running" / «En curso», `done` "Done" / «Hecho», `failed` "Failed" / «Falló», `denied` "Denied" / «Denegado», `waiting` "Waiting" / «En espera», `elapsed` and `took` "{duration}", the units `second` … `day` ("{count} s"), `more` "Show all {count} lines" / «Mostrar las {count} líneas», `less` "Show fewer lines" / «Mostrar menos líneas», `copy` "Copy" / «Copiar», `copied` "Copied" / «Copiado», `refused` "Could not copy. The text is selected: copy it with your keyboard." / «No se pudo copiar. El texto está seleccionado: cópialo con el teclado.».

React: `<Rail label>` holds React children on one line, `<RailItem glyph tone>` (driven by the DOM class, its children placed through a portal) and `<RailMoment text datetime title />` (since 0.12.0). `<ActivityRow glyph title meta state since until duration tail body open clock locale labels />`, where `title` and `meta` are text and `body` is a function returning React content (called once the row is first opened) or section records; values are applied when they change and `open` opens or closes it. `<ActivityGroup glyph title meta rows open clock locale labels />`, where `rows` are row values (`key`, `glyph`, `title`, `meta`, `state`, the times, `tail`, and `body` as section records), patched by key.

```js
import { Composer, LiveText, ActivityRow, ActivityGroup } from '@beyond-js/ui';
```

**Lanes** (DOM `Lanes`, React `Lanes`; since 0.27.0, the multi-agent round of 2026-10-23). Concurrent work as lanes on one time axis: a coding agent's subagents first (Conduict's crew block), and any parallel work a product follows (a preparation's parallel steps, a delivery's jobs). `new Lanes({ label, clock, locale, labels, trace, choose })` draws a group named by `label` (without one, `labels.group`, "Work in parallel") holding one button per lane, in the product's order, never sorted. `update({ lanes, label?, trace? })` patches lanes by `key`: a lane that stays keeps its button and focus, a new one takes its place in the order, one no longer given is removed (when it held focus, the lane now in its place takes it). `focus(key, options?)` moves focus to a lane and gives it the tab order; `keys` lists them in order. A lane is `{ key, depth = 0, state, stateLabel, title, kind = null, now = null, facts = [], since = null, until = null, current = false }`.

- **Columns.** The list is one grid and each lane a subgrid row of it, so the columns align across lanes by their content with nothing measured in script: the mark and the title, indented one step per `depth` (0 to 3) under a tree connector (├ while a sibling follows, └ for the last, │ where a shallower level continues); the kind (muted); the live line `now` (muted, changed in place); the time (tabular, end-aligned, at least 5rem wide, so its ticking never moves a column); the facts (tabular, end-aligned; a fact is a string or `{ text, label }`, the label read and not shown: "48k" read "48k tokens"); the trace. The kind, facts and trace columns exist only when some lane has them.
- **States** (`Lanes.states`): `running`, `waiting`, `done`, `failed`, `stopped` and `unknown` (anything else reads `unknown`), each with a `Status` mark's shape and hue (a half-filled circle, a diamond, a filled circle, a square, a hollow circle, a dashed circle) that never pulses (LOAD-01). The product's word (`stateLabel`, "Waiting for you") is read before the title and not shown; without one, `labels` says Running, Waiting, Done, Failed, Stopped or Unknown.
- **Time.** A lane is live while `running` or `waiting` with `since` and no `until`: its time counts on `clock` (`Clock.system` by default) in the long-operation components' words ("1 min 42 s"); an ended one says how long it ran, and one whose end is not known says nothing. A tick writes only the live lanes' times and bars, and the axis' span when it reaches its next step; the clock is heard only while a lane is live.
- **Trace** (`trace`, true by default). One axis for the block, from the earliest `since`: while a lane is live it ends at the first step strictly above the time run (`Lanes.steps`: 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 30, 60 and 120 minutes, then doubling), so a live bar grows at its end and the scale changes rarely, and an end reported past the page's own now (a server's clock ahead) still fits; once all ended it ends at the last `until`. A bar is 4 px, rounded, hued by state on a faint track the width of the axis, placed by custom properties (`--bui-lanes-from` and `--bui-lanes-to` in milliseconds against the block's `--bui-lanes-span`) and hidden from assistive technology; a lane whose end is not known marks only its start. The trace's tooltip (the package's `Tooltip`, placed by `Floating`, made on the first hover) says "Started 10:42:05 · ran 1 min 42 s", or "Started 10:42:05 · 1 min 42 s so far" kept current while shown; the start is also the lane's description (`aria-describedby`), so a screen reader hears state, title, kind, live line, time, facts and start.
- **Geometry.** One line a lane from 40rem of the block's own width and two below it (the title and the time, then the live line under the title's words and the facts; no kind and no trace), by a container query: the block is a size container (`container: bui-lanes / inline-size`) and takes its container's width. No change of words, times or facts changes a lane's height. A cut title (or kind) shows whole in a tooltip on hover and keyboard focus (D44). Nothing in it scrolls the page, moves focus by itself or animates; the rows hang their padding into the gutter, so the marks keep the content's edge (P5).
- **Keyboard and choosing.** One lane in the tab order: the focused one, else the `current` one, else the last focused, else the first. ArrowDown and ArrowUp move, Home and End jump, without wrapping; Enter and Space, like a press, call `choose(key, event)`. The product decides what choosing opens (Conduict's subagent sheet) and marks that lane `current`: `aria-current="true"`, the selection's surface and a coral edge (C2). Hover and focus are the package's rows'.
- **Copy** (`Lanes.labels.en` / `.es`): the units `second` to `day`; `started`, a function of `{ time, day }` ("Started {time}" / «Empezó a las {time}», and «Empezó el {time}» on another day); `ran` "ran {duration}" / «duró {duration}»; `elapsed` "{duration} so far" / «{duration} hasta ahora»; the six states (Running / «En curso», Waiting / «En espera», Done / «Hecho», Failed / «Falló», Stopped / «Detenido», Unknown / «Desconocido»); `group` "Work in parallel" / «Trabajo en paralelo».
- **React**: `<Lanes label lanes trace onChoose clock locale labels ref />`: `lanes` applied when their content changes (a lane's `title`, `kind` and `now` are text), `label` and `trace` in place, `onChoose` read from the latest props, `clock`, `locale` and `labels` making a new instance (memoize `labels`), and a ref with `focus(key, options?)`.
- **Classes**: `bui-lanes` (`data-kind`, `data-facts`, `data-trace`), `bui-lanes-list`, `bui-lanes-lane` (`data-key`, `data-state`, `data-tone`, `data-depth`, `data-branch`, `data-through`), `bui-lanes-head`, `bui-lanes-mark`, `bui-lanes-title`, `bui-lanes-kind`, `bui-lanes-now`, `bui-lanes-time`, `bui-lanes-facts`, `bui-lanes-fact`, `bui-lanes-trace`, `bui-lanes-bar` (`data-open`). Every part is under `bui-lanes-`: `.bui-lane` and `.bui-lane-wide` stay the thread template's lane (D96).

```js
import { Lanes } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const lanes = new Lanes({ label: 'Subagents of this turn', clock, choose: key => sheet.open(key) }).mount(block);
lanes.update({ lanes: [{ key: 'call-1', state: 'running', stateLabel: 'Working', title: 'Explore the auth module', kind: 'Explore', now: 'Reading src/auth/session.ts', facts: [{ text: '48k', label: 'tokens' }], since: '2026-10-23T10:42:05Z' }] });
```

**TurnIndex** (DOM `TurnIndex`, React `TurnIndex`; since 0.29.0, D182, the owner's direction of 2026-10-24, after Codex's index of earlier messages). A thread's index of its turns: one mark per turn in the thread's gutter, beside the turns and halfway down the window while the thread scrolls (`position: sticky`, its list hanging before the column), shown from two turns and from a 1024 px region. `new TurnIndex({ label, labels, choose, onview, keys })` draws a navigation named by `label` (without one, `labels.group`, "Turns" / «Turnos»); the product places its `element` first in the thread's column (in a thread `Page`, its `region`), where it takes no room. `update({ turns, label? })` patches marks by `key`, so the one that holds focus keeps it; one that leaves hands focus to the mark now in its place. A turn is `{ key, title, summary?, facts?, state?, stateLabel?, href?, element?, head? }`: `title` names it (the person's message, plain text), `summary` the start of its answer, `facts` short words ("Changed 2 files", "12:01", "1 min 5 s"), `element` the turn on the page and `head` its opening message.

- **Marks.** A dash each, 8 px at rest, in the product's order, standing at its row's end beside the turns and growing away from them into the gutter; the turn being read is 14 px in the text's colour (`aria-current="true"`), and the mark under the pointer and its two neighbours on each side lengthen (18, 12 and 10 px), easing with `--motion-quick` and never under reduced motion. A turn that runs (`state: 'running'`), needs the person (`waiting`) or failed (`failed`) carries that state's `Status` shape before its dash, still (a half-filled circle, a diamond, a square); any other turn is a dash alone. Each mark is a whole row, so the pointer never falls between two turns: 4 to 12 px apart as the window holds them (12 to 24 px on a coarse pointer), in at most 60 % of the window, the list scrolling to keep the current one in view past that.
- **Names.** A mark is a link to the turn's `href` (so the browser follows it, adds it to history and opens it in another tab), or a button without one; its name is the title and then the state in words, the product's `stateLabel` or `labels` ("Running", "Needs you", "Failed" / «En curso», «Te necesita», «Falló»); the dash and the shape are hidden from assistive technology.
- **Preview.** The pointer resting on a mark, or keyboard focus on it, shows one card beside the index at the mark's height (placed by `Floating` through a point beside the list, flipped above near the window's bottom, kept inside the viewport, in the top layer; `--elevation-menu`): the title (semibold, two lines), the summary (muted, two lines), the state in words with its shape and the facts in tabular figures, and the keys that move between turns ("⌥↑ ⌥↓ Previous and next turn", `Alt+` outside Apple's platforms; `labels.keys`; not drawn on a touch screen). It describes the mark it shows (`aria-describedby`, `role="tooltip"`) and never takes the pointer; Escape hides it, and one Escape hid stays hidden when the pointer leaves. A touch press chooses; it is no hover.
- **Reading** (`onview`). The turn under the page's sticky edge (the document's `scroll-padding-top`) is the one being read, the last one once the page is scrolled to its end; `onview({ key, covered })` says which, and whether its `head` has gone above that edge, so the page can say the message in its header's compact line (`PageHeader`'s `subject`, D181). It is measured on scroll and resize at most once per animation frame, and at once by `measure()`; `current` and `covered` read it.
- **Keyboard.** One mark in the tab order: the focused one, else the turn being read, else the last focused, else the first; ArrowUp and ArrowDown move, Home and End jump, without wrapping; Enter follows the link. With `keys` (default true) Option/Alt with ArrowUp and ArrowDown move to the previous and next turn from anywhere on the page (`move(step)`), never while focus is in a field, a dialog, a menu or a list box, or when another part already took the key; going back from inside a turn whose message is out of view goes to that message first, and a second press moves on from the first one's turn before the page has scrolled. A move calls `choose(key, 'key')`, else replaces the address with the turn's `href`, else scrolls to the turn's `element`; a press on a mark without `href` calls `choose(key, 'press')`.
- **React**: `<TurnIndex label turns onChoose onView keys labels ref />`: `turns` applied when the array changes (memoize it; `element` and `head` come from refs), `label` in place, `onChoose` and `onView` read from the latest props, `keys` and `labels` making a new instance (memoize `labels`), and a ref with `focus(key, options?)`, `move(step)` and `measure()`. Its host takes no box of its own (`display: contents`), so the index stays sticky in the thread's column.
- **Classes**: `bui-turns`, `bui-turns-list`, `bui-turns-row`, `bui-turns-mark` (`aria-current`, `data-near`, `data-state`), `bui-turns-dash`, `bui-turns-state`, `bui-turns-preview`, `bui-turns-preview-title`, `bui-turns-preview-summary`, `bui-turns-preview-facts`, `bui-turns-preview-keys`.

```js
import { TurnIndex } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const index = new TurnIndex({ onview: ({ key, covered }) => (header.subject = covered ? { text: messages.get(key), href: `#turn=${key}` } : null) });
page.region.prepend(index.element);
index.update({ turns: turns.map(turn => ({ key: turn.key, title: turn.message, summary: turn.answer, facts: [turn.time], state: turn.waits ? 'waiting' : null, href: `#turn=${turn.key}`, element: turn.element, head: turn.card })) });
```

React: `<Icon name="plus" />`, `<Icon name="alert" size={16} />`, `<Icon name="bell" size={24} label="Notifications" />`. **Tooltips of icon-only controls** (since 0.3.0). Every icon-only control of the package shows its accessible name as a tooltip: the dialog's close button, a toast's dismiss button, a chip's remove button, the header's navigation toggle, `Help`, the notification bell, the family bar's account before a name is known and an `ActionMenu` shown as its glyph alone (`label: null` with `name`). The tooltip shows on hover after 300 ms, at once on keyboard focus (`:focus-visible`), and for 1.5 s on a touch press; a press, leaving, blur and Escape hide it, and it never shows over the open panel of an expanded control. It repeats the name the control already has, so it is `aria-hidden` and describes nothing: a screen reader hears the name once. Inside a modal dialog it is drawn in the dialog, above the page. A `Disclosure` takes `hint: true` for a glyph-only button. ## Preferences

**Preferences** (DOM `Preferences`, React `Preferences` and `usePreferences`; since 0.3.0, decision D07). A person's language (`en`, `es`) and appearance (`system`, `light`, `dark`) in one product. Accounts holds the person's choice; the product applies it when it learns who the person is and keeps a device copy for the next first paint. A change made inside the product applies on this device only and lasts until the account's values change (since 0.3.1: a reload or a new arrival with the same account values keeps it); the product offers "Change for all of Beyond", a link to the Accounts account page, and never writes the account setting. Motion and density stay per device and are not part of it.

```js
import { Preferences } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const preferences = new Preferences({ key: 'beyond-projects', fallback: { appearance: 'light', locale: 'en' } });
preferences.restore();                                      // first paint, before rendering: the device copy or the fallback
preferences.apply({ appearance: identity.appearance, locale: identity.locale }); // on every arrival: the account wins when its values changed
preferences.choose({ appearance: 'dark' });                 // a change made in the product: this device only
preferences.appearance; preferences.locale; preferences.current; // 'dark', 'en', a frozen { appearance, locale }
const release = preferences.subscribe(({ appearance, locale }) => render()); // returns the release
link.textContent = preferences.labels.everywhere;           // "Change for all of Beyond" / "Cambiar en todo Beyond"
```

- **Options.** `key`: the device copy's storage key, one per product. `fallback: { appearance, locale }`: the product's defaults (Beyond Home's appearance is `light`). `locales` (default `['en', 'es']`): the languages the product is localized in; an English-only product passes `['en']` and stays English whatever the account says. `storage` (default `localStorage`; `null` stores nothing) and `root` (default `<html>`) exist for tests and embedded surfaces.
- **Effect.** Every call sets `data-beyond-mode` on the root to `light` or `dark`, removes it for `system` (the stylesheet then follows the system preference), and sets `lang` to the language. Each returns the frozen `current`.
- **`apply(values)`.** The account's values win and become the device copy, unless they equal the account values this device last applied: then a choice made here since stays (so a product may call `apply` on every page load). The first arrival on a device, another person's values, or a change made at Accounts wins. A device copy from 0.3.0, which does not remember the account's values, is overridden once. A missing, `null` or unknown appearance is unset: the product's default applies, even over an earlier device choice. The earlier account default `system` is accepted as a choice. A language outside `locales` leaves the product's default. Fields other than the two are ignored and never stored.
- **`choose(values)`.** Changes only the values given, on this device; an appearance or language outside the lists throws a `TypeError` and changes nothing.
- **Device copy.** JSON `{ appearance, locale, account: { appearance, locale } }` under `key`: the values in effect and the account's values last applied, where `null` is a value never chosen; never an account identifier. Every storage access is guarded: when storage is missing, full or refused (a private window, blocked site data), the values last until the page closes.
- **`subscribe(listener)`.** Calls the listener with `current` after each change of the values in effect (not when a call changes nothing) and returns the release; it may be passed detached. A throwing listener does not stop the others; its error goes to `reportError`.
- **Copy.** `Preferences.labels.en` and `.es`: `everywhere` ("Change for all of Beyond", "Cambiar en todo Beyond"), `appearance` ("Appearance", "Apariencia"), `system` ("System", "Sistema"), `light` ("Light", "Claro"), `dark` ("Dark", "Oscuro"), `language` ("Language", "Idioma"); `preferences.labels` is the set of the language in effect. `Preferences.appearances` lists the three appearances.
- **React.** Create the instance once outside React and call `restore()` before the first render; `usePreferences(preferences)` returns `current` and re-renders after each change.

## Diff (0.11.2)

**Diff** (DOM `Diff`, React `Diff`). A change's files in a unified view a developer can trust, for a side panel such as Conduict's "View changes". It never invents a line: what the input does not carry is said, never offered, and what cannot be read is said while the rest shows.

```js
import { Diff } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const diff = new Diff({ patch, label: 'Changes of the conversation' }).mount(panel);
diff.patch = next;                    // or diff.files = [...]: each file keeps its open state by path, the focused header keeps focus
diff.open(path); diff.close(path); diff.reveal(path); // reveal opens, scrolls (at once under reduced motion) and focuses its header
diff.files;                           // the files as data with their counts
const files = Diff.parse(text);       // the same data without a DOM; never throws
```

```jsx
<Diff patch={patch} labels={Diff.labels.es} locale="es" />
```

- **Input.** `patch`: a unified diff in git's format (`diff --git`, `new file mode`, `deleted file mode`, `old mode`/`new mode`, `similarity index`, `rename from`/`to`, `copy from`/`to`, `index`, `Binary files … differ`, `GIT binary patch`, `---`/`+++` with `/dev/null`, `@@ -a,b +c,d @@ section`, `\ No newline at end of file`) or a plain unified diff of `---`/`+++` pairs (a timestamp after a tab is dropped; `a/` and `b/` are stripped only when both sides carry them). A hunk's body is read by its header's counts, so a deleted line that reads `--- x` is a deletion. Quoted paths are decoded (C escapes and octal bytes as UTF-8); CRLF is tolerated; text before the first file (a commit message, a diffstat) and a trailing signature are not parts. `files`: `[{ path, previous?, status?, binary?, mode?: { old?, new? }, hunks?: [{ old, new, header?, lines }], added?, removed? }]`, each line `' x'`, `'-x'`, `'+x'`, `'\ …'` or `{ kind: 'context'|'add'|'delete'|'note', text }`; counts come from the lines when absent and the status is inferred when absent (a `previous` is a rename, a hunk from old line 0 with only additions is an addition, one to new line 0 with only deletions a deletion). `patch` wins when both are given.
- **Data.** `Diff.parse(text)` and `diff.files` give `{ path, previous, status, binary, mode, hunks: [{ old, new, header, lines: [{ kind, text }] }], added, removed, unread }`, where `unread` counts the parts of that file that could not be read (a hunk header that does not parse, a hunk cut short, lines past its counts, a hunk or line of `files` without numbers or a known kind). A part outside any file, a file without a path, or text with no file at all is said at the top.
- **Drawn.** The summary line (`summary`, true by default): "3 files changed · +52 −3" / «3 archivos cambiados · +52 −3», with Open all and Fold all from two files. From `Diff.jump` (4) files, a `nav` "Files in this change" lists every file (path, status, counts); a press opens it, scrolls to it and focuses its header. Each file is a `section` named by its path; its header is a heading (`level`, 3 by default) holding the disclosure button (`aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`, the `chevron` glyph turning beside the words, the path with its directory muted and its name in medium weight, wrapping anywhere and never cut, a rename as "old → new", the status in words, "Added", "Modified", "Deleted", "Renamed", "Copied" or "Mode changed", and "+12 −3" read as "12 added lines, 3 removed lines"), with a `CopyButton` "Copy path" beside it ("Copy the path {path}") that copies the new path, the old one for a deletion. The diff's root carries one hidden description of its keys (`aria-describedby`).
- **Lines.** A unified view with old and new numbers and a sign column (`+`, `−`), the code in `--font-mono` with its whitespace, additions and deletions on `--color-success-surface` and `--color-danger-surface` with the sign in `--color-success` and `--color-danger`, so they differ by more than color. Numbers and signs are generated by the stylesheet (`data-number`, `data-sign`) in an `aria-hidden` gutter, and a hidden prefix that a selection skips says "Added line 12: ", "Removed line 9: " or "Line 14: " when a screen reader reaches the line: a selection of the code copies only code. Nothing is announced by itself. Hunk headers are muted rows (`@@ -12,7 +12,9 @@ section`) that `]` and `[` focus (`tabindex="-1"`). Between hunks whose old lines skip, "{n} unchanged lines not in this patch" (never an expander); inside a hunk, a run of more than `2 × Diff.context + 2` unchanged lines (`Diff.context` is 3) keeps three at each end and folds the rest behind "Show {n} unchanged lines", which shows exactly the input's lines. `\ No newline at end of file` is a muted row "No newline at end of file". Notes under the header: "Binary file · not shown", "Renamed without changes", "Copied without changes", "File mode changed from 100644 to 100755", "Empty file", "Whitespace changes only" (each change block equal once whitespace is removed; the lines still show) and "This part of the patch couldn't be read".
- **Width.** Each file's lines scroll sideways in their own box (`overflow-x: auto`, `tabindex="0"`, `role="group"` named "Lines of {path}"), never the page, with the gutter kept in view (`position: sticky`, on the row's background); hunk headers, gaps and folded runs keep their words in view the same way. No sideways scroll at 320 px.
- **Budget.** `Diff.budget = { lines: 3000, file: 600 }`: with `open: 'auto'` (default) files open while their running total of lines fits `lines` (the first that does not ends it); a file over `file` lines starts folded and opens on "Large diff · {n} lines" with Show, which draws `Diff.chunk` (500) rows per animation frame with the box `aria-busy` meanwhile, a timer standing in where there are no frames; `destroy()` cancels the next frame. A folded file builds nothing until it first opens. `open: 'all'` opens every file (a large one still asks), `'none'` none.
- **Keys.** On a file's header, ArrowDown and ArrowUp move to the next and previous header and Home and End to the first and last (the accordion pattern, no wrapping); anywhere inside, `]` and `[` move to the next and previous hunk header of the open files and Alt+ArrowDown and Alt+ArrowUp to the next and previous file's header. Keys typed in a field inside and keys with Ctrl or ⌘ are left alone.
- **Options.** `patch`, `files`, `label` (the root's accessible name, "Changes" / «Cambios»), `level` (2, 3 or 4), `open` (`auto`, `all`, `none`), `summary`, `labels` and `locale` (the counts' digits through `Intl.NumberFormat`). React applies a new `patch` or `files` to the same diff; the other props make a new one. `Diff.parse` and `Diff.labels` are on the React component too.
- **Copy.** `Diff.labels.en` and `.es`: counts are functions of `{ count, number }` for each language's plurals (a product may give a string with `{number}`), and each set carries `CopyButton`'s result words under `done`, `refused`, `said`, `why` and `selected`.
- **No syntax highlighting.** The package ships no highlighter and adds no dependency for one: code is shown as written in the monospaced face, and a product that wants highlighting needs a separate decision about its library and the cost of shipping it.
- **Classes.** `bui-diff` (root `section`), `bui-diff-top`, `bui-diff-summary`, `bui-diff-total`, `bui-diff-actions`, `bui-diff-jump` (`nav`), `bui-diff-jump-list`, `bui-diff-jump-item` (`data-path`, `data-status`), `bui-diff-files`, `bui-diff-file` (`data-status`, `data-path`, `data-open`), `bui-diff-head`, `bui-diff-heading`, `bui-diff-toggle`, `bui-diff-chevron`, `bui-diff-path`, `bui-diff-dir`, `bui-diff-name`, `bui-diff-arrow`, `bui-diff-status`, `bui-diff-count`, `bui-diff-plus`, `bui-diff-minus`, `bui-diff-body`, `bui-diff-note`, `bui-diff-large`, `bui-diff-lines`, `bui-diff-rows`, `bui-diff-hunk`, `bui-diff-line` (`data-kind`: `context`, `add`, `delete`, `note`), `bui-diff-gutter`, `bui-diff-num` (`data-number`), `bui-diff-sign` (`data-sign`), `bui-diff-code`, `bui-diff-said`, `bui-diff-gap`, `bui-diff-more`, `bui-diff-reveal`, `bui-diff-aside`. Styles in `diff.css` and `diff-lines.css`, tokens only.
- **Checked by** `tests/diff.test.mjs` (the parser), `diff-view.test.mjs` (drawing, notes, summary, list, Copy path, Spanish), `diff-keys.test.mjs` (keys, budget, chunks per frame), `diff-update.test.mjs` (`files`, updates, destroy) and `react-diff.test.mjs`, with patches in `tests/fixtures/diff/`; in the browser by `acceptance/checks/diffing.mjs` and `diffing-keys.mjs` on the `diff.html` pages (English DOM, Spanish React) in Chrome, Firefox and WebKit.

## One inset (D184)

Since 0.32.0 every box of the package that holds text or controls inside a border or a fill keeps one inset on every side, measured optically (D184, the owner's direction of 2026-10-24, first carried out by the composer in 0.29.0): from the inner border edge to the first line's capitals, to the controls' edges and to the text's start, its controls starting on the text's edge and ending on its end. A box names its inset as a custom property on the box, a step of the spacing scale that is its earlier side padding, so widths do not change; a product may set it on one box. The rules live in `styles/insets.css` (the page's boxes) and `styles/insets-floating.css` (the surfaces over the page), late in the cascade:

- **The box's own text** starts or ends it: its padding takes off the half-leading the line keeps above its capitals and below its baseline, `calc(inset − (1lh − 1cap) / 2)`, as the composer's does, inside `@supports (padding-top: calc(1lh − 1cap))`. Inter's capitals sit in the middle of its line, so this holds within 0.3 px in Firefox and WebKit and 0.7 px in Chrome at the body, small and label sizes.
- **A heading, a label or code** in another size or face leads or ends it: that line trims itself to its capitals and its baseline (`text-box: trim-start | trim-end cap alphabetic`, inside `@supports (text-box: …)`), exact in any font. Chrome places a baseline on a whole pixel, which puts the formula 1.3 px off for a 19 px title-2 line. A code block trims its `code` (a `<code>` inside takes the browser's own monospace, so it is that line's face that counts); text that scrolls in its own box takes off its own half-leading instead (`margin-block-start: calc((1cap − 1lh) / 2)`), since Firefox trims nothing in a scroller.
- **A row of controls** starts or ends it: the padding is the inset itself, to the controls' edges. A borderless control is its words or its glyph: a close button's glyph ends one inset from the end and its 32 px target hangs past it (`calc(inset − (target − 18px) / 2)`); folded Technical details at a box's end, and a quiet action that ends a callout's, an `Unavailable`'s or an empty state's last row, hang their target below their words (`margin-block-end: calc((1cap − height) / 2)`), the quiet action keeping a bordered neighbour's top.
- **A free side** (a short line in a box wider than what it holds, a last row that is the product's own) may hold more room but never less than the inset.
- Without `lh` and `cap`, or `text-box`, a box keeps its earlier padding.

## Focus (D187)

Since 0.34.0 the package draws the keyboard's place by what the keys do there, never one ring for everything (`styles/focus.css`, right after `base.css`):

- **The ring: a control the keyboard reached.** `--color-focus`, 2 px, 2 px outside the control's own shape (an outline follows its radius), only on `:focus-visible`, so a press draws none. It settles onto the control in `--motion-quick`, from 6 px out and clear, and appears at once under reduced motion. The rule has no specificity and covers every element of the page, a product's own included, so a product drops its own copy of the ring; a component's or a product's own rule wins.
- **The edge: a box that takes typing or keys.** A `Field`'s input, textarea and select, `SecretField`, the searches (`Collection`'s, the sidebar's, a `Picker`'s, a `ChoiceMenu`'s, the family bar's), a `CommandCard`'s editable command and the composer's box light their own border in `--color-focus-edge` with `--bui-focus-halo`, a 3 px ring of that light at 14 %, from a pointer as from the keyboard. Nothing rings them, and a field inside a lit box draws nothing. The caret is coral (`caret-color: var(--color-focus)`). A wrong value keeps the danger's edge and takes `--bui-focus-halo-danger`. A product's own box takes the edge with the class `bui-edge`, lighting while a field inside it has the focus (`data-invalid` for its wrong value).
  - A `CommandCard` takes Enter and Escape while it has the focus, however it got it, so it lights on `:focus`: a line of the edge's light around its border, keeping its attention stripe and its elevation.
  - A diff's lines, a region that scrolls by keys with no border of its own, light their edge inside them.
  - The command palette's field is the whole of what is open and keeps only its coral caret.
- **The cursor: the item the arrow keys are on.** A row (`ListRow` in `ul.bui-rows`, `Collection`'s list and table), a menu's entry, a `Picker`'s active option, a turn's mark, a lane, a diff's hunk and the sidebar's and bar's entries are lit as the pointer lights them and ringed inside (`outline-offset: -2px`). The cursor moves at once (`animation: none`). The selection keeps its tint and its coral bar at the start; the cursor never takes that bar.
- **Nothing: a place reading starts.** An element the product focuses so reading starts there draws nothing, from the keyboard as from a pointer, when Tab does not reach it (`tabindex="-1"`) and it has no interactive role: a page's title after a navigation, a side sheet's title, `Collection`'s region after a page change, or a product's heading, row or main. The next Tab rings the next control. Until 0.32.1 a side sheet's title and `Collection`'s region drew a ring from the keyboard. An item the arrow keys reach names its role, or the rule leaves it nothing.
- **Never covered.** `PageDock` measures the docked composer into `--bui-page-dock`. The document's `scroll-padding-bottom` then keeps what the keyboard reaches, or what the page scrolls to, above the dock and its fade, as `scroll-padding-top` keeps it under the sticky lines (WCAG 2.4.11).
- **A forced-colors theme.** Shadows and fills are not drawn there, so an edge takes a 2 px `Highlight` outline; a ring is an outline already.
- **A product's reset.** The ring has no specificity, so a reset that styles focus unlayered outranks it. Tailwind 3's preflight ships `:-moz-focusring { outline: auto; }`, which Firefox applies to every focused control, the package's own included, once the product's own ring is gone (Chromium ignores it). A product with such a reset restores the family's ring after it with `:-moz-focusring { outline: 2px solid var(--color-focus); }`. Tailwind 4's preflight sits in `@layer base`, which the package's unlayered rules outrank.
- **The composer.** Its box lights while its field has the focus and rests while one of its controls has it, which is ringed. A press in the box where nothing answers it (its padding, the toolbar's gaps, the room under the words) puts the caret in the field without the focus leaving, so the edge never flickers. The live line runs 1 px inside the top edge, so a lit edge never hides it.

`--color-focus-edge` holds 3:1 on the canvas, the surface and the raised surface (WCAG 1.4.11). From `--color-border-control` it changes by 3.6:1 in light and 2.3:1 on navy, where the halo and the caret add to it; WCAG 2.4.13, a AAA criterion, is not met on navy. Until 0.32.1 the composer's box drew a 2 px coral border with a glow inside it, and a field a coral ring 1 px outside its border. On a box, a coral border reads as its error: the dark coral (`#ffa385`) sits next to the dark danger (`#ff8a80`), and the light coral next to the light danger.
