# Component catalog

Every component of `@beyond-js/ui` 0.11.0: what it is for, its variants and main options, the states it states in words, and its consumers. DOM names come from `@beyond-js/ui` (or `/dom`); React names from `@beyond-js/ui/react`. Every component takes `labels` for its copy (strings with `{placeholders}` or functions of the values) and every DOM class has `mount(parent)` and `destroy()`. Type declarations in `types/` list every option.

**Consumers** (each product vendors the tarball in its own `tools/` and records its adoption, residual copies and evidence in its own repository; the family reference catalogs them component by component in `branding/src/family/components/consumers.js`): on 0.1.7 or later since 2026-09-28 (the Collection consumers, Delegate, Conduict and Branding, on 0.1.8): the Beyond desktop (plain DOM), the Delegate application (React, the whole application), Branding (plain DOM), the Conduict interface and its Desktop surface (plain DOM), the Projects interface (plain DOM served by its service), the Snapshots and Accounts frontends and the Workspace client (React), and the CDN administration and backoffice (React, through the package's `Dialog`). Every product that names itself beside the wordmark uses `Lockup`.

## Foundations

**Tokens** (`@beyond-js/ui/tokens`, `@beyond-js/ui/tokens.css`). Token set 0.2.0, `status: 'approved'`: the owner approved the palette, roles, type scale and elevation on 2026-09-29 (decisions D08, D09, D10, D12 and D20, recorded in `tokens.approval`). It is token set 0.1.0 (packages 0.1.0 to 0.2.2) plus `--color-border-control`, `--weight-regular`, the window elevations and the amended label; nothing else changed. Each entry's `provenance` still says whether its value was captured from the public site or introduced by the family reference. Components read semantic roles only (`--color-<role>`), never primitives, and so do products on their own surfaces instead of local color values. The theme follows `data-beyond-mode` (`light` or `dark`), falling back to the system preference. Density follows `data-density="compact"` on the root; coarse pointers always get 44 px targets. Consumer: Branding (tokens only).

- **Borders.** `--color-border` and `--color-border-strong` separate content: dividers, in-page surfaces, tags. `--color-border-control` (`#767676` light, 4.54:1 on white; `#8e99bb` dark, 5.78:1 on the dark surface) is the boundary of inputs, selects, checkboxes, toggles, menu and disclosure buttons and secondary buttons, a `graphic` pair (3:1) the token tests check in both themes. A divider inside a control (between toggles) keeps `border-strong`.
- **Type.** `--text-title-1` 1.625rem, `--text-title-2` 1.1875rem, `--text-title-3` 1rem, `--text-body` 0.9375rem (15 px), `--text-small` 0.8125rem (13 px), `--text-label` 0.75rem (12 px, the smallest text) with `--tracking-label` 0.02em. Weights: `--weight-light` (300) only at body size and larger; `--weight-regular` (400) for small text, tables, hints and reasons; `--weight-medium` (500) for headings, labels, names and buttons. A label (table heading, tag, menu heading, a stacked row's column label) is told apart by weight 500 and the muted color, shown as written in sentence case: no component transforms case (D20).
- **Rubik** (`@beyond-js/ui/fonts.css`). The package ships Rubik 300, 400 and 500 in the latin and latin-ext subsets as woff2 (`@beyond-js/ui/fonts/rubik-<subset>-<weight>-normal.woff2`, the files of `@fontsource/rubik` 5.3.0, unchanged) with its licence (`@beyond-js/ui/fonts/OFL.txt`, SIL Open Font License 1.1). `fonts.css` declares one `@font-face` per weight and subset with its `unicode-range` and `font-display: swap`, so a page downloads only the weights it uses and latin-ext only when its text needs it. `styles.css` loads no font: a bundled product imports `@beyond-js/ui/fonts.css` once, beside the two stylesheets; a product that is not bundled serves `fonts.css` and the woff2 files together, keeping their names. Products drop Google Fonts, `@fontsource` packages and their own copies.
- **Elevation** (D10). Only what overlaps other content casts a shadow: `--elevation-menu` (menus, popovers, tooltips, toasts, drawers), `--elevation-dialog` (dialogs and, since 0.7.0, side sheets), and `--elevation-window` and `--elevation-window-focus` (Desktop windows at rest and focused, from the Desktop's frame shadows, resolved per theme). Inset or ring shadows that mark a state or draw focus are marks, not elevation. Surfaces inside the page are flat, with 1 px borders; a unit test holds the stylesheet to it.
- **The brand orange** (D12). `#e46f4e` (coral-400, the wordmark) is the brand orange; on light backgrounds its text and fill variant is `#9e533e` (coral-600, the light `action` and `link`), on navy its text variant is `#ffa385` (coral-200, the dark `link`). `#cb6245` (coral-500) is only the light `accent` and `focus` color, never called the brand color. White text never sits on `#e46f4e` (3.14:1): the dark action puts navy on it (`on-action`). The primitives state this in their `use` field.

**Motion.** 150 ms and 200 ms with one easing, for entering panels and dialogs and for control color changes. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` nothing animates or transitions; spinners stop and state stays in words.

## Actions

**Button** (`Button`; React `Button`, `useBusy`). Variants `primary` (one per view), `secondary`, `quiet`, `danger`; `small`; `glyph`; `href` renders a link styled as a button. `busy` keeps focus and place, marks `aria-disabled`, shows a spinner and says it is working; `run(work)` and React `useBusy()` wrap an asynchronous action so a second press is ignored.

**ActionMenu** (`ActionMenu`). A menu button of actions (ARIA menu pattern) for secondary actions on a record. Items: `label`, `run` (React `onSelect`) or `href`, `disabled` with `reason`, `tone: 'danger'`. ArrowDown/ArrowUp open on the first/last item, arrows, Home and End move, Escape closes and returns focus, Tab closes, a press outside closes. A disabled item stays reachable and reads its reason. The list stays inside the viewport (since 0.1.4): `placement: 'auto'` (default) opens below the button, or above it when there is no room below and more above; `'below'` and `'above'` fix the side; it is shifted sideways when an edge would cut it and capped to the room on its side, where it scrolls. Focus moves into it, within it and back to the button without scrolling the page or any scroll container around it. Use links in navigation, not this menu.

**Disclosure** (`Disclosure`). A button that shows and hides a panel (account menu, header panels). Escape and the button close and return focus; a press outside closes. It traps nothing. Closing hides the panel at once; since 0.1.5 an inert picture of a floating panel (`.bui-disclosure-leaving`: no identifiers, hidden from assistive technology) eases out where it was and removes itself. The picture keeps the panel's classes, so since 0.5.1 it never outlives a reopening: opening again or destroying removes it, and an open panel never has a copy beside it (a query for an entry's class in an open menu finds one). A panel in the page's flow (help) closes with no picture, which would hold its place, and with reduced motion there is none. A product that restyles the panel styles the picture with it.

## Forms

**Field** (`Field`). Label, hint, control and error wired with `aria-describedby` and `aria-invalid`. DOM `check()` uses the browser's constraints plus `validate(value)` and shows the consumer's `messages.<failure>`; after an error the field rechecks while typing. React `Field` wraps one control child and takes `hint`, `error`, `optional`. **A suggested value** (since 0.7.0, D56, CNT-95): `suggest` shows a value derived from context (a slug of a project's name, the next unique name, a directory from a package name), marked "Suggested" («Sugerido») after the label, and the field follows every new `field.suggest = …` until the person types a value of their own (`edited`); a field emptied and left takes the suggestion back, `follow()` returns to it, and a `value` the product gives stands. React: `const name = useSuggestion(suggestion)` returns `{ value, onChange, onBlur, suggested, follow }` with the same rule, and `<Field suggested={name.suggested}>` draws the mark. `Field.labels.en` / `.es` carry the copy.

**Choices** (`Choices`). Checkboxes (value is an array) or radios (value is a string) in a fieldset with legend, hint and error; options may be disabled with a visible `reason`. Since 0.7.0 a radio group with one option that can be chosen is a statement, not a choice (D56): the legend as a label and the option as text (an `<output>`), with its value submitted under `name` (`stated`, `statement: false` keeps the radio). A single checkbox stays a checkbox: it is a yes or no. Since 0.7.1 the statement keeps everything the option said: its `hint` on its own line, describing the statement (`aria-describedby`), and its `status` (`[label, tone]`), which an option of a group also shows beside its label, so a lone AI engine reads "Claude Code · Ready" with "Connected · Max plan" under it and needs no `statement: false`.

**Select** (`Select`). A styled native select for short finite lists; keeps native keyboard and touch pickers. Use it for plain values a person reads at a glance; use `ChoiceMenu` for a few things that have a state, and `Picker` for searchable or large entity collections. A native select clips a chosen text wider than its box; since 0.5.0 (D44 as amended on 2026-10-03) that text shows whole in a tooltip on hover and keyboard focus, only while it is cut, measured in the select's own font (DOM and React alike; the tooltip is `aria-hidden`, since the select's value already says the whole text, and a press hides it). The platform's list shows every option whole. A product that draws its own select markup gives it the same tooltip with `Select.tip(control)`, which returns a `NameTip` to destroy with the control (declared since 0.5.1). Fixed labels must fit: where they may not, use `ChoiceMenu`, which wraps. Since 0.7.0 one option that can be chosen is a statement (D56): the option as text in an `<output>` that the `Field`'s label names, its value submitted under `name`; `stated` is true and `control` is the output; `statement: false` keeps the select. A disabled single option is not stated. Since 0.7.1 an option's `hint` (or `detail`) and `status`, which a native select cannot show, are kept by its statement. The React `Select` renders the same markup.

**ChoiceMenu** (`ChoiceMenu`, since 0.3.2; React `ChoiceMenu` since 0.7.0, CNT-94). One choice among a few things that have a state, such as an environment that may have failed or an AI engine that may need a sign-in. The button names the choice and its value (`label`, "Environment: lab"), with the state's dot and, when the state asks for attention (`danger`, `warning`), its words and a border of that tone; a calm state stays in the accessible name. It never cuts its words: it grows, and wraps in a narrow container (so D44 holds without a tooltip, checked again in 0.5.0). It opens the family's menu (ARIA menu pattern) with the options as `menuitemradio` (`value`, `label`, `detail`, `status: [label, tone]`, `disabled` with `reason`), the chosen one checked, and `actions` (`label`, `run`) after a separator, such as "New environment…". ArrowDown and ArrowUp open on the chosen option, arrows, Home and End move, Enter or Space chooses, Escape closes and returns focus, Tab closes and moves on, a press outside closes. A disabled option stays reachable so its reason can be read and is never chosen. `onchange(value)` runs only for a different choice; setting `value`, `options`, `actions` or `disabled` redraws without calling it. Since 0.7.0: printable keys move to the next option that starts with them (type-ahead); a list longer than `ChoiceMenu.threshold` (15), or with `search: true` or a number as its own threshold, opens on a search field above the options that filters by the beginning of the text or of any segment after `/`, `-`, `_`, `.` or a space, ignoring case and accents ("login" finds `feature/login-page`), says "Nothing matches …", chooses the first match on Enter, clears on Escape and closes on a second Escape; ArrowDown goes into the options and ArrowUp from the first back to the field; an option's `search` is what it matches when its label is not plain text. One option that can be chosen and no action is a statement (D56): "Infrastructure Docker on this computer" as text with its state and, since 0.7.1, its `detail` on its own line, `value` that option's and nothing to open (`stated`; `statement: false` keeps the menu). `name` submits the value in a hidden input; `labels` (`ChoiceMenu.labels.en` / `.es`: placeholder, search, none). React `ChoiceMenu` takes the same props with `onChange` and actions' `onSelect`, read from the latest props; option labels are strings there. Consumers: Conduict's composer (environment, AI engine, repository, saved instructions).

**ChoiceChip** (DOM and React `ChoiceChip`; since 0.11.0, built on `ChoiceMenu`). A compact choice for a composer's settings slot or a form: the chip shows a muted label, its value and, when there is one, a state in words with its tone ("Model Opus 5.5 Default", "Environment My first VM Stopped"); the state's dot only ever sits beside its word, never alone (D42), and a calm state is shown too, unlike `ChoiceMenu`'s button. It opens the same menu: options with their label, description (`detail`), state in words and, when they cannot be chosen, `disabled` with `reason` (reachable and read, never chosen); the same keyboard (ArrowDown/ArrowUp open on the chosen option, arrows, Home, End, type-ahead, Enter or Space, Escape back to the chip, Tab out), the search past the threshold and one option stated (compactly, with its state and hint). It takes `ChoiceMenu`'s options and `state` (`[label, tone]`): the chip's own state shown over the chosen option's, such as an engine that needs a sign-in whatever model is chosen; the `state` setter redraws without calling `onchange`. It is the target size (`--bui-target`, 44 px on a touch screen) and wraps rather than cuts. `ChoiceChip.labels` are `ChoiceMenu.labels`. Classes: `bui-chip` on the holder, `bui-chip-state`. The holder draws no box of its own: the chip's one border is its button's (since 0.11.1; the picker's chosen-chip box is scoped to its list, `.bui-chips > .bui-chip`, which 0.11.0's holder also matched, drawing a second border around the button). React: `<ChoiceChip label options value state onChange … />` with `ChoiceMenu`'s props.

```js
import { ChoiceChip, Composer } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const model = new ChoiceChip({ label: 'Model', options: [{ value: 'opus', label: 'Opus 5.5', detail: 'claude-opus-5-5', status: ['Default', 'neutral'] }, { value: 'sonnet', label: 'Sonnet 5' }], value: 'opus', onchange: value => conversation.model(value) });
const autonomy = new ChoiceChip({ label: 'Autonomy', options: [{ value: 'ask', label: 'Ask me' }, { value: 'folder', label: 'Work in its folder', disabled: true, reason: "This engine's route can't enforce it" }], value: 'ask' });
```

**Picker** (`Picker`). Searchable single or multiple entity picker over an asynchronous paged `source({ query, filters, cursor, limit, signal })` returning `{ items, next, total }`. Options: `label`, `multiple`, `selected` (initial, may carry `state: 'stale' | 'ineligible' | 'unavailable'` and `reason`), `filters` (finite selects), `name` (hidden inputs for forms), `hint`, `limit`, `delay`, `all` (multiple only: a "Select all shown" action that adds, in one change, every result shown that can be chosen; offered while one is left, focus returning to the search field when it leaves; since 0.1.3). States: loading, results count (`12 of 45 shown`), no matches for the query or filters, nothing to choose, failure with retry, "Load more". Choices are kept by id across queries, filters and pages, counted ("3 selected · 1 needs attention") and listed as removable chips; removing a chip keeps focus. Disabled results show their reason and are never chosen. Names are never cut (D44): chips, results and their descriptions wrap; its filters are `Select`s with the 0.5.0 tooltip. Keyboard: arrows and Page Up/Down move the active option (`aria-activedescendant`), Enter chooses without submitting a form, Escape clears the query. A late answer of an older query never replaces a newer one. The product revalidates on commit and reports findings with `mark(id, { state, reason })`; authorization stays in the product.

**The resource picker** (`Picker` since 0.7.0, D56 "Choose, never type", CNT-93, the repository connection review's section 8 and design S2). The same component with the parts a resource needs; every part is optional and the earlier picker is unchanged without them.

- **From [account ▾]** (`accounts: { items, value?, connect? }`): a `ChoiceMenu` labelled "From" («Desde») above the list with the accounts the results come from (`{ id, label, detail?, status?, disabled?, reason? }`, such as "acme · GitHub organization · Selected repositories"); one that cannot be used (suspended, removed, paused) stays listed with its one-line reason and is never chosen; `connect` (`{ label, run }`, "Install on another GitHub organization") is the action after them. The account in view is the picker's state (`account`), passed to the source as `account`; without `value` it is the first usable one; the `accounts` setter replaces them, keeping the one in view while usable, and a new account in view searches again. A choice records the account it came from (`selected[i].account`). One account and no action is a statement ("From acme").
- **The gate** (`gate`, the `gate` setter): `Unavailable`'s options (`title`, `reason`, `owner`, `action`, `secondary`, `kind`, default `association`) shown in place of the count, search and list while nothing can be listed yet, with the product's one action: not connected ("Connect your GitHub account to see the repositories you can choose", Connect GitHub), no installation (Install the Beyond app / Ask an owner or admin with a `CopyMessage`), single sign-on (Sign in to acme on GitHub, Check again), adding restricted. The source is asked nothing while it is gated; `gate = null` lists again.
- **Unavailable, never empty** (D40): every request is bounded (`bound`, default 20 s); a source that does not answer in time is aborted and the status says "The choices didn't answer in time, so they can't be listed now." with Try again; `explain(error)` gives the product's words ("Beyond Projects didn't answer, so repositories can't be listed now."). A failure is never "nothing to choose".
- **Rows** (`PickerItem`): `avatar` (an image address) or `glyph`; the name, then `marks` (`[{ label, tone?, reason? }]`: "In Website", "Already here", "Archived on GitHub"), each a tag whose reason follows in words; one line of facts: "Private" with a lock or "Public" in words (D11), `meta` (the default branch), "Updated 2 h ago" (`updated`), one `state` (`[label, tone]`); the description; and the reason of a `disabled` row. A row's one action is choosing it: it holds no link, button or field (Projects puts "Use in" beside the picker). Conflict marks that block are a `disabled` row with its `reason`; the product maps its codes (`in-project`, `in-other-project`, `archived`, `awaiting-confirmation`) to marks, `disabled` and words.
- **A suggested group**: the source may answer `suggested: { label, items }` with the first page, drawn first as an ARIA group named by its heading ("Recent", default "Suggested"), its items not listed twice.
- **A paste recognizer** (`recognize(text)` → `{ label, id? | match?(item), query?, value? }` or null; `onrecognize(found)`): text typed or pasted in the search field that the product recognizes (`https://github.com/acme/web`, `git@github.com:acme/web.git`, `acme/web`) is searched as `query`; once the list answers, a listed match is chosen and announced ("acme/web selected"), a disabled one says why ("acme/site can't be chosen: Already in Storefront"), and one not listed says "acme/web isn't in this list." while `onrecognize` hands the product `{ label, value, outcome: 'picked' | 'refused' | 'missing', item }` to offer its own way (an inline "Add a public repository by its address"). The parsed `label` is shown, never the raw text; a paste is read at once, typing after the delay; a recognizer that throws is ignored. `recognized` holds the current one. Nothing is preselected.
- **"Can't find it?"** («¿No lo encuentras?», `footer`: nodes, React content): the product's escape ("Choose which repositories Beyond can see on GitHub ↗", through a `ProviderWindow`), shown under the list in every state: results, no match, nothing to choose, failure and the gate.
- **Spanish**: `Picker.labels.en` / `.es` carry every string.

React `Picker` takes `accounts` (with `connect.onSelect`), `gate` (with `action` and `secondary` as React content), `footer` (React content), `recognize`, `onRecognize`, `explain` and `bound`; the ref adds `account` and `recognized`.

**RefChooser** (DOM and React `RefChooser`; since 0.7.0, D56 shared piece 3). A branch or another Git ref, for Conduict's composer and add form, Delegate's deliveries and CDN's git sources. A `ChoiceMenu` with the default branch first and marked "Default", the others in the order given (`refs: [{ name, default?, note? }]`, Projects' `GET /v1/repositories/{rep}/branches`), a search past the threshold that matches the beginning or a segment, type-ahead, and the action **Use a commit or another ref…** («Usar un commit u otra referencia…», `escape`, default true), which opens one field in place with Use and Cancel: Enter uses it, Escape cancels back to the chooser, Git's naming rules (no spaces, no `..`, none of `~ ^ : ? * [ \`, no leading or trailing `/` or `.`, no `.lock` end) and the product's `validate(value)` keep an error beside it. A typed ref is listed first with "Commit or other ref". States: `loading` (a placeholder line and a status, never a made-up list), `unavailable` (`{ text?, retry? }`: "Couldn't read the branches." with Try again, the escape still usable; React `unavailable.onRetry`), empty ("No branches to choose from." and the escape); the `refs` setter (a late or refreshed list) ends both. `layout: 'field'` (default) puts the label above the control, as a form's; `'inline'` names it on the button ("Branch: main"), for a composer's toolbar. One branch and `escape: false` is a statement. `name` submits the value; `onchange(value)` runs for each new choice; `RefChooser.labels.en` / `.es`.

**ProjectPicker** (DOM and React `ProjectPicker`; since 0.7.0, D56 shared piece 2). The family bar's project list as a form control, for Delegate's adopt (CNT-21), Workspace's projects page (CNT-78) and Conduict's project choice (CNT-67): the organization's `projects` (`{ id, name, here? }`, from the descriptor the product relays) in a `ChoiceMenu` ordered by the page language's collation, each with its state here from `ProjectList.state(here)`: `used` shows nothing, `unset` "Not set up in {product}" and can be chosen, `denied` "No access in {product}" and cannot, `only` "Only in {product}". `only: 'unset'` lists only the projects not set up here; more than `ProjectList.threshold` (8) open with the search field; `actions` ("New project…") follow; no project is chosen for the person. An empty catalog says "No projects to choose from."; `ProjectPicker.labels.en` / `.es`.

**SecretField** (DOM and React `SecretField`; since 0.7.0, FR-2, D56 shared piece 4). A secret with the integration first, for CDN's registries, Delegate's Supabase, Workspace's Codex key and Projects' transition token: `connect` (`{ label, run | href }`, "Connect Supabase") is the primary action; "Paste the {credential} instead" is a folded `<details>` beneath it, opening with "Only when connecting isn't possible…" and a password input the browser neither fills nor remembers. A held secret (`stored`) reads "Stored · Replace" and is never shown or prefilled: until Replace there is no enabled input, so a form submits nothing for it and the stored one is kept; "Keep the stored {credential}" goes back. A closed fold submits nothing either. `value` is the pasted text while an input is open, else null; setting `stored` forgets any text. It keeps no draft. `SecretField.labels.en` / `.es`.

**Draft** (DOM and React export `Draft`; since 0.7.0, D56 shared piece 7). A carried draft, so "New …" from a place keeps that place: `new Draft({ environment, provider, agent, from, for }).address(base)` writes the known keys (short identifiers only) into `base`'s search, or into its hash's query for an address routed in its hash (`#/acme/storefront/…?add=…`), replacing those keys and keeping other parameters; `Draft.read(location)` restores them from either, dropping unknown and malformed values; `values.for` falls back to `from` (CNT-12). Values are hints: the page that reads them checks each against what the person may see.

**FocusedForm** (`FocusedForm`; `FocusedForm.labels.en` / `.es` since 0.7.2: "That did not work. Try again." / «No funcionó. Vuelve a intentarlo.»). Submission behavior for a dedicated create or edit form: validates fields (or the browser's constraints), runs `submit` once, marks the form busy and ignores double clicks and repeated Enter, shows a failure as an alert with `explain(error)` and keeps every value. React `FocusedForm` renders a function of `busy`.

## Dialogs and questions

**Dialog** (`Dialog`; React `Dialog` with `open`, `onClose`, `actions`). Modal on native `<dialog>`, named by its title, described by its description. Focus moves to `[data-autofocus]`, the first field or the first action; Tab wraps; closing restores focus to the opener (the focused element, or, where a click does not focus a button as in Safari, the element last pressed; since 0.2.1) or `restore`, never to the page's body. Escape and the close button dismiss unless `escape: false`; the backdrop dismisses only with `backdrop: true`. While `busy` nothing dismisses it, including repeated Escape. Sizes `small`, `medium`, `large`. `Dialog.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.1) carry its close button's name (React `Dialog.labels`). The dialog never grows past the viewport: when the content is taller, the body scrolls on its own while the title, description and footer actions stay in view (at 320 px and 200 % zoom too); only on viewports shorter than 20 rem does the whole dialog scroll. Use it for short focused tasks (LR-07: a decision or a short edit that fits one 390 px screen); long create and edit flows deserve their own navigable screen. The React `Dialog` calls `onClose` only when the person dismisses the dialog it holds or `close(value)` runs inside it: closing it through `open`, a replacement when a shaping prop (`size`, `description`, `escape`, `backdrop`, the close label) changes, unmounting it and React's development double mount report nothing, and a destroyed dialog is never opened.

**SideSheet** (DOM `SideSheet`, React `SideSheet`; since 0.7.0, LR-07, FAM-40). A task or an item opened from the inline end while the page it came from stays in view, such as Projects' add task hosted in Conduict (phase 1.1). A native modal `<dialog>` (the page behind is inert and does not scroll) named by its `title` (or `label`), with an optional `description` line, the body (`body`, `fill`) and a footer of `actions` that stays in view while the body scrolls; as tall as the window, `--layout-form` wide (`width: 'standard'`: `--layout-standard`, leaving the sidebar's width) and the whole width below the family cut (1024 px); the dialog's elevation and scrim; it slides in with `--motion-standard`, at once under reduced motion. Opening (`open({ restore })`, a promise of the value given to `close(value)`) moves focus to `[data-autofocus]` inside it, else to its title; Tab and Shift+Tab stay inside (moved by the sheet itself, so WebKit's Tab never reaches the page behind); closing returns focus to `restore`, else to the element that opened it, else to the constructor's `restore`, never to the body. A task sheet never closes on a press outside. Escape and the close button (an icon button with its tooltip, "Close" / «Cerrar») close it with null, except while `busy`: then nothing closes it, `close()` included. `error(node)` shows a failure at its top (an alert: "Beyond Projects didn't answer" with Try again) without closing it or losing what the person did; `error(null)` clears it. React `SideSheet` takes `open`, `onClose` (only for a dismissal or a `close(value)` inside, as `Dialog`), `busy`, `error` and `actions` (React content), `restore`, `width`. A non-modal sheet beside the main column is not built: on a wide region `ListDetail` keeps an item beside its list. `SideSheet.labels.en` / `.es`.

**confirm, prompt, alert** (DOM functions; React `useConfirm(labels)`). Promise-returning in-app questions: `confirm` resolves true or false, `prompt` the value or null, `alert` once acknowledged. Since 0.7.1 their copy ships in English and Spanish as `Question.labels`, also `confirm.labels`, `prompt.labels` and `alert.labels` (`confirm({ …, labels: confirm.labels.es })`; React `useConfirm(useConfirm.labels.es)`): Cancelar, Confirmar, Aceptar, Cerrar, the failure line, "Lo que se pierde", "Lo que se conserva", "Cómo deshacerlo", and a prompt field's "(opcional)" and "Revisa este valor."; `accept` stays the action's own verb. `tone: 'danger'` focuses the safe choice; `focus: 'cancel' | 'accept'` sets the starting button independently of the tone (since 0.1.3), so a consequential but not destructive step such as a publication starts on Cancel without the danger styling. With `work`, accepting runs the operation busy and non-dismissible; a failure stays in the dialog (`explain`) for retry or cancel. Intended for guards such as Delegate's unsaved-drafts question; they do not replace the browser's own `beforeunload`.

**Consequence first** (decision D17; since 0.2.0). A destructive or irreversible confirmation states its consequence: `confirm({ …, consequence: { lost, kept, recovery } })` shows a short list under the message, each part only when given (text, a node or a list of them), labelled "What is lost", "What is kept" and "How to undo" (`labels.lost`, `labels.kept`, `labels.recovery`), shown as written, in sentence case (upper case by style until 0.2.2). The accept button is always the action's own verb (`accept: 'Delete project'`): "OK" and "Confirm" are wrong for a named action, and the default "Confirm" remains only for compatibility. Since 0.7.2 two more parts, both optional: `affected`, the work it stops or interrupts ("Work it affects" / «Trabajo afectado»: running conversations, deliveries in progress), and `costing`, what is still charged ("What keeps costing" / «Qué sigue costando»); the list reads affected, lost, kept, costing, recovery. A product whose confirmation is a dialog of its own (Conduict's, which also checks a lost answer) draws the same list with `consequence(parts, labels)` (DOM, also exported by `/react`; React `<Consequence parts labels />`), `consequence.labels.en` / `.es`, and returns null when nothing is stated; it replaces a product's local copy.

```js
await confirm({
	title: 'Delete Storefront?',
	message: 'The project is deleted for everyone in Northwind.',
	accept: 'Delete project',
	tone: 'danger',
	consequence: { lost: ['Its entries in each product', 'Its repositories'], kept: 'Each product’s own records', recovery: 'Deletion cannot be undone.' }
});
```

## A session that ended

**Session** (DOM `Session`, React `useSession`; since 0.8.0, the family rule D63 that presents D26, amended on 2026-10-07 so its dialog cannot be dismissed (0.9.0), Beyond Suite's `docs/family/session-renewal.md`). The one answer, in every product, to a session that ends while the person works. A product creates it once its own session read says who is signed in, with:

- `product` and `read()`: the product's read, answering `{ state: 'signed', person, expires? }` or `{ state: 'ended', reason }`; a rejection is an outage, never a sign-out;
- `start(mode)`: the product's hand-off start for `silent` (`prompt=none` in a hidden frame), `window` and `tab`;
- `other` (the bar's "Sign out of Beyond", offered as "Use another account"), `accounts` (Beyond Accounts' account page), `onrenewed` (read the view again, reconnect live transports) and `onchanged`;
- `delegate`, inside a Beyond Desktop window, where the Desktop asks for every kind (expired, revoked, suspended and Accounts not answering, since 0.9.2), so a frame never draws a dialog: `true` once signed in, `false` when the window is closing (the session then ends with nothing shown), and a rejection is asked again by itself after 5, 15, 30 and then every 60 s;
- `signin` (0.8.2), the product's own sign-in behind Continue, for an installed shell that signs in through the system browser.

**The transport.** It sends every `UNAUTHENTICATED`, `SESSION_*` and `ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED` answer to `lost({ reason, replay })`, which resolves with whether to send the request again:

- `read`: once renewed;
- `write`: once renewed while the request waited;
- `false` (destructive or irreversible): never. Show `session.text('unsent')` beside the action.

**What the person sees.**

- **Expired, with a living Beyond session:** nothing. The session renews in a hidden frame.
- **Expired, without one:** one small dialog, "Sign in again to continue", with the person's avatar, name and email, "What you were doing stays here.", **Continue as {name}** (the sign-in in a window that closes itself) and "Use another account".
- **Revoked:** "You were signed out of Beyond" over an opaque backdrop.
- **Suspended:** no sign-in, **Open Beyond Accounts** and "Use another account".
- **Beyond Accounts not answering:** "This page tries again by itself", **Try again**, and retries by itself after 5, 15, 30 and then every 60 s.

**The dialog cannot be dismissed** (0.9.0): no ×, Escape nor press outside, for every kind. Since 0.9.2 it is announced as an alert dialog (`role="alertdialog"`) named by its title and described by its sentence (`aria-describedby`). Its actions are the ways on, and cancelling the sign-in window returns to the dialog. There is no state in which the page is open without a session (`state` is `signed`, `renewing`, `asking` or `ended`), so a request the page makes while the dialog asks waits behind it and is sent by its replay class once renewed. `FamilyBar` has no "Sign in" and the page carries no `data-session` marker.

**Live transports.** `check()` resolves with the state after looking. An event stream or poll that drops calls it first and shows its own "reconnecting" only for `signed`; otherwise the dialog speaks, and `onrenewed` reconnects it.

**Checks and tabs.** It looks at the session when the tab is shown, the network returns, the computer wakes and a minute before a known expiry. A renewal in one tab reaches the others (`beyond-session`). Someone else signing in drops what was held and calls `onchanged`. There is at most one silent attempt per minute, so a loop ends in the dialog.

**The landing.** The product's hand-off ends on a page that runs `@beyond-js/ui/session/landed`. The module has no imports, carries only the outcome, closes the sign-in window, and is a declared side effect since 0.8.1.

`Session.labels.en` / `.es`.

## Help

**Help** (`Help`). Essential help behind a toggle button next to what it explains, named "Help: <topic>". Opens with click, tap, Enter or Space; Escape closes and returns focus; the explanation flows in the layout at any width. `Help.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.2): «Ayuda: {topic}», «Cerrar la ayuda».

**Tooltip** (`Tooltip`). A supplementary description on hover, keyboard focus and a touch press; hoverable, Escape hides it, positioned inside the viewport. Never the only copy of essential information. Since 0.5.0, `when` (a function) is asked each time it would show and it shows only while that returns true: the package uses it to show a cut user-written name whole only while it is cut (D44).

**Hint** (DOM and React export `Hint`, React `useHint`; public since 0.11.1). The family tooltip of glyph-only controls (D11), the one the package's own icon buttons use, for a product's own: `new Hint(root)` over the element that holds them; every control inside with `data-bui-hint` and an `aria-label` (a glyph of the closed list alone) shows its name after a short delay on hover, at once on keyboard focus and for a moment on a touch press, with ` · {data-bui-shortcut}` when the control names a key; a click, leaving, blur and Escape hide it, and it never shows over what an expanded control opened. It repeats the accessible name, so it is hidden from assistive technology and describes nothing. It is drawn in the dialog that holds the root, else in the body. One hint per root (the nearest root wins for nested ones); `destroy()` releases every listener and leaves the root as it was. React: `const ref = useHint()` on the element that holds the controls (StrictMode-safe). Use `Tooltip` for a description that is not a control's name.

## Collections and feedback

**Collection** (`Collection`; React columns may `render` React content). Compact list with search, finite filters, a table whose primary cell links to the detail (`link(row)`, `onopen` for applications that route themselves) and paging by `total` or `more`. States: loading (skeleton), failure with retry, empty with its first action, no matches with "Clear search and filters". `state` (`query`, `filters`, `page`) and `onstate` keep list context in the address across back, forward and reload. A page change moves focus to the new rows. An empty list shows no range (since 0.3.1): its empty state already says why. Rows stack with their column labels below 640 px. **Column priority** (since 0.7.0, LR-03, FAM-40): a column with `priority` (1, 2, 3 …) leaves as the region narrows, the highest number first, instead of the table scrolling; the primary column and a column without a priority always stay. The natural width of each column is measured once per drawing and the fit follows the region's own width on every resize (a `ResizeObserver`); a hidden column leaves every row. "Show 2 more columns" (`aria-expanded`) reveals them, the table then scrolling in its own box, and "Show fewer columns" hides them again; the choice is kept across pages and searches. Stacked rows show every column. `Collection.labels.en` / `.es` (since 0.7.0) carry the collection's copy.

**Status, Badge, Callout, Loading, Skeleton.** `status(label, tone)` is a state with a dot and words; `badge` a short tag; `callout({ tone, title, body, actions, live })` a message block (`live` announces, assertively for danger); `loading(label)` an announced spinner; `skeleton(lines)` hidden placeholders. Tones: `neutral`, `success`, `warning`, `danger`, `info` (and `progress` for status). `loading.labels` (React `Loading.labels`; 0.7.2) holds the default label, "Loading…" / «Cargando…». Since 0.7.6 a loading line can name what it waits for: `loading.text({ name, kind, language })` fills `loading.names` (`loading` "Loading {name}…", `opening` "Opening {name}…", `reading` "Reading {name}…"; «Cargando», «Abriendo», «Leyendo»), where `name` is the thing as the product calls it, article included ("the conversation", «la conversación»); React `Loading.names` and `Loading.text` are the same.

**availability** (DOM and React `availability`; since 0.2.0). **Unavailable** (DOM `Unavailable`, React `Unavailable`; since 0.2.0). "Not available here" (decision D06): a missing permission, admission, association or capability explained in place instead of a refusal card, a raw error envelope or a hidden button. Options: `title` (what is unavailable), `reason` (why), `owner` (who can change it, after "Who can change this: ", `labels.owner`), `action` and `secondary` (nodes in the DOM, React content in React), `kind` (`access` with a lock, `association` with a plug, `capability` with an information mark), `code` (the refusal's code as a neutral tag) and `level` (the heading, 2 to 6, default 2). **StatusRow** (DOM and React `StatusRow`; since 0.7.0, design S3, E54). One thing with one state, such as a GitHub connection, a person's GitHub account, an AI engine or a service connection: a region named by its `title`, with its `kind` beside it, a tone glyph, the state with how fresh it is (`state: { label, tone, checked, connected }` drawn by `Freshness`: "Suspended · Checked 2 min ago", or "Last known: …" with a neutral mark while the source is disconnected), the `reason` in words, "Who can change this: …" (`owner`), a few `facts`, then the state's one `action` and "More actions for {title}" (`more`, an `ActionMenu`). `update(values)` replaces any part. React `StatusRow` takes `action` as React content and `more` with `onSelect`. `StatusRow.labels.en` / `.es` (and `labels.freshness`).

**CopyMessage** (DOM and React `CopyMessage`; since 0.7.0, FR-5, D56 shared piece 9). A prewritten text a person copies to send or run elsewhere: a message for whoever can act ("Could you approve the Beyond app for acme on GitHub? …"), a link for them (`kind: 'link'`) or a command with its values filled (`kind: 'command'`, kept in its lines and scrolling in its own box), read-only and whole, with an optional `label` above and one action, "Copy message", "Copy link" or "Copy command", that says "Copied". The copy is bounded (`CopyMessage.bound`, 5 s, D40): a refused, missing or silent clipboard is said in place and the text is selected for the keyboard. The `text` setter replaces it. `CopyMessage.labels.en` / `.es`.

**ProviderWindow** (DOM and React `ProviderWindow`; since 0.7.0, FR-G2, design S2 2.3 to 2.4). A provider's own window, such as GitHub's installation page or Supabase's authorization: "Continue to {provider}" opens `href` (the product's start address, which holds the attempt on its server) in a window from the press; while it is open, an `Awaited` card "Finish on {provider}" ("Taking longer than usual" past the 90th percentile of `expected`, by default 2 and 10 min) with "Complete the steps in the {provider} window. This page updates by itself.", **Reopen the {provider} window** and **Cancel**. A message `{ type: 'beyond-provider' }` from `origin`, and only from it, or the window closing (polled, the only signal a cross-origin window gives) makes it call the product's `read()`, bounded by `bound` (20 s): `{ state: 'done' }` ends it (`onend('done', answer)`), `waiting` hands the product a state it draws itself (an owner's approval), anything else after the window closed says "The {provider} window closed before you finished. Nothing was connected." with Continue again; a read that fails or does not answer says it is not known yet, with **Check again**. A blocked window offers **Continue in this tab** and **Try the window again**; on a touch screen or inside a frame (`same: 'auto'`) the address opens in this tab and the product calls `check()` when the person comes back. The outcome is always the server's, never the window's. `state` is `idle`, `open`, `blocked`, `checking`, `closed`, `unknown`, `done` or `away`; the React ref exposes `open()`, `check()`, `cancel()` and `state`. `ProviderWindow.labels.en` / `.es` (and `labels.awaited`). Since 0.7.4 the window loses its opener; since 0.7.5 it opens blank on this origin, loses its opener there and then goes to `href`, the attempt is also read every `interval` ms (2500) while the window stays open, for at most `follow` (15 min), one read at a time, a same-origin `BroadcastChannel` (`channel`, 'beyond-provider') wakes it, and the page closes the window only while it is on this origin: a landing on another origin closes itself.

**Toaster** (`Toaster`; React `useToaster`). Transient outcome messages, one region per page: successes and information announced politely and removed after `duration`; failures assertive and kept until dismissed. A toast says what just happened on this screen; it is never stored and is not a notification. `Toaster.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.1; React `useToaster(useToaster.labels.es)`) carry the region's name and Dismiss. An outcome repeats the action's verb in the past tense with its object ("Project deleted", "Proyecto borrado"); the package offers no helper for it, because past tenses are the product's language. Since 0.1.6 the toaster sits `--bui-toaster-bottom` above the viewport's bottom edge (default `--space-4`), so a surface with a bottom bar of its own, such as the Desktop's dock, raises it there.

## Long operations

Since 0.5.0 (decision D50, approved on 2026-10-03, and the suite's long-operations guide). Work that takes minutes is followed, never guessed: these components are drawn from the operation's record (which lives on the server) and a clock, so a reload, another tab or a return an hour later shows the same. "Usually about X" is the **measured median**; "Taking longer than usual" starts **past the 90th percentile** (strictly), not at the median. Times keep current with the clock, never through a live region; a polite live region (`aria-live="polite"`) says only what changed. Nothing moves under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Every component takes `locale` for times of day (24-hour) and `labels`, and carries its English and Spanish copy as `Component.labels.en` / `.es` (frozen; in React too, `Steps.labels.es`), so a product in Spanish passes `labels: Steps.labels.es` instead of writing its own. Durations use the units `second`, `minute`, `hour`, `day` ("{count} s", "{count} min", "{count} h", "{count} d", the same in Spanish).

**Clock** (DOM and React `Clock`). The page's time and beat: `new Clock({ now?, every? })` (`now` returns milliseconds, `Date.now` by default; `every` the beat, 1000 ms). One timer while anything listens (`subscribe(listener)` returns the release), none otherwise; `tick()` re-evaluates every listener at once; `Clock.system` is the shared clock on the system time, the default of every component. Tests and products inject `now`; a product passes one clock to every component of a page. A throwing listener goes to `reportError` and does not stop the others.

**Steps** (DOM `Steps`, React `Steps`). The steps of a long operation. Options: `label` (the list's name, for assistive technology), `steps`, `clock`, `locale`, `labels` (`labels.details` reaches the technical details). Each step: `{ id?, label, state, since?, until?, expected?: { median, p90 }, phase?: { label, since? }, reason?: text | { text, details?: { text, request, time } } }`, with `state` one of `done`, `progress`, `stalled` (it finished its part and something else blocks it), `failed` and `waiting` (not begun; an unknown state reads as waiting). A finished step with both times says "Took {duration}" ("Took 1 min 24 s"); the step in progress says "{elapsed} so far · usually about {median}" (the median rounded to one unit), or "{elapsed} so far" without an expected time, and past its 90th percentile "Taking longer than usual · {elapsed} so far · usually about {median}" (`.bui-step-slow`); its phase reads "Now: {phase} · {elapsed}" ("Last phase: {phase}" on a step that stopped); a stalled or failed step shows its reason with "Technical details" folded under it. Every state is in words: "In progress", "Blocked" and "Failed" as a status beside the name, "Done" and "Waiting" as hidden text; the mark is a check, a pulsing dot, an alert, a cross or the step's number, never the only sign. The step in progress has `aria-current="step"`. The `steps` setter (React prop) redraws; a step whose state changed (by `id`, else by place) is announced ("Startup: Done"), and so is a step that becomes slow, once ("Startup: taking longer than usual"). `tick()` re-evaluates now; `announced` reads the last words said. Since 0.10.0 `announce: false` (React prop `announce`) leaves out the steps' own live region, for a page that says changes through one region of its own (a plan inside a conversation): nothing is announced and `announced` still holds the words, for the page to say. Classes: `bui-steps`, `bui-steps-list`, `bui-step`, `bui-step-{state}` (and `data-state`), `bui-step-mark`, `bui-step-ring`, `bui-step-body`, `bui-step-head`, `bui-step-label`, `bui-step-time`, `bui-step-phase`, `bui-step-reason`, `bui-step-slow`, `bui-announcer`.

Labels: `done` "Done" / «Hecho», `progress` "In progress" / «En curso», `stalled` "Blocked" / «Bloqueado», `failed` "Failed" / «Falló», `waiting` "Waiting" / «En espera», `took` "Took {duration}" / «Tardó {duration}», `running` "{elapsed} so far" / «{elapsed} hasta ahora», `usual` "{elapsed} so far · usually about {expected}" / «{elapsed} hasta ahora · suele tardar unos {expected}», `slow` "Taking longer than usual · {elapsed} so far · usually about {expected}" / «Está tardando más de lo habitual · …», `phase` "Now: {phase} · {elapsed}" / «Ahora: {phase} · {elapsed}», `stopped` "Last phase: {phase}" / «Última fase: {phase}», `change` "{step}: {state}", `late` "{step}: taking longer than usual" / «{step}: está tardando más de lo habitual».

**Awaited** (DOM `Awaited`, React `Awaited`). One card that moves while a person waits (Conduict's E52, generalized): its title says what is happening (`title`, a heading of `level`, 2 by default) and since when ("Since 19:47", or the day and time on another day); the time left of the usual one, "About {duration} left" (whole minutes rounded up) or "Less than a minute left", counted toward the median and, from the median on (state `late`), toward the 90th percentile; past the 90th percentile "Taking longer than usual" with **Check again**; a bar (`<progress>`) toward the usual time that only the end completes (up to 0.9 at the median and 0.95 at the 90th percentile), indeterminate when the time cannot be told (no `since` or `expected`) or has passed, with the time as its `aria-valuetext`; the operation's `steps` inside (a `Steps`); and, once `reason` is given (`{ text, way?, action?: { label, href? | run? }, details? }`), why it cannot continue and the way on in place of the time and the bar, with its action, its technical details and **Check again**. **Check again** runs `check()` once at a time (a second press is ignored; the button shows it is running) and, if the check fails, says "The check did not finish. Try again." in place. `end(outcome)` (`'done'` or `'failed'`; React prop `ended`) completes the bar for `done`, says "Done" or "Did not finish", announces the end and calls `onend(outcome)` (React `onEnd`) once: the hook where a product says "Ready · …" in the tab's title or leaves a notice. `update(values)` (React props) replaces `title`, `since`, `expected`, `steps`, `reason` or `check`; `state` reads `progress`, `late`, `slow`, `stalled`, `done` or `failed` (also `data-state`); `ended` the outcome. It announces becoming slow, a reason and the end. Classes: `bui-awaited`, `bui-awaited-head`, `bui-awaited-mark`, `bui-awaited-heading`, `bui-awaited-title`, `bui-awaited-since`, `bui-awaited-time`, `bui-awaited-bar`, `bui-awaited-body`, `bui-awaited-reason`, `bui-awaited-why`, `bui-awaited-actions`, `bui-awaited-note`. It sits in the page's flow, with no elevation.

**AwaitedLine** (DOM `AwaitedLine`, React `AwaitedLine`; since 0.7.1, design S5). `Awaited` in one line, for a row of a list (a clone, an update): the title, then "40 s so far · usually about 1 min" (the median as "usually about"), past the 90th percentile, strictly, "Taking longer than usual · 12 min so far" with **Check again** (`check`, one run at a time, a failure said in place), with a `reason` why it cannot continue in place of the time, and after `end(outcome)` (React `ended`) "Done · took 52 s" or "Did not finish", calling `onend` (React `onEnd`) once. Without `expected` it says the time so far alone, never a guess. Drawn from the record and the `Clock` only; a polite live region says becoming slow, a reason and the end, never the ticking time. A spinner, a check or an alert mark leads it; it wraps at narrow widths and is never cut. `update`, `tick`, `again`, `state`, `ended`, `announced`, `text`; `AwaitedLine.labels.en` / `.es`.

Labels: `since` "Since {time}" / «Desde {time}», `left` "About {duration} left" / «Queda aproximadamente {duration}», `brief` "Less than a minute left" / «Queda menos de un minuto», `slow` "Taking longer than usual" / «Está tardando más de lo habitual», `unknown` "Time left unknown" / «Tiempo restante desconocido», `progress` "Progress: {title}" / «Progreso: {title}», `check` "Check again" / «Comprobar de nuevo», `unchecked` "The check did not finish. Try again." / «La comprobación no terminó. Vuelve a intentarlo.», `done` "Done" / «Listo», `failed` "Did not finish" / «No terminó», and the announcements `ended` "{title}: done", `stopped` "{title}: did not finish", `late` "{title}: taking longer than usual", `blocked` "{title}: {reason}"; `labels.steps` and `labels.details` reach the parts.

**Freshness** (DOM `Freshness`, React `Freshness`). A state with how fresh it is: the state's word with its dot (`label`, `tone`: a status tone, or an `availability` entry's `label` and `tone`) and "Checked 2 min ago" (`checked`; "Checked just now" under `Freshness.recent`, 5 s), kept current by the clock. With `connected: false` it reads "Last known: Running · 10:42" (or "Last known: Running" without `checked`) with a neutral dot and muted words, so a lost live channel never reads as the work having stopped. The words always carry the meaning. `update(values)` (React props), `stale`, `text`. Labels: `checked` "Checked {duration} ago" / «Comprobado hace {duration}», `now` "Checked just now" / «Comprobado ahora mismo», `known` "Last known: {state} · {time}" / «Último estado conocido: {state} · {time}», `last` "Last known: {state}" / «Último estado conocido: {state}». Classes: `bui-freshness`, `bui-freshness-state`, `bui-freshness-age`, `bui-freshness-stale`.

**TechnicalDetails** (DOM `TechnicalDetails`, React `TechnicalDetails`; D43 as amended on 2026-10-03). A closed native disclosure, "Technical details", holding the source's own words (`text`, preformatted), the request identifier (`request`) and the time (`time`, shown in the person's language), with **Copy details**, which copies all of it for support: the words, then "Request: …" and "Time: …" in ISO 8601 (`report`). It copies through the Clipboard API under `TechnicalDetails.bound` (5 s); copied, it says "Copied"; refused (no API, no permission or no answer), it says so in place and selects the text for the keyboard. Both are said in a `role="status"` line. `update(values)`, `open`, `copy()` (resolves whether it copied). Labels: `summary` "Technical details" / «Detalles técnicos», `request` "Request" / «Solicitud», `time` "Time" / «Hora», `copy` "Copy details" / «Copiar detalles», `copied` "Copied" / «Copiado», `refused` "Could not copy. The details are selected: copy them with your keyboard." / «No se pudo copiar. Los detalles están seleccionados: cópialos con el teclado.». Classes: `bui-details`, `bui-details-summary`, `bui-details-body`, `bui-details-content`, `bui-details-text`, `bui-details-facts`, `bui-details-actions`, `bui-details-result`, `bui-details-refused`.

```js
import { Awaited, Clock, Steps } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const clock = new Clock();                         // one per page
const card = new Awaited({
	title: 'Starting My first VM', since: record.started, expected: { median: 120_000, p90: 240_000 },
	steps: record.steps, check: () => relay.refresh(), clock,
	onend: outcome => (document.title = `${outcome === 'done' ? 'Ready' : 'Not ready'} · My first VM`),
	labels: { ...Awaited.labels.es, steps: Steps.labels.es }
}).mount(main);
relay.onchange = record => (record.ended ? card.end(record.outcome) : card.update({ steps: record.steps, reason: record.reason }));
```

The copy adds `searching`, `none`, `unavailable`, `retry`, `results`, `all` and `count` (a function of `{ count }`) in English and Spanish: «Buscando…», «Nada coincide con «{query}».», «La búsqueda no respondió, así que ahora no se pueden mostrar resultados.», «Reintentar», «Resultados», «Ver todos los resultados», «{count} resultados». **ProductNav** (DOM `ProductNav`, React `ProductNav`; since 0.2.0). A product's own navigation as a light row of tabs under the family bar: `items` (`{ label, href, current }`), `label` (the navigation's name, default `labels.nav` "Product"), `onnavigate` (React `onNavigate`) for plain clicks, `sticky` to stay under the bar (not sticky by default). The row scrolls sideways when narrow and keeps the current tab (`aria-current="page"`) in view, whole pixels inside its padding (WebKit keeps only whole pixels of a scroll position); focus rings are drawn inside the tabs. `ProductNav.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.2).

**Header** (`Header`). The shared header primitive (signed-in products render `FamilyBar`, which is built on it): `brand` (link home: text, the family lockup with `lockup`, a `logo` node or an `image`; with a picture, `label` is the link's one accessible name and the picture is not read, since 0.1.7), `context` (breadcrumb entries), `nav` (product links), `notifications` and `account` slots, `onnavigate` for routed applications. Below 768 px the navigation collapses behind a toggle; with `toggle: { controls, expanded, onchange }` the same button opens a product's own sidebar instead. It places content and decides nothing about access. `Header.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.2).

**NotificationEntry** (`NotificationEntry`). The header bell with the unread count and a panel of recent items. Options: `adapter`, `href` (the full inbox), `onopen(destination)`, `onview`, `products` (display names), `locale`, `limit`, `interval`. The count is hidden while unknown, after a failure and while unavailable. A summary with `more: true` counted up to its bound, so the count reads "N+" for any N ("12+") and the bell's name says "12 or more unread"; without `more`, a count past 99 reads "99+". The panel sets its own text roles (since 0.4.0): its title (16 px, weight 500) outranks the items (body size) whatever the product's base stylesheet, and an empty inbox is one quiet line of body text. A quick answer replaces the panel's content directly; the loading indicator appears only after `NotificationEntry.delay` (250 ms; the fixture relay answers in about 80 ms) and goes with the answer; the body is `aria-busy` while loading, its height change is animated with `--motion-standard` except under reduced motion, and an answer that arrives after the panel closed is discarded. The panel states loading, empty (without "View all"), failure with retry, unavailable with "Try again" and no "View all" (for example a product running without Projects) and partial results naming the unreachable products by their display names from `products` (the id only when no name is given); it offers "Mark as read", "Mark all as read" (bounded by when the list loaded) and "View all". "View all" (a plain primary click) closes the panel and returns focus to the bell, then either follows `href` or, with `onview`, hands over to the product; a click with a modifier key opens elsewhere and leaves the panel. Opening an item asks the adapter, which rechecks access; an item that is gone is removed with a message. Item text is forgotten when the panel closes. Closing it from code (`close()`) returns focus to the bell when focus was inside the panel. Read-only `count`, `more` (the count stopped at the bound) and `missing` (product ids the last summary named as unreachable, which also sets `data-state="partial"`). `NotificationEntry.labels.en` / `.es` and `NotificationInbox.labels` (the same sets, 0.7.2) carry every string of the bell, the panel and the inbox in English and Spanish.

React `NotificationEntry` takes `onOpen`, `onView` and the same options as props. Its ref (`NotificationEntryHandle`) exposes `refresh()`, `open()`, `close()` and the read-only `count`, `more` and `expanded`, so a product closes the panel itself, for example on a route change.

**NotificationInbox** (`NotificationInbox`). The full inbox: unread or all, product filter, items grouped by `group` with their earlier updates behind a toggle, "Load more" by cursor, mark read and unread, mark all read for the filter shown, open. States as the entry. `state` and `onstate` keep the filter in the address.

**Adapter.** Both take `{ summary(), list({ state, product, cursor, limit }), read(ids | { before, product }), unread(ids), open(id) }`, shaped after `beyond-notifications/1` as the product's relay answers: `summary` → `{ unread, more?, sources?, unavailable?, available? }`; `list` → `{ items, next, sources?, unavailable?, available? }` with items `{ id, product, title, summary?, occurred, read, group? }`; `open` → `{ destination }` or a rejection with `code: 'NOT_FOUND'`. `available: false` means the aggregation is absent or unreachable. Beyond Projects answers `{ unread (0–99), more, sources: [{ product, state }] }`: `more: true` means its count stopped at the scan bound, and a source with `state: 'unavailable'` is a product whose items are hidden because it did not answer (the partial signal, in a summary and in a list page alike). A product relay may instead name those products in `unavailable` (ids); both shapes are read, together if both are present. Marking read never performs a business action.

**Notification copy.** Both take `labels` (EN defaults; products pass ES or any language). Entries added or changed in 0.1.1: `button({ count, more })`, the bell's accessible name (`more` is true for a bounded count); `badge({ count, more })`, the visible count (default "N+" with `more`, "99+" past 99 without it); `partial({ products })`, where `products` is now the display names joined by ", ". Since 0.7.6 an unavailable aggregation says why when the relay's `{ available: false, reason }` names one of `beyond-notifications/1`'s codes: `cause` ("Notifications are unavailable right now because {reason}. Everything else works.") with the reason's own entry, `not_configured`, `not_platform_session`, `session_rejected`, `projects_unavailable` or `accounts_unavailable`, in English and Spanish. Any other reason, or none, keeps the plain `unavailable` sentence. Since 0.9.1 `session_rejected` speaks of access ("Beyond Projects could not confirm your access to them yet"): only `Session`'s dialog speaks of the session, and a product passes this reason only after `session.check()` answered `signed`.

## Pages

Since 0.6.0 (decision D52, settled contract S23, approved on 2026-10-04: full width for the frame and collections, readable widths for text and forms; LR-01 to LR-09 and LR-11 of the family's layout rules; D54 for the arrival line and the preferences dialog). Every signed-in page is the family bar, the product's navigation (the shared `Sidebar`, D53) and one **Page**: the region beside the navigation, edge to edge, whose content starts one gutter after it, never centered. Moving between products keeps the bar, the navigation's position, the gutter, the header, the width tiers, the section style and the control sizes (LR-11). The numeric values are engineering defaults, to be measured.

- **Page** (DOM `Page`, React `Page`). Place it in the shell's main element, after the `Sidebar` (or under `ProductNav`). Options: `template` (`overview`, `list`, `detail`, `settings`, `task`, `tool`; `data-template`, default `detail`; `tool` has full-bleed panes and no gutter, the recorded departure of Workspace's editor and Conduict's terminal), `width` (the main column's tier: `fluid` for collections and tools up to `--layout-fluid-max`, `standard` for a main column with its aside up to `--layout-standard`, `form` for inputs, settings and tasks at `--layout-form`, `reading` for prose at `--layout-measure`, and since 0.11.0 `thread` for a conversation's thread at `--layout-thread`, 52rem, the family proposal D67 adopted by Conduict and not an approved family rule; default `standard`), `arrival` (the `Arrival` line, first), `header` (the `PageHeader`), `children` (the main column), `aside` (a resource page's side panel; no panel without it) and `label` (the panel's accessible name). Setters `main`, `aside` and `width` redraw; `region` is the main column's element.
- **The gutter** (`--layout-gutter`) is decided by the region's own width through a container query, never by the window's: 16 px under 640 px (`--layout-gutter`), 24 px up to 1023 px (`--layout-gutter-medium`), 32 px from 1024 px (`--layout-gutter-wide`). Products with the same navigation therefore start their content at the same place at every width (LR-01, LR-09). Unused width falls only on the far side.
- **The side panel** moves beside the main column once the region holds a form-width column and the panel (a 68rem region, about a 1,300 px window with the sidebar), at `--layout-aside`; below that it follows the main column (LR-03).
- **A panel kept in view** (`panel`, since 0.10.0; the conversation's context panel, design 2.2 and 2.8). With `panel: { cut?, open?, onchange?, title?, labels? }` the aside is a `PagePanel` (`page.panel`): beside the main column from `cut` (the region's own width: CSS pixels or a `rem`/`px` length, `'68rem'` by default; Conduict uses `'73rem'`), `--layout-aside` wide, sticky under the family bar (`--bui-page-sticky` moves its top) with a scroll of its own; the person may hide it there (`close()`, `toggle()`), and `onchange(shown)` reports each choice for the product to keep on the device and give back as `open`. Below the cut it is out of the page's flow and `page.panel.open()` shows the same content in a `SideSheet` (titled `title`, else the panel's `label`; modal, focus in and back to the toggle that was pressed, else the panel's first toggle, also where a click does not focus a button, as in WebKit; `open(from)` and `toggle(from)` name another). The region's width is observed, and a change is decided in the next frame, never inside the observer's callback. A page made outside the document can only guess from the window's width: `mount()` decides as it places the page, and a page a product places itself (`append(page.element)`) in the task that made it is decided in the next frame, before it is first drawn; one placed later calls `page.panel.measure()` once placed. `page.panel.control(button)` makes a button the panel's toggle ("Details"): a press shows or hides it, or opens its sheet, and the button's `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` follow; it returns the release. Crossing the cut while the sheet is open closes it and shows the panel beside, focus included. `mode` reads `beside` or `sheet` (also `data-panel` on the page), `shown` the kept choice (setting it reports nothing), `expanded` whether it is in view now. Without `panel` the aside flows exactly as before. React: `<Page panel={{ cut, open, onChange, title, labels, head, wide }} panelRef={ref} aside={…}>` with `<PanelToggle label="Details" />` anywhere inside the page (such as the header's actions). Classes: `bui-page-panel`, `bui-page-slot`.
- **The panel's head, width and wide form** (since 0.11.0; Conduict's CB-07 and CB-Q4, proposal D67). `panel.head: true` gives the panel a head of its own beside the main column: its `title` as an H2 and a hide control, the `close` glyph alone (D11's closed list) named "Hide {title}" («Ocultar {title}», `PagePanel.labels`, or `labels.hide`) with its tooltip; hiding moves focus to the panel's first toggle, else to the page's H1, and reports `onchange(false)` as `close()` does. In its sheet the sheet's own head and Close stand for it. `page.panel.title = …` renames the head. Beside the main column its width is fluid: from `--layout-aside` (22rem) up to `--layout-aside-max` (30rem), taking what a capped main column leaves, so on a wide screen the extra width goes to the panel instead of a band between the thread and the panel (in the `thread` tier the thread keeps its 52rem first and the body stops at the thread, the gap and the panel's maximum; any width beyond falls on the far side). `panel.wide` (or `page.panel.wide = true`, React `panel.wide`) is the wide form a product toggles, such as while the panel shows a review of changes: the panel grows to `--layout-aside-wide` (48rem) and the main column gives up to the form tier (40rem); `data-panel-wide` marks the page. The panel is still decided before its first frame (0.10.0).
- **Reading width.** The package's own texts stop at `--layout-measure` (54ch since token set 0.3.1, measured so no line of prose passes 80 characters, LR-02; 68ch before): a callout's text, an empty state's body, a section's description, the header's facts, and since 0.7.8 every part that holds a product's sentences: a field's or a statement's hint, a choice's description and reason, `Unavailable`'s reason, the picker's status, a notice's summary, `Awaited`'s and a step's reason, technical details' text, a consequence, a question's message and the rest listed in `src/styles/prose.css`. `.bui-reading` gives a product's own paragraph the same measure and `.bui-form-width` the form tier. Since 0.6.2 every block of the main column, the side panel and a section's body may shrink below its content, so a wide table scrolls in its own box instead of widening the page.
- **PageHeader** (DOM `PageHeader`, React `PageHeader`). The page's one header (LR-05): `crumbs` (`[{ label, href? }]`, only from the second level down), `title` (the H1, naming what is in view: never the organization when a project or one of its resources is in view), `status` (one, right after the H1, D42), `facts` (one muted line), `actions` (the title line's end: one primary button, then an `ActionMenu`; centred on the H1's line), `tabs` (a `Tabs` below). Under a 640 px region the status and the actions follow the title. The H1 has `tabindex="-1"`: `focus()` (React `headingRef`) moves focus to it after a navigation. Setters redraw; empty parts take no room. Never in it: preferences, product switching, organization or project selectors (D46), return buttons. Since 0.6.1 its tabs scroll inside their row and never widen the page at 320 px. Since 0.6.4 the `<header>` takes no accessible name: inside `<main>` it has the generic role, which takes none (ARIA 1.2), and the H1 names the page; place the `Page` inside the shell's `<main>`, never beside it, or the header becomes a second banner. Label: `crumbs` "Breadcrumb". `PageHeader.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.2): the crumbs' name, «Ruta de navegación». Since 0.7.7 a crumb's link is a 24 px target (WCAG 2.5.8) on a line of unchanged height: its block padding is given back by an equal negative margin; since 0.7.9 it is about 26 px, so no rounding takes it under 24.
- **A compact header** (`compact`, since 0.11.0; Conduict's header on long threads). `compact: true` keeps the title and the one status in view on a long page: once the title's line has scrolled out under the family bar (and under the sidebar's product row below its cut), one sticky line, `--bui-page-compact` (3rem) tall, shows the title on one line (cut only while a tooltip shows it whole on hover, D44) and the status, and `compact: { actions: [nodes] }` adds the product's own actions at its end (React `compact={{ actions: <…/> }}`; the product's own controls, such as a second Create pull request, not the header's). The line's holder has no height and sits in the page's frame right before the header (`header.bar`: `Page` places it; a product that places the header itself places `bar` before it), so showing it shifts nothing; it fades in with `--motion-quick` and without motion under reduced motion; a panel kept in view sits under it. Its title and status are hidden from assistive technology, since the header says them. It is measured on scroll and resize at most once per animation frame (`header.compacted`, `header.measure()`); `destroy()` releases its listeners. Classes: `bui-page-compact` (`data-shown`), `bui-page-compact-line`, `bui-page-compact-title`, `bui-page-compact-status`, `bui-page-compact-actions`.
- **Tabs** (DOM `Tabs`, React `Tabs`). A resource's areas as links in the address with `aria-current="page"`, drawn like `ProductNav` and scrolling sideways with the current one in view. Options: `items`, `label` (default "Sections of this page"), `name` (0.7.6: what the tabs belong to, giving "Sections of {name}" when no `label` is given), `onnavigate`. `Tabs.labels.en` / `.es` (0.7.2): «Secciones de esta página»; `named` (0.7.6): «Secciones de {name}».
- **Section** (DOM `Section`, React `Section`). A flat section (LR-06, D10): `title` (an H2, or H3 with `level: 3`), one `description` line at the reading width, `actions` at the end of the heading's line and its content. Sections are set apart by space and a 1 px divider; never put a bordered box inside another.
- **ListDetail** (DOM `ListDetail`, React `ListDetail`; since 0.7.0, LR-03, FAM-40). List and detail: the open item (`detail`) sits beside the `list` once the region is `ListDetail.cut` (72rem) wide, decided by the region's own width, and takes the region's whole width, as its own page, on a narrower one, starting with its way back (`back: { label, href, onnavigate? }`, a link to the list's own address; not drawn while the list is in view). Without a detail the list takes the region. The product keeps the open item in its address (`&repository=rep_…`) and sets `detail` from it, so a reload and the history show the same; `focus()` moves focus to the detail. React renders the same markup.
- **Arrival** (DOM `Arrival`, React `Arrival`; D54, which merges D05 and FR-1). The first line of a page opened from another product: "Opened from {product} · Back to {product}" («Abierto desde {product} · Volver a {product}», `Arrival.labels.en` / `.es`). `href` reopens the exact place the person came from, built from the product's own configuration; it is also the way to cancel a task, and on a task's success the product returns there by itself. `onnavigate(item, event)` takes over a plain same-origin click; `ondismiss` offers Dismiss (an icon button with its tooltip). It is the only way back a page draws: never a button among its controls, never in the family bar. Since 0.7.7 "Back to {product}" is a 24 px target on a line of unchanged height, as a crumb is.

```js
import { Page, PageHeader, Tabs, Section, Arrival, Button, status } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const header = new PageHeader({ title: environment.name, crumbs: [{ label: 'Environments', href: '#/environments' }], status: status('Ready', 'success'), facts: 'Compute Engine · us-east4', actions: [new Button({ label: 'Start a conversation', variant: 'primary' })], tabs: new Tabs({ items }) });
const page = new Page({ template: 'detail', width: 'standard', arrival: from ? new Arrival({ product: 'Conduict', href: back }) : null, header, children: [new Section({ title: 'Repositories', children: [list] }).element], aside: [facts] }).mount(main);
header.focus(); // after a navigation
```

## Resource panels

Since 0.11.0 (Conduict's conversation benchmark, CB-01, CB-04, CB-05, B-06: a panel of label and value rows with its numbers and states, and uses against a limit said honestly). Both are flat: no card and no elevation (D10, D52, D53); put them in a `PagePanel`, a `Section` or a side sheet.

**Facts** (DOM `Facts`, React `Facts`). Label and value rows for a resource's panel, a description list (`dl` of rows, each a `dt` and its `dd`). Options: `head` (`{ title?, value?, state?, level? }`: the title as a heading of `level`, 3 by default, which names the rows; one summary value such as "3 files · +52 −3"; one state in words with its tone, `[label, tone]` or `{ label, tone }`, a `status` at the head's end; a state without words is none), `rows` (`[{ key?, label, value, mono?, action?, stale? }]`), `label` (the rows' accessible name when the head has no title) and `labels`. A row's value sits at its end; `mono` sets it in the monospaced face for identifiers, commands, paths and models; `action` is the row's own control (a node or a component, such as a quiet Copy button) after the value, at the row's end on the value's first line, its words on the value's baseline beside the label and under it (since 0.11.1: a long value wraps beside the action instead of pushing it to a line of its own, and its target grows around its words); `stale` marks a value that is not current: muted, followed by its note (`true` says "Not current" / «No actualizado», a string is the product's note, "Not reported since 23:10"), read with the value. When the container is narrower than 16rem (a container query, never the window) each value wraps under its label. Setters `head` and `rows`; rows are patched by `key` (else their label): a row that stays keeps its element, and an action already in place is never moved, so a focused Copy keeps its focus through a live change. Classes: `bui-facts`, `bui-facts-head`, `bui-facts-title`, `bui-facts-summary`, `bui-facts-state`, `bui-facts-list`, `bui-facts-row` (`data-stale`), `bui-facts-label`, `bui-facts-data`, `bui-facts-value` (`data-mono`), `bui-facts-note`, `bui-facts-action`. React renders the same markup, with `action` as React content.

**Meter** (DOM `Meter`, React `Meter`). A use against a limit, such as an AI engine's usage window, a budget or a quota: `label`, `value` (the share used, 0 to 1; more is past the limit; null is "Not reported"), `reset` (when it resets: the product's words, or a moment, said "Resets {time}" on the page's `Clock`), `stale` (not current: a moment, the product's time words, or `true`; "Not reported since {time}", "Not reported lately") and `thresholds` (`{ warning, danger }`, 0.8 and 0.95 by default; outside 0 to 1, or a warning past the danger, throws a `RangeError`). Its level is said in words past the thresholds, never by color alone: "Near the limit" (`warning`), "Almost at the limit" (`danger`), "Limit reached" (`full`, at 1 or more), none below. A `reset` moment that has passed is said by itself, "Reset since {time} · use not reported since", and makes the meter stale: the use after a reset is unknown until reported again (CB-04); while a reset is ahead it listens to the clock and releases it after, and on `destroy()`. Stale, the track and the value are muted. The track is `role="meter"`, named by the label, with `aria-valuemin` 0, `aria-valuemax` 100, `aria-valuenow` in whole percent and an `aria-valuetext` that says the use, the level and the note ("86% used · Near the limit · Resets 23:10"); the fill moves with `--motion-standard`, not at all under reduced motion. `update(values)` changes what is given; `level` (`ok`, `warning`, `danger`, `full`, `unknown`), `stale` and `track` read it; `clock` and `locale` (the percent and times) are options. Copy (`Meter.labels.en` / `.es`): `used` "{percent} used", `warning`, `danger`, `full`, `unknown` "Not reported", `reset` "Resets {time}", `stale` "Not reported since {time}", `unreported`, `passed`. Classes: `bui-meter` (`data-level`, `data-stale`), `bui-meter-head`, `bui-meter-label`, `bui-meter-value`, `bui-meter-word`, `bui-meter-track`, `bui-meter-fill` (`--bui-meter`), `bui-meter-note`. React `<Meter label value reset stale thresholds clock locale labels />` drives the DOM class.

```js
import { Facts, Meter, Button } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const changes = new Facts({ head: { title: 'Changes', value: '3 files · +52 −3', state: ['Not pushed', 'warning'] }, rows: [{ key: 'branch', label: 'Branch', value: branch, mono: true, action: new Button({ label: 'Copy', variant: 'quiet', onclick: copy }) }, { key: 'uncommitted', label: 'Not committed', value: '1 file' }] });
const usage = new Meter({ label: '5-hour window', value: 0.86, reset: limits.resets });
usage.update({ stale: limits.reported }); // a report that is not current
```

## Conversations

Since 0.10.0 (the conversation experience's component plan: a minimal message box, text that streams, activity normalized across agents). The pieces are generic: the product brings its words, its kinds of work and its renderer. Conduict adopts them first (its conversation, new conversation and Desktop window); a conversation's `Thread` (the ordered list, the sticky dock, follow-the-end) stays a product composition for now.

**Composer** (DOM `Composer`, React `Composer`). A message box, the same wherever a person writes to an agent: an optional **state line** (`status`: one sentence, text or a node with its action, only when it changes what sending does; it describes the field), a **field** of one line when empty that grows with its text to twelve lines and then scrolls (`min` 1, `max` 12; immediate, never animated), named by `label` with no visible label and a `placeholder` that says what sending will do, and a **toolbar**: the product's `tools` at its start (context choices on a new conversation, such as `ChoiceMenu`s and a `RefChooser` with `layout: 'inline'`), then its `extras`, the reason sending is unavailable, the `stop` action and the primary action. Options: `label`, `onsubmit({ text, action })` (required; returns a promise), `placeholder`, `status`, `tools`, `extras`, `actions` (`[{ id, label, primary? }]`: the first marked primary, else the first, is the button; the others are a split menu, the `chevron` alone named "More ways to send" with its tooltip; "Send" by default), `stop` (`{ label, run, busy? }`, a labelled button with the `stop` glyph, busy while `run` works or while `busy` says so; null removes it, and focus on it then goes to the field), `disabled` (`{ reason }`: the primary action stays reachable with `aria-disabled` and the reason said beside it in words, D44, also describing the field; the menu's items are disabled with it), `busy` (the product's own work), `submit` (`'enter'`, the default, or `'mod'`), `value`, `name`, `onchange(text)`, `explain(error)` and `labels`.

- **Keys** (the default `submit: 'enter'` is the conversation design's proposal Q-C, which awaits the owner's decision as the family's rule): on a hardware keyboard Enter sends and Shift+Enter adds a line; ⌘+Enter (Ctrl+Enter elsewhere) also sends; on a touch screen (no hover and a coarse pointer) Enter adds a line and the button sends; with `'mod'` Enter always adds a line. While an input method composes a character, Enter never sends (`isComposing`, and Safari's key code 229 for the Enter that ends a composition). Escape is left to menus: it never clears, sends or interrupts. A visually hidden hint with the field says the keys ("Enter sends. Shift+Enter adds a line.").
- **Sending** is one path for the keys, the button and the menu: the text, trimmed, leaves the field at once (`onchange('')`) and `onsubmit({ text, action })` runs with the action's id; one send at a time, and none while `busy` or `disabled` (`sending` reads it; the button shows "Send…" with a spinner). When the promise rejects, the text comes back (before anything typed meanwhile) and a line under the field says "Not sent. Your message is still here." (`role="alert"`), or `explain(error)`'s words, or nothing when `explain` answers null (another voice speaks, such as `Session`'s dialog). An empty Enter does nothing; an empty press says "Write a message first." Focus stays in the field after sending.
- **Setters and methods**: `value` (without `onchange`), `placeholder`, `status`, `tools`, `extras`, `actions`, `stop`, `disabled`, `busy`; `submit(action?)` sends as a press does and resolves whether it was sent; `focus()`; `fit()` fits the field's height to its text (`mount` does it; a product that places the element itself calls it); `field` is the textarea.
- **Layout**: the box takes `--color-border-control` and draws the focus ring around the whole message. - **Suggestions** (since 0.11.0; B-02). `onsuggest(query, signal)` lists suggestions after a trigger character (`suggest.trigger`, `@` by default, at the start or after a space): it answers items `{ value, label?, detail?, mono? }` (an array or `{ items }`). **LiveText** (DOM `LiveText`, React `LiveText`). Text that arrives in pieces, such as an agent's answer while it is written. `append(piece)` adds to the end and `set(text)` replaces the whole draft (a snapshot after a reconnect); either draws at most once per animation frame, through the product's `render(text)` (a Node or a string: a Markdown renderer that builds text nodes) or as plain text with its line breaks; a hidden tab draws nothing until it is shown again, then the latest text once. It keeps at most `bound` characters (64 000; past it the pieces are dropped and `truncated` is true). While live it is `aria-busy` and ends with a **live mark**: a word for assistive technology ("Writing…" / «Escribiendo…», read in place, never announced) and a small dot that pulses, still under reduced motion, placed at the end of the last paragraph or list item. It has no live region: the page's one region says that work started or ended. `settle(text?)` draws the final text at once without the mark; `abandon(note?)` stops with the text so far, muted, and a note ("Unfinished · not kept" / «Sin terminar · no se guardó», `labels.abandoned`); a completed text that arrives later still settles an abandoned one. Pieces after either are ignored. `text`, `state` (`live`, `settled`, `abandoned`), `truncated`. Paragraphs, lists and headings keep the reading measure; code and tables in the product's rendering may use the column. Classes: `bui-live` (`data-state`), `bui-live-body` (`data-plain` for plain text), `bui-live-mark`, `bui-live-note`. React: `<LiveText text state note render bound labels ref />`, where `text` grows by appending (any other text replaces it), `state` settles or abandons, `render(text)` returns React content committed at most once per frame, and the ref has `append`, `set`, `settle` and `abandon` for a product that streams without rendering per piece.

**ActivityRow** (DOM `ActivityRow`, React `ActivityRow`). One step of work in a feed, such as a coding agent's tool call: a disclosure row with the kind's `glyph` (a catalog name: `file`, `terminal`, `search`, `code`, `plus`, `minus`, `globe`, `plug`, `people`, `box`…), a `title` in the product's words ("Ran npm test", "Read src/a.js"), its `meta` in words ("exit 1", "+12 −3") and its `state`: `running` (a spinner in place of the glyph, still under reduced motion), `done`, `failed`, `denied` or `waiting` (anything else reads as `waiting`), said in words: "Running", "Failed", "Denied", "Waiting" with a dot of their tone, and "Done" for assistive technology only. With the times (`since`, `until`, or `duration` in milliseconds) it says how long it took, or, running, how long it has run on the page's `Clock` (never announced; under a second says nothing). The title takes two lines, then is cut; while cut a tooltip shows it whole on hover and focus and the opened body starts with it whole (D44). `tail` shows the last lines (6) of a running step's output under the row, muted; the product clears it when the step ends. `body` (a function, or the parts themselves) is built on first open: nodes, components, sentences or **section records** `{ label, text, code?, lines?, copy? }` ("Input", "Output", "Diff": text in a code box using the column's width and scrolling sideways in its own box, the first `lines` (12) with "Show all {count} lines" (`aria-expanded`) and "Show fewer lines", and "Copy" under a 5 s bound, saying "Copied" or that the browser refused with the text selected) or `{ label, content }` for the product's own node. Without a body the row is plain text. The head is a button with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` (Enter and Space toggle it); closing with focus inside returns it to the head. `update(values)` changes only what is given (`glyph`, `title`, `meta`, `state`, `since`, `until`, `duration`, `tail`, `body`): a live change keeps the row open or closed and keeps focus; a new `body` is built again at once when open, at the next opening otherwise. `open()`, `close()`, `toggle()`, `expanded`, `state`, `body` (the body's element), `watch(listener)` (hears every update; returns the release). Classes: `bui-activity` (`data-state`), `bui-activity-head`, `bui-activity-mark`, `bui-activity-text`, `bui-activity-title`, `bui-activity-meta`, `bui-activity-time`, `bui-activity-word`, `bui-activity-chevron`, `bui-activity-tail`, `bui-activity-body`, `bui-activity-whole`, `bui-activity-content`, `bui-activity-section`, `bui-activity-label`, `bui-activity-code`, `bui-activity-note`, `bui-activity-actions`, `bui-activity-result`.

**ActivityGroup** (DOM `ActivityGroup`, React `ActivityGroup`). Consecutive rows folded into one ("Read 3 files"): a disclosure row with its `glyph`, its `title` (text, or a function of the number of rows so the product writes its own plural) and `meta`, showing the state that matters most among its rows (running, then failed, denied, waiting, done); opened, it lists the rows, each still a row of its own. `rows` (set: rows that stay keep their place and focus, rows that leave are destroyed), `add(row)`, `remove(row)` (taken out, not destroyed), `update({ glyph, title, meta })`, `open()`, `close()`, `toggle()`, `state`, `expanded`; destroying the group destroys the rows it holds. Which rows fold together is the product's choice. Class `bui-activity-group`, its list `bui-activity-list` of `bui-activity-item`s.

Activity copy (`ActivityRow.labels.en` / `.es`, shared by `ActivityGroup.labels`): `running` "Running" / «En curso», `done` "Done" / «Hecho», `failed` "Failed" / «Falló», `denied` "Denied" / «Denegado», `waiting` "Waiting" / «En espera», `elapsed` and `took` "{duration}", the units `second` … `day` ("{count} s"), `more` "Show all {count} lines" / «Mostrar las {count} líneas», `less` "Show fewer lines" / «Mostrar menos líneas», `copy` "Copy" / «Copiar», `copied` "Copied" / «Copiado», `refused` "Could not copy. The text is selected: copy it with your keyboard." / «No se pudo copiar. El texto está seleccionado: cópialo con el teclado.».

React: `<ActivityRow glyph title meta state since until duration tail body open clock locale labels />`, where `title` and `meta` are text and `body` is a function returning React content (called once the row is first opened) or section records; values are applied when they change and `open` opens or closes it. `<ActivityGroup glyph title meta rows open clock locale labels />`, where `rows` are row values (`key`, `glyph`, `title`, `meta`, `state`, the times, `tail`, and `body` as section records), patched by key.

import { Composer, LiveText, ActivityRow, ActivityGroup } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const composer = new Composer({ label: 'Message to the agent', placeholder: 'Queue a message…', actions: [{ id: 'queue', label: 'Queue' }], stop: { label: 'Interrupt', run: () => api.interrupt() }, onsubmit: ({ text, action }) => api.send(text, action) }).mount(dock);

## Icons

**Icon catalog** (DOM `icon`, `icons`, `unlabeled`; React `Icon`, `icons`, `unlabeled`; since 0.3.0, decision D11). The family's one outline set: 56 glyphs on a 24 px grid drawn with one 1.8 px round stroke in the current text color (`src/dom/core/glyphs.js`). Both render the same `<svg class="bui-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" data-icon="…" data-size="…" width height focusable="false">`: without `label` it carries `aria-hidden="true"`, with one `role="img"` and `aria-label`. `.bui-icon[data-size]` in the stylesheet draws it at its size; the stroke scales with it. **A visible label, except for a closed list.** An icon comes with a visible label. Only these glyphs may stand alone on a control, which then carries an accessible name and a tooltip that shows the name, and its shortcut when there is one (`Tooltip`): `close`, `menu`, `more`, `search`, `bell` (notifications), `chevron` (disclosure) and the window controls `pin`, `minimize`, `maximize` and `restore`. The list is exported as `unlabeled`. Toolbars of tools or views always show labels. A product that shows another glyph alone has a defect to correct, not an exception to add.

`help` and `user` joined the owner's ten at the 0.3.0 integration, **for the owner to confirm**: `Help`'s question mark is the conventional glyph for essential help beside a field (named "Help: {topic}"), and the account avatar shows a person glyph only until the person's name is known (named "Account"); both follow the same rule as the rest of the list. Before 0.3.0 the family bar's Docs link showed its book glyph alone between 480 and 719 px; it now keeps its label and moves into the account menu below 600 px.

**Tooltips of icon-only controls** (since 0.3.0). Every icon-only control of the package shows its accessible name as a tooltip: the dialog's close button, a toast's dismiss button, a chip's remove button, the header's navigation toggle, `Help`, the notification bell, the family bar's account before a name is known and an `ActionMenu` shown as its glyph alone (`label: null` with `name`). The tooltip shows on hover after 300 ms, at once on keyboard focus (`:focus-visible`), and for 1.5 s on a touch press; a press, leaving, blur and Escape hide it, and it never shows over the open panel of an expanded control. It repeats the name the control already has, so it is `aria-hidden` and describes nothing: a screen reader hears the name once. Inside a modal dialog it is drawn in the dialog, above the page. A `Disclosure` takes `hint: true` for a glyph-only button. A product's own icon-only control uses `new Tooltip(button, { text, describe: false })` (React `<Tooltip text describe={false}>`), whose text is the control's `aria-label` and, when there is one, its shortcut (`Close · Ctrl+W`); `describe: false` hides it from assistive technology so the name is not heard twice. A tooltip that adds information the name lacks keeps the default, which describes the control. `tests/icons.test.mjs` and the acceptance's `checks/icons.mjs` fail any icon-only control of the package without a name, without its tooltip or with a glyph outside the list.

**Earlier names.** A product moving to the catalog maps its own names: the family reference's names are unchanged, except that its `book` is drawn as the package's; Workspace's client set maps `files` → `folder`, `review` → `merge`, `preview` → `window`, `diagnostics` → `alert`, `inspect` → `box`, `captures` → `archive`, `talk` → `chat`, `caret` → `right`, `down` → `chevron`, `back` (a chevron) → `left`, and keeps `close`, `plus`, `refresh`, `star`, `send`, `stop`, `more` and `code`; the Desktop's CSS-drawn window glyphs become `pin`, `minimize`, `maximize` and `restore`; CDN's step markers become `check`, `close`, `exclamation` and `minus`.

## Preferences

**Preferences** (DOM `Preferences`, React `Preferences` and `usePreferences`; since 0.3.0, decision D07). A person's language (`en`, `es`) and appearance (`system`, `light`, `dark`) in one product. Accounts holds the person's choice; the product applies it when it learns who the person is and keeps a device copy for the next first paint. A change made inside the product applies on this device only and lasts until the account's values change (since 0.3.1: a reload or a new arrival with the same account values keeps it); the product offers "Change for all of Beyond", a link to the Accounts account page, and never writes the account setting. Motion and density stay per device and are not part of it.

```js
import { Preferences } from '@beyond-js/ui';
const preferences = new Preferences({ key: 'beyond-projects', fallback: { appearance: 'light', locale: 'en' } });
preferences.restore();                                      // first paint, before rendering: the device copy or the fallback
preferences.apply({ appearance: identity.appearance, locale: identity.locale }); // on every arrival: the account wins when its values changed
preferences.choose({ appearance: 'dark' });                 // a change made in the product: this device only
preferences.appearance; preferences.locale; preferences.current; // 'dark', 'en', a frozen { appearance, locale }
const release = preferences.subscribe(({ appearance, locale }) => render()); // returns the release
link.textContent = preferences.labels.everywhere;           // "Change for all of Beyond" / "Cambiar en todo Beyond"
```

- **Options.** `key`: the device copy's storage key, one per product. `fallback: { appearance, locale }`: the product's defaults (the Desktop's appearance is `light`). `locales` (default `['en', 'es']`): the languages the product is localized in; an English-only product passes `['en']` and stays English whatever the account says. `storage` (default `localStorage`; `null` stores nothing) and `root` (default `<html>`) exist for tests and embedded surfaces.
- **Effect.** Every call sets `data-beyond-mode` on the root to `light` or `dark`, removes it for `system` (the stylesheet then follows the system preference), and sets `lang` to the language. Each returns the frozen `current`.
- **`apply(values)`.** The account's values win and become the device copy, unless they equal the account values this device last applied: then a choice made here since stays (so a product may call `apply` on every page load). The first arrival on a device, another person's values, or a change made at Accounts wins. A device copy from 0.3.0, which does not remember the account's values, is overridden once. A missing, `null` or unknown appearance is unset: the product's default applies, even over an earlier device choice. The earlier account default `system` is accepted as a choice. A language outside `locales` leaves the product's default. Fields other than the two are ignored and never stored.
- **`choose(values)`.** Changes only the values given, on this device; an appearance or language outside the lists throws a `TypeError` and changes nothing.
- **Device copy.** JSON `{ appearance, locale, account: { appearance, locale } }` under `key`: the values in effect and the account's values last applied, where `null` is a value never chosen; never an account identifier. Every storage access is guarded: when storage is missing, full or refused (a private window, blocked site data), the values last until the page closes.
- **`subscribe(listener)`.** Calls the listener with `current` after each change of the values in effect (not when a call changes nothing) and returns the release; it may be passed detached. A throwing listener does not stop the others; its error goes to `reportError`.
- **Copy.** `Preferences.labels.en` and `.es`: `everywhere` ("Change for all of Beyond", "Cambiar en todo Beyond"), `appearance` ("Appearance", "Apariencia"), `system` ("System", "Sistema"), `light` ("Light", "Claro"), `dark` ("Dark", "Oscuro"), `language` ("Language", "Idioma"); `preferences.labels` is the set of the language in effect. `Preferences.appearances` lists the three appearances.
- **React.** Create the instance once outside React and call `restore()` before the first render; `usePreferences(preferences)` returns `current` and re-renders after each change.
- **PreferencesDialog** (DOM `PreferencesDialog`, also exported by `/react`; since 0.6.0, D54). The one place a product offers language and appearance: a small dialog with Language and Appearance (each change applies at once, through `choose`, on this device), "Applies on this device." and "Change for all of Beyond" (`everywhere`, the account page at Accounts), and Done. Its copy follows the language in effect («Idioma y apariencia», `PreferencesDialog.labels.en` / `.es`). Options: `preferences`, `everywhere` (a string, or since 0.6.2 a function asked at each opening), `locales` (default `['en', 'es']`), `onclose` (0.6.2); `open({ restore })` resolves when it closes and does nothing while open (`restore` may be a function asked at closing), `shown` says whether it is open and `leave()` releases it once it closes. Since 0.6.2 a language chosen in it rewrites its copy in place, with the dialog open and focus where it was, and it outlives a bar drawn again (the bar's `destroy()` leaves it). In the family bar, "Change for all of Beyond" is the product's `everywhere` (since 0.6.3 a product that routes itself passes a function, asked at each opening, so the address carries the page in view); without one, Accounts' account page from the descriptor's `links.manage.account`, completed with the product and the page in view (0.6.2); to follow each choice, subscribe to the `Preferences` instance. In React, `everywhere` and `onclose` are read from the latest props (0.6.4): a new address, a string or an inline function, never draws the bar again. A product does not draw it itself: it passes the family bar `account.preferences: { preferences, everywhere }`, which puts "Language and appearance" first in the profile menu's product group (D48) and returns focus to the profile button. Preferences never sit on content pages, as a strip or a menu item that acts by itself (LR-08); Accounts keeps them on its account page.

const preferences = new Preferences({ key: 'beyond-delegate', fallback: { appearance: 'system', locale: 'en' } });
preferences.restore();

}
